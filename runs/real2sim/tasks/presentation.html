Making robot tasks from a real video

One real robot video in, a simulated scene with automatically generated language tasks out. Source data: DROID (public real-robot dataset). 10-15, exploratory.

About the task videos below: they are demonstrations, there to show what each task is. They come from a scripted expert (a planner that knows the exact simulated object positions and computes the arm motion), not from a VLA or any learned policy. What they show: the scene rebuilt from the real video supports these tasks physically, and the expert completes them. Testing a VLA (GR00T-N1.7-DROID) on the same tasks is the next step.

1 · Scene: cluttered cart (8 blocks, bowl, radish, apple)

Original real video (DROID, AUTOLab, camera 1). The operator's task was "place the toys in the black bowl, then put the apple at the left back corner of the trolley". The scene was rebuilt from its first frame.

Tasks generated from this scene (simulated, composited onto the real camera background):

"put the yellow block on the right in the bowl"

How it works (one line each)

  1. Perceive: objects detected and segmented in frame 0; per-pixel depth from a monocular depth model.
  2. Calibrate: camera pose found by matching the simulated robot's outline to the real robot in the video.
  3. Rebuild: objects placed in MuJoCo with real sizes and colours; meshes retrieved by name where available.
  4. Generate tasks: language instructions and success checks created automatically from the object list; randomised variants of each scene.
  5. Render: simulated robot and objects drawn onto the real camera background, with shadows.

Across all generated tasks (21 tasks × 5 variants), the scripted expert succeeds in 89/105 episodes. Known gaps: grasping the radish mesh, one placement inaccuracy, and a ~5 cm height error in the cluttered scene's reconstruction.
