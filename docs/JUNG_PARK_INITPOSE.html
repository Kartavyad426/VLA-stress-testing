Verdict: overlapping, not the same path
  • Same macro-question. Both projects ask whether a VLA's start-pose failures can be traced to a specific input and then fixed by targeted data. Both are on GR00T, and both conclude the wrist camera matters. Theirs: Unitree G1 humanoid, GR00T N1.5, one task (PickApple), 17 start poses. Ours: GR00T N1.7, LIBERO-Spatial, LIBERO-Plus start-pose perturbations.
  • Different instruments. They diagnose with:
    • behavioural metrics: a per-pose success grid and an early-window left/right action-imbalance score;
    • out-of-distribution wrist-camera masking, which they correctly read as sensitivity, not contribution;
    • physical joint swaps at reset, which change proprioception and both camera images at once.
    We diagnose with in-distribution counterfactual splices of single inputs (wrist vs agent view vs state token), a transfer fraction, a time localisation ("decided at forward k"), and a first-chunk rescue.
  • Different data targeting. Their fix is pose-space coverage: 2 coupled poses → 8 diversified poses at matched episode count, then +2 teleop poses next to one failing pose (30% → 75%, 6/20 → 15/20). Ours is a mechanism-specified frame specification: which frames need new labels, how many forwards, and from which source. Theirs worked at one pose, with no budget-matched or broad-coverage control for the targeted step. Ours (R-056) did not move held-out success, for reasons the post-mortem and D20 trace to coverage and dose, not capacity.
  • Still novel for us:
    • splice-based, in-distribution attribution to a single camera (W = 0.88 at forward 0);
    • time localisation (k* = 1) plus a rescue that proves the first chunk decides the episode (R-053: 20/24 flips vs 0/22 for a re-run);
    • a pre-registered mechanism readout before and after training (W 0.88 → 0.84);
    • an offset-resolved success curve that sets the coverage target in cm.
    None of these is in the paper.
  • Borrow:
    1. their equal-budget coverage control (304 vs 306 episodes);
    2. a physical joint-subset swap with a control, to test whether "wrist" really means "where the wrist camera ends up";
    3. reporting a per-pose grid, not an average;
    4. their warning that diluting target-task data erases gains (sim ratio 45% vs 9%), which is our dose finding again.

1. The claim, and what it would take to prove it

The paper makes three claims verified:

  1. Initial-pose × policy interaction. The same start pose gives very different success across fine-tuned policies, and one policy varies widely across poses. Average success hides this.
  2. A "policy-induced hand prior". At some poses the policy picks a hand from its own start configuration rather than from where the target is. Two specific right-arm joints, Rarm,3 and Rarm,5, are "a causal handle" on that prior. Wrist observations "modulate its expression" but do not explain it.
  3. Coverage fixes it. Diversifying training poses and adding poses around a weak one improves robustness. Auxiliary real data helps only if it does not dilute the target task.

Proving (2) would need four things. First, show the joints matter beyond their magnitude (a magnitude-matched control). Second, separate the proprioceptive channel from the visual appearance of the arm, since a physical swap changes both. Third, have enough episodes per cell. Fourth, show the effect across policies. The paper does the first partially, the second not at all, and the third with 4–14 episodes per cell; the fourth gives mixed results (§5).

Proving (3) as targeting rather than more data needs an equal-budget comparison: the same number of added episodes spread broadly vs placed near the failing pose. It also needs a check that the targeted pose's gain didn't cost other poses. The diversification step (2 → 8 poses at 306 vs 304 episodes) is budget-matched. The targeted step (8 → 10 poses) is not, and only the targeted pose is reported.

3. How they localise the failure: the method from first principles

3.1 The hand prior as a conditional distribution

Write the fine-tuned policy as πθD, trained on data D. The object of interest is the policy's preference over early hand-action modes:

pπ( h | s0, o0, xapple ),   h ∈ {left, right, bimanual, undecided},   x ∈ {ℓ, c, r}(1)

Here s0 is the start proprioception and o0 the first images from all three cameras. The policy never outputs (1) directly. It is operationalised over an initial window WT = {t0, …, t0+T−1}, with T = 120. The paper does not say whether T is in control steps or frames inferred.

3.2 HandPriorScore: an action-imbalance metric

For side S ∈ {L, R}, joint group G ∈ {arm, hand} and joint j:

ΔaGS,t,j = aGS,t,j − qGS,t,j    (commanded target minus current joint)(2)
σGS,j = Stde∈Rnorm, t∈WT ΔaGe,S,t,j,    MGS,t = √( (1/d) Σj ( ΔaGS,t,j / (σGS,j + ε) )² )(3)
AS = (1/T) Σt∈WT ( MarmS,t + λ MhandS,t ),    H = ( AR − AL ) / ( AR + AL + ε ) ∈ [−1, 1](4)

Positive H means right-hand activity dominates the early window. Two decompositions follow, with μx the mean H over episodes with the apple at x:

Hprior = (μℓ + μr) / 2   (bias left over when the target flips sides),    Htarget = (μr − μℓ) / 2   (how much the target moves it)(5–6)

This is a two-factor main-effects decomposition: Hprior is the intercept, Htarget the slope on target side. A healthy policy has Hprior ≈ 0 and a large positive Htarget. A "shortcut" pose has large |Hprior| and small Htarget. Notes:

  • σ is an in-sample normaliser, pooled over all policies, so H is comparable across policies but not across papers.
  • λ (the hand weight) is never given.
  • H measures activity, not direction toward the apple. A large right-arm motion away from a right apple still counts as "right preference".

3.3 Wrist-camera masking: how wrist influence was established

Four lines of evidence verified:

  1. Training ablation. A1 (head camera only) vs A2 (head + wrists), same data: overall success 61.5% → 76.8%; diversified poses 56.9% → 81.2%, better on 7 of 8. But at the diagnostic pose teleop_default, wrists made it worse: Hprior +0.12 → +0.25, wrong-hand crossing 6% → 33%, success 85% → 60% (17/20 → 12/20, Fisher p = 0.16, not significant).
  2. Mixed availability. C1 (wrist images for sim, zero-filled for real) vs B1 (head-only), same mixture: 62.9% → 80.9% overall; teleop_default 30% → 70% (p = 0.03).
  3. Test-time masking on A2 at teleop_default, paired with the full-observation run "from the same model, condition, object location, and episode":
    • both wrists masked: success drops to 20%;
    • one wrist masked: success about unchanged;
    • on left-target episodes, masking the right wrist cuts right-hand attempts 97.1% → 40.5%; masking the left gives 88.2%.
    The masking value is not stated; C-group training uses zero-filled streams, so masking is probably zeros inferred. The authors themselves say masking creates out-of-distribution inputs and "we interpret these ablations as measures of input-channel sensitivity rather than causal contribution percentages".
  4. Representation traces. For masked vs full, the cosine distance over 0–20 s at several taps:
    • visual embedding, action-query embedding and tokens, DiT output, absolute action chunk, and the Δa chunk;
    • the absolute chunk barely moves (0.002–0.008);
    • the Δa chunk moves 0.04–0.16, both-wrist > right > left.
    The reading: wrists steer incremental motion, while absolute targets stay anchored to the current state.

Conclusion drawn: the right-wrist stream strengthens the wrong-hand preference at that pose but does not by itself explain it, because masking reduces but does not remove it.

They build new start poses by copying a subset of joint values from one pose into the other, keeping the rest. The robot is physically placed there and run closed-loop verified.

4. How they decide what to fix

4.1 Which data

  • Coverage, budget-matched verified. The "limited" dataset used two poses, each coupled to a target side: left_sim for left/centre apples, right_sim for right/centre; 306 episodes. That coupling is what taught the shortcut. The diversified dataset Psim has 8 poses × 2 apple sides × 19 episodes = 304, "chosen to remain comparable". This is a clean, matched-budget test of decoupled coverage vs coupled coverage.
  • Targeted augmentation verified. A1 failed at right_real (30%). They project start states onto PCA of the 14 arm joints (PC1 38.7%, PC2 19.3%) and add two teleop collection poses "on either side of right_real along PC1", going from 8 to 10 poses. The episode count added is not stated; at the Psim rate it would be 2 × 2 × 19 = 76 inferred. right_real itself is not added.
  • Auxiliary real data: Unitree's G1-Dex3 teleop sets: 1 PickApple task, 7 pick tasks, or 13 tasks, mixed at the natural ratio or reweighted to 45.5% sim.

4.2 Which model parts, and how much training

"All policies were fine-tuned from GR00T-N1.5-3B" via Unitree's unitree_lerobot verified. Nothing else is stated: no trainable modules, LoRA or full, learning rate, steps, batch or seeds. The GR00T N1.5 fine-tuning default freezes the VLM and visual encoder and trains the projector and the diffusion action head. If they used that default, their trainable set is close to ours (we train LoRA on the DiT and VL self-attention, with tune_visual and tune_llm False) inferred.

4.3 How the targeting decision is made

Behaviourally: find the pose with low success in the grid, then sample new start poses near it in joint-PCA space. The joint-swap and masking diagnostics are run at teleop_default, but the targeted data goes to right_real. The mechanism analysis does not feed the data decision. The diagnosis explains one failure; the augmentation fixes another, chosen by low success alone.

6. Critique

  • The "causal handle" is a world intervention, so it can't name the channel. Swapping R3, R5 moves the forearm and wrist. That changes the proprio vector, the arm's appearance in the head camera, and the right wrist camera's pose and view. The paper concludes "the localized initial arm configuration" is causal, which is accurate, but the abstract's framing as distinct from wrist influence is not supported. Their own masking found the right wrist carries part of it. The two results are compatible with a single story: those joints matter because they move the right wrist camera inferred. Neither experiment can tell.
  • The control is not magnitude-matched. The control joints differ by <0.39; the treatment joints by ~1.3. A larger joint change could matter simply by moving further from the training distribution, whichever joints are moved.
  • Tiny, unequal counts. Cells of 4, 7, 8 or 9 episodes; the B1 cross-policy removal (4/14 → 0/12) is p = 0.10, and the reverse direction fails for both B1 and C2. The authors flag "small and unequal sample counts".
  • Masking is out of distribution. They say so. Blank images test whether the network needs the channel, not what the channel's content does. An in-distribution swap (the wrist frame from another real state) is the stronger test, and is what we did (R-042).
  • Targeted augmentation has no control. Adding ~76 episodes anywhere might raise right_real. Adding them there might lower other poses. Neither is reported.
  • Manual success labels, one task, one embodiment, no training details, no seeds. Reproduction from the paper alone is not possible.
  • "Hand prior" is the right idea for a mode choice. A discrete, early, pose-conditioned decision is a cleaner object than our continuous approach error. Their Hprior/Htarget split is a useful template for any binary early decision.

7.1 Where the two agree

  • Wrist camera. Both find it carries start-pose information. Ours is stronger: in-distribution and quantified at 0.88. Theirs adds that wrist data can amplify a bad prior at a specific pose (A2 at teleop_default).
  • Proprio is not the main channel. Their Δa analysis shows absolute targets anchored to the state while wrists move the increments. Our R-039 found the state token near-inert. Neither result says "the policy reads its joint state to decide".
  • Coverage near the failure helps, and dilution hurts. Their +2 poses (30 → 75%) and their sim-ratio result (B2 40.3% → D 61.5% just by reweighting) match our post-mortem: the correction was 1% of draws and never reached >12 cm.

7.2 Where they differ

  • Their localisation does not drive their data choice (§4.3). Ours is designed to: forward 0, wrist-bearing frames, offset bands.
  • They source data by teleoperation at chosen poses, which gives full coverage wherever a human can go. Our sources are generated (rescue minting, bridges), and our coverage problem is a yield problem.
  • Their failure is a discrete mode choice; ours is a continuous approach error with a sharp offset threshold (8–12 cm).

8. What it means for our stack

8.1 Verdict

Overlapping, not the same path. They are the closest prior work on the question: GR00T, start-pose dependence, wrist influence, targeted pose data. They are not prior work on the method. None of these appear in the paper:

  • in-distribution input splices with a transfer fraction;
  • time localisation (decided at forward k);
  • a sufficiency rescue;
  • a mechanism-specified frame-level data specification;
  • a before/after mechanism readout.

Our claim as phrased in DATA_SOURCING_RESEARCH.html Q4 survives the full read: "mechanism-specified data targeting with a pre-registered mechanism readout". The Q4 row should be updated from "unverified beyond the abstract" to this doc. Our one weakness relative to them is that their targeted fix worked at its pose and ours has not yet.

8.2 What to borrow

B1 · An equal-budget coverage control

Their 306 vs 304 episodes is the design our D23 replay-only control should copy. The arms would be:

  • offset-stratified Option A demos;
  • the same number of demos at uniformly sampled offsets;
  • replay only.

Use the same steps and sampler share, and report every offset band, not just the targeted one.

B2 · A joint-subset swap, as a test of "wrist means wrist-camera pose"

R-042 says the wrist camera carries start pose. Their R3/R5 result suggests a specific test: perturb only the joints that move the wrist camera vs only the joints that don't, at matched gripper offset (cm), then run the W/A splice on each. If W collapses for the non-camera-moving set, the start-pose mechanism is "where the wrist camera looks", which sharpens the data spec to wrist-view coverage. Use a magnitude-matched control, which they lacked.

B3 · Report the grid, not the mean

Report success per scene × offset band for every arm. R-056's held-out average hid a one-scene regression in exactly the way their averages hide teleop_default.

B4 · An early-window imbalance metric for start-pose runs

Their Hprior/Htarget split gives a template for a per-episode early-direction score on LIBERO. Take the forward-0 chunk's direction relative to the gripper→bowl vector, split into a pose-driven intercept and a target-driven slope across directions. It is cheap, needs no extra rollouts, and complements τW.

B5 · Watch the dilution ratio

Their B2 → D result (+21 pt from reweighting alone) is the same lesson as our post-mortem §3.1. Our next pilot should fix the frame-0 and bridge share explicitly (Option A's 25–50% dose) and report it before training.
