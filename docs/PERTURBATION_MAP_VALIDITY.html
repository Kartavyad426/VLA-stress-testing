1. What the map measures, and its method lineage

At forward f, the action head runs once per arm from the same state and the same denoising noise ε. Each arm restores one input to its nominal value and leaves the rest perturbed verified (splice.py). The transfer fraction is:

τX(f) = 1 − ‖aX − aN‖ / ‖aP − aN‖,   norms over the 40 × 7 live chunk in normalised action units(1)

2. Threats

T1 · Start pose: is the nominal source the same situation?

(a) Real? Partly. There are two different source paths verified:

  • Splice runs (R-039, R-042 rollouts): the source is the recorded per-forward features of the scene's control episode (RecorderHandle; r039_run.py RECORDED_CATS), aligned by forward index, not by state.
  • The headline wrist 0.88 (R-044, experiments/r044_reverse.py): the control env is reset at the same seed, at forward 0 only.

Check run. Object layout at t = 0, variant vs control, from runs/r042/rollouts.jsonl verified. All 10 pairs have identical objects (max offset ≤ 1.4 × 10⁻¹⁰ m; 5 objects each, none unique to one side). The end-effector differs by 2.9–16.3 cm. At forward 0 the pair is exactly "the factor reverted": same world, different arm. After forward 0 the recorded source follows the control's own trajectory, so for f ≥ 1 it describes a different situation.

(b) Numbers affected. None at forward 0. Start-pose τ at the anchor (W 0.59, T 0.13 in R-042) and any forward > 0 readout mix "the input carried it" with "a different trajectory". R-039's expectation 6 (residual at the anchor, 36/40) includes 10 such instances.

(c) Cheapest check. Done above (layout). For f > 0, there is no stored state-aligned source. A state-aligned source would need a re-render of the control's arm pose into the target state, which is not physical.

(d) Verdict. Forward-0 start-pose claims hold. Start-pose readouts at the anchor or at f > 0 are invalid as pathway attributions.

T3 · The anchor uses outcome information

(a) Real? Yes verified.

  • The anchor f* is the forward of the episode's minimum end-effector-to-bowl distance (r039_run.py, anchor_forward), so it depends on the executed trajectory and hence on outcome.
  • On the camera row, failures (all 18-forward timeouts) anchor at forwards 3–16 (median 6 in r042, and up to 16 in r039). Successes anchor at 2–9, plus one at 17.
  • Success/failure comparisons at the anchor therefore compare different phases of the task.

(b) Affected:

  • "dominant = argmax τ(f*) ≥ 0.9, else residual";
  • R-039 expectation 6 (36/40 residual at the anchor);
  • the anchor columns in the instance tables.

Anchor medians differ from forward 0 by up to 0.4: camera A 0.98 → 0.84, noise A 0.97 → 0.57, start-pose W 0.88 → 0.59. Onset (first f with τ ≥ 0.5) is outcome-blind, and so are all forward-0 readouts.

(c) Check. Report anchor readouts stratified by outcome, or replace the anchor with an outcome-blind forward (a fixed forward, or a phase from the nominal run's timeline).

(d) Verdict. Forward-0 verdicts hold, and they are what the map's summary table uses ("dominant input at forward 0"). Anchor-based classifications are weakened: do not compare them across outcomes.

T4 · How paired are the pairs? (render nondeterminism and the re-render path)

(a) Real? Yes, in two layers.

  • The re-render path verified (libero_env.py nominal_frames). For camera, lighting and noise, N's frames come from sim.forward() plus inner._get_observations(force_update=True) with the camera or lights swapped. P's frames are the env's own reset or step render.
    • impl reports unverified that a force-update re-render with nothing changed already differs from the reset frame, wrist included.
    • R-060's G0c unverified, runs/r060/gates/g0c.json: the variant's nominal frames differ from the control env's own render by ≈ 24k pixels (max 52). Even P differs from the variant env's own render (≈ 1.1k agent, 9.1k wrist pixels).
    • So part of ‖P−N‖ on these rows may be a render-call difference, not the perturbation.
  • Between-run jitter. Two rollouts with the same seed and noise disagree on success 4–9 times in 10–40 (map §0), and stored action-level replicates agree to 0.03.

(b) Size, from what is stored verified:

  • Wrist part. On camera and noise, the wrist arm W restores the nominal re-rendered wrist, whose only difference from P's wrist is the render call (the perturbation leaves the wrist untouched). Its τ is 0.00 (camera, seed range 0.005) and 0.00 (noise). So the wrist re-render difference has no measurable action effect.
  • Agent-view part. Cannot be separated from the perturbation with stored data.
  • Indirect evidence. R-057 drove N's re-rendered frames for whole episodes and got 30/30 camera successes.
  • Replicate spread (base run vs seed 1 vs seed 2; noise seed varies, so render jitter is included). Median per-instance range of τ:
    • camera A 0.002 (max 0.02); noise A 0.008 (max 0.03);
    • start-pose W 0.025 (max 0.17); start-pose I 0.07;
    • lighting A 0.10 / W 0.14 (max 0.63 / 0.36);
    • layout I 0.38 (max 0.49), A 0.14 (max 0.87);
    • texture W 0.21 (max 0.70).
    Relative spread of ‖P−N‖ is 7% (camera, start pose), 12% (lighting), 14% (texture), 20% (noise) and 24% (layout).

(c) Cheapest discriminating check (GPU, ≈ 10 forwards, minutes). On each control (no perturbation) env, compute a(reset frame) vs a(nominal_frames() re-render) at forward 0 under fixed ε, and compare ‖Δa‖ with each row's ‖P−N‖. If it is below ~5% of ‖P−N‖ (≈ 0.06 camera, 0.07 noise, 0.015 lighting), the re-render path is harmless. Owner: impl (GPU); res21 cannot run it.

(d) Verdict.

  • Camera and noise (agent view) hold, pending the null. The wrist bound and R-057 make a material bias unlikely.
  • Start pose is unaffected by the re-render path: both sides are reset frames from their own envs.
  • Lighting and texture are weakened. Their ‖P−N‖ (0.29, 0.31) is small enough that a render-call offset of a few hundredths would be 5–10% of it. Their per-camera seed ranges already exceed 0.1.

T7 · Distances in normalised action space

(a) Real? Partly verified.

  • The checkpoint uses min-max normalisation to [−1, 1] (policy_postprocessor.json: normalize_min_max, clip_normalized_action, gripper binarised).
  • Half-ranges are translation 0.9375, rotation 0.19 / 0.35 / 0.37, gripper 0.5. Normalising therefore amplifies rotation 2.6–4.9× relative to translation.
  • Rotation's share of ‖P−N‖² is 33–49% in normalised units against 6–11% in environment units on the vision rows.
  • The gripper's share is ≤ 4% in both, so clipping and binarisation do not compress anything that matters at forward 0.

(b, c) Re-score in environment units (clip, then un-normalise with the checkpoint's own stats):

  • camera I 0.69 → 0.65;
  • start-pose W 0.88 → 0.90;
  • camera A 0.98 → 0.98;
  • layout A 0.55 → 0.64;
  • layout W 0.16 → 0.27.

Translation-only on the executed steps changes no headline by more than 0.05.

(d) Verdict. Holds. The metric's space changes rotation weighting but no conclusion. The layout row moves the most and is already unstable (T4, T6).

T8 · Off-distribution hybrids, and what denoising and noising each show

(a) Real? Yes, and the map says so ("a state no robot was in"). A/W hybrids pair one camera from one state with the other camera and state from another verified (hybrid_obs).

Interpretation. In Heimersheim & Nanda's convention:

  • denoising (clean into corrupted) tests whether a component is sufficient to restore the clean behaviour;
  • noising (corrupted into clean) tests whether it is necessary for the clean behaviour.

The map uses the effect-centric dual. DenoiseW 0.88 means the perturbed wrist is necessary for the effect. NoiseW 0.46 means it is about half-sufficient on its own. By De Morgan these are the same facts: the clean wrist is nearly sufficient to restore the nominal action, and a clean agent view partly compensates when only the wrist is perturbed.

The map's legend "denoising high and noising high: necessary and sufficient" is correct in its convention. "Denoising high, noising low: redundancy" is the behaviour-centric reading of the same pattern, i.e. an AND of perturbations. Both are right, but the convention is never stated, and a reader trained on the interpretability literature will read "necessary" the other way.

(b) Affected.

  • The start-pose sentence "the wrist is necessary".
  • The texture and lighting mismatch findings (noiseA −0.40). Those are responses to the hybrid itself, by construction OOD. The map reads them as "the head responds to cross-view mismatch", which is a claim about hybrids, not about real perturbations.

(c) Check. Wording only.

(d) Verdict. Holds, with a convention sentence needed.

T9 (added) · Small denominators

τ is a ratio with ‖P−N‖ in the denominator. On lighting (0.29) and texture (0.31), the denominators are a quarter of camera's and 1/14 of start pose's. Their τ seed ranges (0.1–0.7) come from that, not from instability in the network. Verdict: report ‖aX − aN‖ alongside τ for small-effect rows, and do not rank arms on these rows by τ alone.

T10 (added) · Outcome-level claims rest on a different, noisier footing

"Decided at forward 0" (R-057) and the forward-0 rescue (R-053: 20/24 flips vs 0/22 for a re-run) are outcome counts, not τ. They have their own no-op controls and 30 episodes each, and R-057's expectations were all pre-registered and held verified. Verdict: holds. It is not touched by T2, T3 or T7. Outcome flip noise (30–40% per single episode) is the right caveat, and R-053's no-op arm is the control that addresses it.

4. Proposed "Method lineage and validity" paragraph for the map

Text only, for compile_map.py after the user approves (checklist pm2). Numbers are from this audit.

Method lineage and validity.

Each arm is an interchange intervention (Geiger et al. 2021): one input is set to its value under the nominal condition while the rest stay perturbed, and every arm shares the same denoising noise (common random numbers). Restoring nominal inputs into the perturbed run is "denoising" and the reverse is "noising", as in activation patching (Heimersheim & Nanda 2024; Zhang & Nanda 2024), with causal tracing (Meng et al. 2022) as the precedent. The transfer fraction τ is a normalised recovery metric of that family.

Convention: "necessary" and "sufficient" on this page refer to the perturbed input's role in the effect. In the patching literature's behaviour-centric convention, the same numbers read as the clean input being sufficient (denoising) or necessary (noising) for the nominal action.

A CPU audit (docs/PERTURBATION_MAP_VALIDITY.html) re-scored every stored record:

  • The forward-0 claims for camera (agent view 0.98), sensor noise (0.97), start pose (wrist 0.88, W > A 10/10) and the state token (0.04) change by ≤ 0.05 when τ is computed on the 16 executed steps, the first action, environment units or translation only.
  • Their orderings survive Bonferroni correction.
  • Noise and layout image-token transfers are understated by the full-chunk metric (0.45 → 0.85 and 0.40 → 0.74 on executed steps).
  • Per-camera attributions on lighting, texture and layout are within seed-to-seed spread and should be read as unresolved.
  • Anchor-based readouts depend on the outcome, and for start pose on a source that is aligned by forward index, so only forward-0 readouts support the verdicts.

The paired nominal for camera, lighting and noise is a re-render, whose difference from a reset frame is bounded at ≈ 0 for the wrist and is being checked for the agent view. All results are for one checkpoint on failure-enriched instances.

5. What this audit did not do

  • No GPU. The T4 null (≈ 10 forwards) and the second-checkpoint reverse runs (T5) are recommendations for impl.
  • The reverse-direction runs (r044_reverse, r048_reverse, r051_reverse_*) store scalar τ only, so their numbers (noiseW 0.46 and others) could not be re-scored under T2 or T7.
  • R-041 and R-047 sweeps (outcome boundaries) and R-045 probes were checked for their pre-registration status only, not re-scored.
  • No edits to the map, the harness, or any other doc.
