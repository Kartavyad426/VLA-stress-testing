0 · Verdict

Your pipeline (detect → mesh → scale/pose → physics) is the right backbone, and it is now what the field converged on. The newest single-image systems (RoboSnap 2607.06699, DEXTERA 2609.21045, SimuScene 2606.03994) are all that pipeline plus two things you didn't list: (1) a physics "settling" refinement step, and (2) a splat or generated background for appearance. [V]

Your worry is correct, and it is the main failure. Single-view meshes are not physics-ready out of the box. A BMVC 2026 benchmark of 13 methods found that all single-object methods produce meshes in collision in most scenes, and that "the reconstructed meshes would be largely unusable for physics simulation without significant, non-trivial postprocessing" (2505.17966) [V]. In RoboSnap's own ablation, 43% of objects fall without the refinement step and 10% with it [V]. The fix is known: convex decomposition plus pose and shape refinement in a physics loop. It is engineering, not research.

Retrieval beats generation where it can be used, especially for anything with joints. A retrieved asset arrives with clean collision hulls, real scale and (for articulated assets) correct joints. Generation is the fallback for objects not in the library. Locally we already have a library: LIBERO-Plus ships 1,125 MuJoCo object models with convex collision hulls already built (third_party/LIBERO-plus/libero/libero/assets/new_objects) [V].

Monocular metric scale is good enough to start, not good enough to trust. Expect errors of a few percent to ~10% of the distance [I]. For grasping, a few cm can decide success. The CPU test (§7) measures exactly how much error a LIBERO demo tolerates.

Mass and friction cannot be seen. Everyone uses VLM or material-table priors (RoboSnap says so in its limitations [V]). Only interaction (a robot, or a human video with known forces) identifies them. For quasi-static pick-and-place under position control this matters less than geometry [I]. The CPU test includes a sensitivity check.

What to try locally (8 GB): Grounded-SAM-2 (masks) + MoGe-2 (metric depth, MIT) + retrieval from the LIBERO-Plus library, with Hunyuan3D-2mini (6 GB, shape only) or TripoSG (8 GB, MIT) as the generation fallback + CoACD (MIT) + a physics-settle step + MuJoCo or ManiSkill. That fits on our card if run one model at a time [I]. Heavy-compute upgrade: SAM 3D Objects (needs ≥32 GB VRAM [V]) + Isaac Sim (≥16 GB, RTX 4080-class minimum [V]) + a splat background, i.e. the RoboSnap stack.

Splats alone don't give physics. Video world models don't give actions you can trust. Deformables from monocular RGB are not feasible yet. (§3, §6.)

1 · What "good enough" means for training rollouts

A rollout video is accurate if it is right on two separate axes. Approaches differ on which one they buy.

AxisRequirementTraining data needsEvaluation twin needs
Motion (contact, dynamics)Collision geometry: watertight, convex-decomposed, no interpenetration at t=0Must have. Bad hulls give impossible grasps.Must have.
Metric scale and resting posePlausible: within the policy's tolerance. Randomisation covers the rest.Matched to the real scene, or the success rate is off.
Stability (objects stay put when nothing touches them)Must haveMust have
Mass, friction, inertiaPlausible range; randomiseMatched, if the task involves sliding, pushing or heavy objects
Articulation (joint type, axis, limits, damping)Correct type and axis; limits approximateMatched
AppearanceTextures, lighting, background, cameraPlausible; randomisation helps (ACDC: "cousins" beat exact twins, 90% vs 25% zero-shot [V])Close match. SIMPLER/PolaRiS show it drives sim–real correlation (see REAL_SIM_PAIRED_DATA)

The key asymmetry: for training data, "plausible plus randomised" is enough, and that is cheaper than "matched". ACDC's result is the evidence: training on several digital cousins (similar, not identical, scenes) transferred better than training on the one exact twin [V]. Evaluation needs matching. Training data doesn't.

Does metric scale matter for physics? Yes, through the robot. Gravity, the gripper and the arm's reach are real size. Shrink a bowl by 10% and the grasp height and closing width change. Move it by 3 cm and an open-loop demo misses [I]. How much it matters for generated demos is a different question. If demos are planned in the reconstructed scene (DMP/planner), scale errors cancel inside the sim and only hurt transfer back to the real scene. §7 measures both.

4 · Inputs

  • A few monocular frames. Enough for rigid objects with retrieval plus refinement (RoboSnap and ACDC use one image [V]). The back faces and hidden interiors are invented.
  • Human video is strictly better than a few frames. It adds (1) multiple views: Depth Anything 3 and VGGT estimate camera pose plus depth from many views [V], which steadies metric scale; (2) object motion: tracked part motion gives articulation axes and limits (R2R2R does this [V]); (3) hand–object contact: tools like RoboWheel and HA-HOI turn monocular HOI video into physically plausible object trajectories [V] (abstract level). It still does not give mass or friction.
  • Teleop logs: your scepticism is mostly right. They help only in three cases: (a) the log's cameras see the scene, so they are extra views for reconstruction; (b) the log has joint torques or gripper width, which constrain object size and mass (system ID, as in scalable-real2sim [V]); (c) as a validation set: replay the logged trajectory in the reconstructed scene and check that it still succeeds (RoboSnap's trajectory-replay metric [V]). Without visible scene or force data, they add nothing to the reconstruction.
  • No depth camera. Fine for a start. A known camera FOV (intrinsics) is the cheapest accuracy boost (MoGe takes it as an input [V]).

6 · Deformables (cloth, liquids)

  • Verdict: not feasible from monocular RGB today; feasible only with multi-view RGB-D and a dedicated simulator.
  • PhysTwin (ICCV 2025) is the best open result: spring-mass physics + Gaussian rendering for ropes, cloth, plush toys. It needs three RGB-D views of the object being manipulated [V]. A follow-up uses it for policy evaluation with real–sim correlation on soft bodies (2511.04665) [V].
  • I found no monocular, physics-parameterised cloth real-to-sim method. 2026 monocular cloth work is avatar animation, not simulation [V] (search level).
  • Liquids: no real-to-sim method found. Genesis (SPH/MPM) can simulate them, but parameters would be hand-set [I].
  • For training data, procedural deformables (generated cloth in MuJoCo flex or Genesis) are a more realistic route than reconstructing them [I].

7 · CPU feasibility test on LIBERO (design; not run)

Why LIBERO: we know the true scene (meshes, poses, physics), so every reconstruction error can be measured, and the demos give a success check. Question: how much reconstruction error can a LIBERO demo tolerate, and does monocular depth plus mesh fitting land inside that tolerance?

7.0 · Prerequisite: replay check (also answers the open D22 item)

Set the env to a demo's states[0] and replay its actions open-loop. Nobody has checked yet whether D22 demos replay (HANDOFF_8Oct §5). If replay fails at true state, open-loop replay can't be the success measure, and I fall back to a scripted pick-and-place driven by object poses.

True masks are used on purpose: detection is solved enough (§2a), and this keeps the test about geometry.

7.2 · Method

  1. 5 LIBERO-Spatial tasks × 3 demos each. Render agentview RGB, depth and segmentation at the demo's states[0], plus camera intrinsics and extrinsics.
  2. Back-project masked pixels to points. Fit the table plane (RANSAC). Register each mesh to its points: ICP with gravity prior (upright, resting on the table); scale fitted only in C2.
  3. Write the estimated poses into the env (and, for C2, swap in the cousin mesh). Settle 2 s under gravity.
  4. Replay the demo (or the scripted fallback).

7.3 · Scores

  • Position error (cm), yaw error (°), scale error (%) per object.
  • Stability: displacement during settling; interpenetration depth at t=0.
  • Success of the replayed demo (the number that matters for training data), read against the N tolerance curve.
  • Image match: PSNR/SSIM of the rebuilt scene's first frame against the original.

7.4 · Cost and dependencies

A new venv .venvs/real2sim with MoGe (MIT, CPU inference), trimesh, Open3D (or scipy ICP), CoACD. The MoGe-2 ViT-S weights are about 35M params [V]. LIBERO itself runs from the existing libero-plus venv. Expected runtime: minutes on CPU [I]. Results get tagged UNVERIFIED until val checks them (PIPELINE_CHECKS rule).

8 · Recommendation

  1. Now (CPU, after you approve the code): run §7. It tells us the pose tolerance of LIBERO demos and whether MoGe-2 + retrieval lands inside it. That decides whether monocular input is enough.
  2. Then, the "weaker local scene" for your compute ask (8 GB GPU, with your permission): one real photo of a tabletop (a phone photo is fine) → Grounded-SAM-2 → MoGe-2 → retrieval from the LIBERO-Plus library (SigLIP/CLIP match), Hunyuan3D-2mini/TripoSG as fallback → CoACD → physics settle → a Panda in MuJoCo running a scripted grasp or DMP demo → video. Side-by-side with the photo. That is the demo to show.
  3. With more compute (≥32 GB GPU): the RoboSnap stack. SAM 3 + SAM 3D Objects (scene-level, handles occlusion) + VGGT/DA3 + refinement + Isaac Sim + a splat background. Photoreal, articulated, published sim–real correlation r = 0.887 [V]. Check whether RoboSnap's code is out first.
  4. Not recommended now: world models as the data generator (no physics, no trustworthy actions; costly), splat-only twins (no contact), deformables.
