C1 · Position DMP form [§1.2–1.3]

OptionProsCons
a. Hoffmann/Pastor 2009 (forcing term in absolute units, linear start→goal shift)Cannot flip or overscale. Keeps the demo's shape at its real size (lift height, approach curve), which is what a correction should look like. Well known, simple to fit and test. Behaves predictably for far roots.Does not adapt the shape to a longer or rotated start→goal vector: for a far root the shape is the demo's, plus a straight-line shift, which can look less natural than a scaled shape. Differs from the source design, so it needs the user's OK.
b. Original Ijspeert 2013 (forcing term × (g − y₀) per axis)The textbook form; scales the shape with the displacement.Flips or blows up on axes where the demo's displacement is near zero (common at the end of the approach). Known failure mode; I would not use it.
c. Vector-wise scaling (rotate and scale f to the new displacement; source design S4)No flip. Shape rotates with the new start→goal direction, so the motion looks "aimed".Still scales the shape by ‖g−y₀‖/‖g_d−y_d,0‖: 2–3× for far roots, so lift heights and curvature stretch with it (Sidiropoulos & Doulgeri 2024 report this overscaling). Rotation is ill-defined when the demo's displacement is short.
Recommendation: (a) as default, (c) as an extra arm on the 4 scenarios (~20 lines). The comparison tells us whether scaling helps at far roots, with no extra build risk.

C2 · Success definition [§5.2]

OptionProsCons
a. Full-task success (env's BDDL check: bowl on plate)The same check as the eval; a kept episode is a real success. One rule for every root type. Nothing to define or validate.A grasp root has to succeed at the place step too, so a good grasp correction can be lost to a bad place; yield is lower and mixes two causes.
b. Per-segment predicate (e.g. grasp.lift for grasp roots)Isolates whether the correction of that phase worked. Higher yield, shorter rollouts.Needs predicates per phase that are not validated (ROOT_IDENTIFICATION §4). A "successful" episode may still fail the task, so it is not usable training data as is.
Recommendation: (a) for keep/reject. Also log whether the segment's own predicate was met, so a failure can be attributed to the grasp or the place without changing the bar.

C3 · Step budget [§5.1]

OptionProsCons
a. 200 after the root, capped at the original 280A kept episode is also a success under the eval's own limit, so it is consistent with how the policy is scored and directly usable as a full episode.Late roots (e.g. B at t = 242) get only 38 steps, which is probably too few to re-place. B could fail for budget reasons, not method reasons.
b. 200 after the root, no capEvery root gets the same chance; the method is judged fairly across early and late roots.Some kept episodes would be over 280 steps; fine for corrective segments (root→goal only), not for full-episode use.
Recommendation (changed after writing this out): (b), uncapped, and log whether each kept episode would also fit inside 280. Training uses the root→goal segment anyway (I-Gen keeps only the post-mistake part), so the cap mostly penalises late roots like scenario B. Decided (user, 10-14): (b), uncapped; the main page is aligned.

C4 · Root rules for the sweep [§8]

OptionProsCons
a. Reuse the existing exploratory rules (poc_toy_instance.py / UQ10 probe)Available now; no wait. The PoC only needs a root step to restart from, not a validated label. Running both root rules gives two roots per failure, which covers the rule ambiguity.Known weak spots: rules not unique (115/176 fire 2+ modes), blind spots (wrong object, pushing). The per-failure-type table inherits those labelling errors.
b. Wait for MINING_SPEC_V1 rulesBetter labels; the failure-type table would mean more. Shared code with the mining line.Blocks the sweep on another session's schedule (v1 awaits the user's review and hand-labelled data).
c. Hand-pick roots by watching episodesMost accurate per root.Does not scale to 176; needs renders (GPU).
Recommendation: (a), with the failure-type table marked as resting on unvalidated labels. Swap in v1 rules later; the sweep is cheap to rerun.

Decided (user, 10-14): (a) for now; a more robust root-selection process is needed (main page UQ4).

M1 · Cartesian vs joint space [§1.1]

OptionProsCons
a. Cartesian EEFThe recorded actions and the policy's actions are EEF deltas, so the reference maps straight to actions. Object-frame goals are natural. Independent of the arm's joint configuration, which is what R-047 perturbs.Needs orientation handling (M2). Ignores the null space: the arm's posture may drift from the demos' (could cause joint limits at extreme start poses).
b. Joint spaceOrientation comes for free; no singularity issues in the DMP itself.Needs IK for goals; demo joint shapes don't transfer when the start joints differ by 0.5 rad; actions would have to be converted back to EEF deltas anyway.
Recommendation: (a). Log joint-limit hits so (b)'s one advantage is checked.

M2 · Orientation [§1.4]

OptionProsCons
a. Rotation-vector DMP relative to goalReuses the position DMP; easy to test and explain. Exact while rotations stay well below 180°, which LIBERO-Spatial's near top-down grasps satisfy.Breaks down for large rotations (wrap-around near π). Not the method in the source design.
b. Quaternion DMP (Ude 2014 + 2019 correction)Correct for any rotation; the source design's choice.More to get right (the 2019 paper exists because the 2014 form oscillates); harder to unit-test.
c. Hold orientation fixed at the goalSimplest.Throws away the demo's wrist motion; the result would not look like the demos.
Recommendation: (a), with a check of the largest rotation over all demos and roots; switch to (b) only if any exceeds 60°.

M3 · Gripper [§1.5]

OptionProsCons
a. Binary switch at segment ends + demo's settle timeMatches how the demos command it (±1 only). No extra model.Timing is fixed to the reference reaching the segment end; if tracking lags, the gripper closes early relative to the actual pose.
b. Close when the achieved pose is within a tolerance of the grasp goalRobust to tracking lag.Adds a tolerance to tune; departs from the demo's timing.
Recommendation: (a), and add (b)'s tolerance as a guard only if T2 shows tracking lag at the grasp point.

M4 · Join at the root [§3]

OptionProsCons
a. Position + velocity match (z₀ = τ·ẏ_root)No jump in position or velocity; the res 10-14 addition. Honest: it is what the robot is doing at the root.Acceleration still jumps; if the policy was moving the wrong way, the first few steps continue that motion.
b. Start from rest (z₀ = 0)Simplest; the DMP's native case.Velocity jump at the join: a visible stop that the policy could learn.
c. Also match acceleration (quintic blend)Smoothest; removes the jerk spike.~0.5 d extra; may not be needed.
Recommendation: (a), with (b) as a comparison arm on the scenarios; add (c) only if the jerk check fails often.

M5 · Demo matching [§4]

OptionProsCons
a. Single frame + velocity, normalised state distance, top-3Cheap and simple; velocity carries most of the motion context. Uses privileged sim state, which we have.Ignores clutter and longer motion context; may match a demo that was going somewhere else.
b. 5–10 step window (source design)Captures direction of travel more robustly.Slower, more parameters; the root's recent past is the policy's (off-distribution) motion, so matching it closely may not help.
c. Learned embedding (VINN-style)Would work without privileged state (real data).Needs training and validation; not needed in sim.
Top-k: 1 vs 3 vs 5k = 3 gives a yield per root with little cost.k = 1 makes coverage noisy; k = 5 adds runs with weaker matches.
Recommendation: (a) with k = 3; move to (b) only if the visual check shows wrong matches.

M6 · Root restore [§5.1]

OptionProsCons
a. Replay logged actions on CPUNo GPU, no policy, no new rollout. Exact if physics is deterministic.Not proven yet; long replays (B: 242 steps) are the most exposed to drift. Depends on rebuilding the identical LIBERO-Plus env and start-pose perturbation.
b. Re-run the policy with the same seedsAlso records full sim state for future use.GPU through prim; render nondeterminism could change the policy's actions, so it may not reproduce the same failure.
c. Set the state from logged joints + object posesInstant; no replay.Logs have no velocities of objects, no orientations of all objects, no contact state; an approximate state, especially bad with the bowl in hand (B).
Recommendation: (a), gated by the 1 mm check; (c) as a fallback for grasp roots only if (a) drifts; (b) last.

M7 · Tracking controller [§5.1]

OptionProsCons
a. Closed-loop P on the reference, fixed look-aheadCorrects drift; one parameter, tuned once in T2.Actions are a function of tracking error, so their statistics may differ from human actions.
b. Open-loop: reference differences as actionsActions look like the demo's actions.Drifts, especially after contact; lower yield.
Recommendation: (a); report action statistics against the demos' so the difference is visible.

M8 · Linear-bridge baseline arm [§2.1]

OptionProsCons
a. Include itThe only way to know the DMP adds anything over I-Gen's published method; same code path, small extra cost.Doubles executions (still minutes to hours on CPU).
b. Skip itSlightly faster.A positive result could not be attributed to the DMP.
Recommendation: (a).

M9 · Pilot shape [§7]

OptionProsCons
a. 4 chosen scenarios + full 176-failure sweepAnswers "most failures?" directly; CPU makes the sweep cheap. Scenarios give interpretable cases.More runs for val to check; sweep labels rest on C4's rules.
b. 20 roots from one scene (source design)Controls for scene; smaller.Says nothing about other scenes or failure types, which is the question asked.
Recommendation: (a).

M10 · Coverage-gap threshold [§4]

OptionProsCons
a. 95th percentile of leave-one-demo-out distancesData-driven, per task and segment; means "farther than demos are from each other".Percentile is arbitrary; tight if demos are very similar.
b. Fixed by hand (e.g. 5 cm)Easy to explain.Ignores orientation, velocity and per-task spread.
Recommendation: (a), and report yield in bins of d/d_gap so the choice of percentile does not hide anything.
