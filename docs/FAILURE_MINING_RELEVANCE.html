What unit do people mine failures over, and does it matter to a client?

Literature review, 2026-09-29. Most papers were checked against their arXiv abstract page only, not read in full; the standards and blogs from search summaries. Check before citing.

Short answer. Measure at (policy, task, perturbation and its size). Report what matters to a client by weighting that with how often each perturbation actually happens in the client's deployment, times what a failure costs them.

  • Almost all VLA papers stop at the measurement. They report (policy, perturbation) averaged over tasks, or they hunt for as many failures as possible with no idea of how likely each one is.
  • Self-driving car safety answered your question years ago: an operational design domain (ODD) scopes which conditions count, and exposure weights how often they occur.
  • Our own PLAN.md §9.3 already says this: we ship P(fail | condition), the client supplies P(condition), and severity is computed at delivery. It hasn't been built.

What this means for us

  1. Take the client's ODD as input. Ask what their deployment looks like: camera mount tolerance, how much start poses vary, lighting range, clutter, instruction style. Map each answer onto our perturbation axes, which gives each axis a range. Test densely inside it, plus a margin band just outside.
  2. Estimate prevalence from their logs if they have them (camera extrinsics drift, the spread of joint start poses, lux). If they don't, a short site survey. This fills condition_prevalence, the field PLAN §9.3 already defines.
  3. The headline number becomes margin, not robustness: how far the edge of their ODD sits from our measured failure boundary. "Your camera can drift 12° before failures start; your mounts drift 3°" is a finding they can act on. A boundary well outside their ODD is good news, and it goes in the appendix.
  4. Report three tiers: (a) failure rate inside the ODD, weighted by prevalence; (b) margin to the boundary on each axis; (c) findings outside the ODD, labelled as out of scope for them. Brittleness out there is still a warning sign, because ODDs drift: a camera gets bumped, a new site is added.
  5. When a client can't describe an ODD, fall back to runtime monitoring and log mining (Gupta et al.). Their production failures define what's relevant, and our harness reproduces and explains them in simulation.

The gap, and a possible direction #8

Open: no VLA paper found combines failure search with an explicit deployment distribution (D2RL-style oversampling plus reweighting, over a declared ODD). The self-driving world does this; robot manipulation doesn't yet (the criticality-model paper, 2607.28251, is the first step, and it targets training, not evaluation). A pilot: define a mock ODD (e.g. "camera within ±10° yaw, start pose within 0.15 rad"), give it a prevalence distribution, and compare three estimates of the failure rate: uniform-grid LIBERO-Plus, failure search, and importance-weighted search. Show that only the weighted one matches brute-force Monte Carlo inside the ODD, at a fraction of the rollouts.
