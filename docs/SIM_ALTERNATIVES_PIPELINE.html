Verdict
  • Nothing here replaces LIBERO-Plus on MuJoCo as our primary substrate.
    • Every alternative either can't run on the 8 GB laptop (Isaac Sim 6.0 minimum is 16 GB VRAM and 32 GB RAM; Cosmos Transfer 2.5 needs 65.4 GB) or changes the pixels or physics the GR00T LIBERO checkpoint was trained on.
    • That would break our conformance gate. MuJoCo Warp needs mujoco ≥ 3.12, and our README pins < 3.4 because 3.4.0 silently changes a LIBERO task.
  • Isaac Lab-Arena v0.3 is closer to us than the 24 Sep landscape assumed, but not on our seams.
    • From the code: it records per-episode variation draws and seeds, per-step subtask-predicate events, and an sbi neural-posterior "sensitivity analysis" of factors vs success.
    • Isaac Lab underneath can record full per-step scene state and reset_to it.
    • It has no robot start-pose variation.
    • Its camera variation is translation-only, ±5 mm by default.
    • It has no paired counterfactual or input-level attribution.
    • GR00T support is the N1.6 path (DROID / G1 / GR1), with no LIBERO-Franka config.
  • No faithful LIBERO port to Isaac exists. Lightwheel's 130 "LIBERO tasks" are re-authored in their own kitchens with different USD assets. ROBOGATE measured a GR00T N1.6 LIBERO-Spatial checkpoint at 97.65% in MuJoCo and 0% on Isaac Sim scenes. RoboVerse's LIBERO port is bitwise-faithful but MuJoCo-only: it is a cross-implementation check, not a cross-simulator one.
  • Cosmos Transfer can't run closed-loop, and we don't need it for lighting. It re-renders 93-frame clips in minutes on datacenter GPUs. LIBERO-Plus lighting is MuJoCo light parameters (diffuse, direction, specular, shadows), so the fix is our knob, not a generative re-render.
  • World-model evaluators are not a perturbation pre-screen. The best real-vs-imagined success gap reported is 12% (PiL-World), larger than the effects we measure. Action-following degrades off the expert distribution, which is exactly where start-pose failures live.
  • The pipeline: keep our Rollout contract as the seam and add backends behind adapters. MuJoCo LIBERO-Plus stays primary, RoboVerse-native is the cross-implementation check, and Arena is the cloud backend for new tasks and scale. Put the policy behind a server that can execute splice arms. Use agents for authoring only. First spend: one rented-GPU Arena day, about $6–25 of GPU, with seven pass/fail questions (§8).

1. The question and the constraints

We need a simulation stack that keeps the four things that are ours, and adds scale or fidelity only where they don't cost those four:

  1. the Rollout contract (vla_harness/schema.py): per-step obs_state, actions, image refs, content-addressed ids, identity() and scene_descriptor() fingerprints, and a frozen RegressionSet;
  2. per-step privileged state for phase detection and closest-approach;
  3. paired same-seed counterfactuals (nominal.py, revert one knob);
  4. token and pixel splices inside the policy (R-039..R-051).

2b. Per-step state, seeding, replay with one factor reverted

  • verified Per-episode records. EpisodeRecorderManager writes one JSONL row per finished episode, with core fields (env id, indices, success, seed, timing), each variation's sampled value for that (env, episode), and progress-tracking state with predicate events. Variation draws are keyed by EnvEpisodeKey(env_id, episode_idx) (variations/variation_recorder.py).
  • verified Per-step predicate events. ProgressTrackingRecorder publishes, every step, PredicateEvent(env_idx, step, criteria_name, sequence_name, predicate_index, predicate_name, score_delta) for grasp, lift, transport and place. This is a direct analogue of our phase segmentation, computed from privileged state.
  • verified Full per-step state is in Isaac Lab, not Arena's default. PostStepStatesRecorder records scene.get_state(is_relative=True) each step (articulation joints, root poses, rigid objects). InitialStateRecorder and PreStepActionsRecorder exist. ManagerBasedEnv.reset_to(state, env_ids, seed, is_relative) restores a recorded state. Arena metrics already run through Isaac Lab's HDF5 recorder. Contacts need a ContactSensor; the EEF comes from the embodiment's ee_frame inferred.
  • verified Seeding. --seed (default 42) → env_cfg.seed; a separate placement_seed controls the layout solver; rebuilds offset the seed by the rebuild index.
  • Paired counterfactual, not built in. Nothing replays an episode with one variation reverted. Two ways to build it inferred:
    1. record the initial state, reset_to it, and force the sampler for one factor to its nominal value;
    2. for camera and lighting factors, render a second, nominal camera in the same env. That gives a paired observation every step for free, the Isaac analogue of our LiberoEnv.nominal_observation.
    Caveat: GPU PhysX determinism across different num_envs or hardware is not guaranteed. A zero-action replay-determinism check is the first thing to test (§8, Q6).

2c. What "sensitivity analysis" actually is

verified (analysis/sensitivity/). It reads episode_results.jsonl. Each episode's recorded variation draws become θ; vector draws are split per component and strings become categoricals. The chosen outcome fields (default success) become x. It then fits an sbi neural posterior estimator (NPE, or MNPE when any factor is categorical) under a uniform box prior, with continuous factors normalised to [0, 1]. Conditioning on success = 1, it samples the joint posterior and plots each factor's marginal (a density, or a bar per category). In words:

report = marginals of  p(θ | success = 1) ∝ p(success = 1 | θ) · Uniform(θ)(1)

So it shows which factor values are compatible with success: a behavioural factor → outcome map, the Bayesian cousin of our R-047 per-factor success curves. It says nothing about which input channel or time the failure travels through. Two cautions inferred:

  • With a uniform prior and a few hundred episodes, the posterior marginal is close to a smoothed success-rate curve per factor. It adds joint modelling, not new information.
  • It is sensitive to uneven sampling; the code warns when categoricals are unevenly drawn.

2d. Policy server: could GR00T N1.7 and our Rollout schema plug in?

  • verified Two wire paths.
    • π0/π0.5 via the openpi websocket msgpack protocol (policy/remote_policy_base.py, an EmbodimentAdapter packs observations into requests and receives action chunks; RemoteChunkReplayPolicy replays chunks closed-loop).
    • GR00T via GR00T's native PolicyClient against gr00t/eval/run_gr00t_server.py (isaaclab_arena_gr00t/policy/gr00t_remote_closedloop_policy.py).
  • verified The maintained GR00T is N1.6. Dockerfile.gr00t_1_6 downloads nvidia/GR00T-N1.6-3B; the serve-gr00t-policy skill says "maintained N1.6-DROID"; embodiment configs exist for droid, g1 and gr1. There is no LIBERO-Franka modality or embodiment config.
  • inferred N1.7 "unchanged". The wire protocol may carry over, since both are GR00T's own server. Our checkpoint (nvidia/gr00t17-lerobot-libero_spatial-640, embodiment_tag=libero_sim) would still need:
    1. an N1.7 server;
    2. a Franka joint and modality YAML matching LIBERO's 8-number state and 7-D OSC delta action;
    3. camera names and resolutions mapped to agent view and wrist.
    Even then it would see Isaac-rendered scenes it was never trained on (§2f), so its success in Arena would not be comparable to its LIBERO numbers.
  • inferred Emitting our Rollout. Feasible as a recorder term plus an offline converter:
    • Step.obs_state ← PostStepStatesRecorder + ee_frame;
    • Step.action ← PreStepActionsRecorder;
    • image_refs ← camera frames written per step;
    • perturbation ← the variation record;
    • success / termination ← episode record;
    • scene_descriptor ← env spec + layout;
    • identity() ← Arena commit + env spec hash + embodiment + policy config.
    The one field that does not map is diagnosis from splices: those need the policy in-process (§7).

2e. Can it run on 8 GB?

Not usefully inferred, from verified facts:

  • The laptop is half the 16 GB minimum before GR00T's 7.4 GB.

2f. Is there a LIBERO port to Isaac?

  • verified Official Arena: no. The only LIBERO mention is a README link to Lightwheel.
  • verified Lightwheel LW-BenchHub (Apache-2.0, built on Arena; README badge Isaac Lab 5.0.0; last commit 18 May 2026) ships "130 Lightwheel-LIBERO-Tasks". They are re-authored: each task is an LwTaskBase in Lightwheel kitchen fixtures, with their own USD assets (e.g. Bowl008.usd, Plate012.usd), their own placement regions and robot-base references, and robots including Franka, PandaOmron, G1, Piper, ARX and SO-100/101. It is semantically LIBERO, not pixel- or physics-faithful.
  • verified Why it matters. ROBOGATE (2603.22126): "a fine-tuned NVIDIA GR00T N1.6 (3B) trained on LIBERO-Spatial … achieves 97.65% success rate on LIBERO (MuJoCo) but 0% on RoboGate's 68 industrial scenarios in NVIDIA Isaac Sim". Those were different scenes, so this is a scene gap plus a simulator gap. Either way, our LIBERO checkpoint and our measured boundaries do not carry across.

Closed-loop at policy-input time: no. GR00T needs a new frame pair every 16 control steps. Transfer generates whole 93-frame clips conditioned on a control video, so it needs frames that don't exist yet. Even the distilled model takes minutes per clip on a datacenter GPU, and it cannot load on 8 GB inferred.

We don't need it for LIBERO-Plus lighting. The benchmark's lighting variants are MuJoCo light parameters (diffuse, direction, specular, shadows; robustness_of_exps.html §1). Our gap is the knob: it sets diffuse × (1 + m), clipped, and covers ~4% of the benchmark's variants. Reproducing the benchmark's own light settings in our env is a CPU-side change with no generative model. That is the fix.

Where Cosmos could help, offline only:

  1. appearance augmentation of training demos for D21, with DATAFARM's style warning applying in reverse: visual style must match too;
  2. re-rendering recorded first frames to test the forward-0 action under appearance shifts, open-loop.

Cosmos Policy (2601.16163, ICLR 2026; LIBERO 98.5%, RoboCasa 67.1% verified) is a policy, not a renderer. It is a candidate fourth architecture for direction #4. Its VRAM on 8 GB is unknown.

4. MuJoCo Warp

  • verified Version. pyproject.toml requires mujoco>=3.12.0. Our run venv is 3.3.2, and the README warns that ≥ 3.4.0 changes a LIBERO task. Adopting MJWarp means crossing the version boundary our reproduction gate exists to guard.
  • verified Unsupported features (io.py): body, actuator and sensor plugins; the noslip solver; some flex cases. Multi-CCD with margin is limited to one contact for some pairs.
  • verified Batch renderer. A Warp BVH ray tracer with Phong shading, per-geom-type texture mapping, skybox, and heterogeneous multi-camera support. It is not MuJoCo's OpenGL renderer, whose frames GR00T's LIBERO data came from. Expect a pixel-distribution shift that the conformance gate would have to clear inferred.
  • inferred Porting effort.
    • LIBERO scenes are MJCF, so the physics model probably loads.
    • robosuite's OSC_POSE controller, observation assembly and LIBERO's BDDL success checks are per-env Python/NumPy and would need batched GPU rewrites.
    • The LIBERO-Plus perturbation pipeline (XML edits per variant) would need re-plumbing.
    • Weeks, plus a full re-validation.
    The pay-off is throughput, and simulation is not our bottleneck: one GR00T forward on the shared 8 GB GPU is.

Verdict: no, for now. Revisit only if we move to a rented GPU and need thousands of MuJoCo envs for RL or data generation.

5. RoboVerse as a cross-simulator check

From RoboVerse's own LIBERO integration doc verified:

  • Passthrough: 130/130 LIBERO tasks with observations and steps bitwise identical; 10,120/10,120 LIBERO-Plus tasks with state, reward and done bitwise identical.
  • Native (no libero or robosuite import): all 130 base tasks on MetaSim's MuJoCo handler.
    • The BDDL checker has "0 mismatch vs env._check_success".
    • The OSC_POSE port's joint-torque difference is 5.55×10⁻¹⁵ N·m.
    • Render difference is mean MAE 1.85/255 from re-triangulated meshes.
  • MuJoCo only: "porting its MJCF to SAPIEN/Newton needs asset re-authoring + a different contact model = an approximate cross-sim port, not 1:1". It pins mujoco==3.2.3. A process-global EGL context forces sequential rendering. LIBERO-Plus variants are passthrough-only.

Answer. RoboVerse cannot test whether our start-pose boundary survives a different simulator. It can cheaply test whether it survives a different implementation: an independent OSC, an independent success checker and MuJoCo 3.2.3 instead of 3.3.2. That rules out the boundary being an artefact of our env wrapper or initial-state knob inferred. It runs on our laptop, since it is still MuJoCo. A true cross-simulator check of start pose would need an Isaac scene the policy was trained in, and no such checkpoint exists for LIBERO.

7. Recommended pipeline, with the Rollout contract as the seam

1 · Scenario specPerturbationSpec with the LIBERO-Plus factor names (LIBERO_PLUS_FACTORS), task, seed. Agents may draft it.
→
2 · Sim backends via adapters
  • (a) LIBERO-Plus MuJoCo: primary, local
  • (b) RoboVerse-native: cross-implementation check, local
  • (c) Isaac Lab-Arena: new tasks and scale, cloud
Each implements Env: reset(seed, spec), step, identity, scene_descriptor.
→
3 · Policy serverGR00T native server or openpi websocket, extended with splice arms: act(obs, arms=[T, I, S, A, W], noise=ε) returns one chunk per arm.
↓
4 · CaptureRollout: per-step privileged state, paired nominal renders (a second camera in Isaac; nominal_observation in MuJoCo), splice arms, content-addressed id
→
5 · Our diagnosistransfer τ per input, decided-at-forward-k, rescue, offset curves. Humans decide.
→
6 · DataOption A bridge-and-join on MuJoCo; GR00T-Mimic only for Isaac-native tasks
→
7 · Retrain → frozen regression gateLoRA; promote only through RegressionSet with non-target cells included
  • Why the splice must live in the policy server. A token or pixel splice needs the model's internals for the same observation and noise. Once the policy sits behind a wire (Arena's architecture), splices are possible only if the server implements them. Specifying "arms" in the request makes our mechanism layer simulator-agnostic, and is the single most valuable interface decision here inferred.
  • Where agents help: scenario authoring (Arena's NL → Env Spec), asset finding, wiring backends (Isaac Sim MCP and skills), writing adapters and recorder terms, and running experiment definitions.
  • Where they don't: diagnosis, deciding what data to collect, and promotion. AgenticRobotics (2608.07555) verified: the loop is an "operational claim, not a selection claim … not because the loop picks better checkpoints than a human; on the one lineage we measured, it does not." Its gates did buy false-promotion control (0.001 vs 0.005–0.021). Borrow the gates, not the decisions.
