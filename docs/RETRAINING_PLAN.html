From the map to a retrained policy

How many perturbation-and-correction pairs there are to find, what it takes to be sure of each, and what a retraining run costs, needs and should be expected to deliver.

1 · The map

How many pairs there are

A pair is a perturbation category on one side and the input that carries it on the other. The inputs are fixed by the architecture, so the map is small. At the pixel level there are three: the agent-view image, the wrist image and the proprioceptive state. At the token level there are three more, text positions, image positions and the state token, plus the pairwise combination. The benchmark has seven perturbation categories, and the harness adds continuous axes for camera pose, lighting brightness and start pose.

2 · The protocol per perturbation, and its size

What one row costs

Each rollout of an instance computes every arm at every forward from the same state and the same noise, so one rollout yields all the denoising numbers for that instance. Restoring an input and measuring how far the action returns toward nominal is the base measurement; the checks around it are what make it a claim.

  1. Denoising map. 10 instances of the category, one rollout each with all eight arms (P, N, text, image, state, image+text, agent pixels, wrist pixels). About 10 minutes. Yields the dominant input and the residual.
  2. Reverse direction. The same 10 instances at forward 0 only, corrupting one input of the nominal run. No rollouts, about 3 minutes. Yields necessity, and detects redundancy between inputs.
  3. Noise seeds. The 10 instances under two more fixed-noise seeds. 20 rollouts, about 20 minutes. Yields stability of the dominant input.
  4. Forward-0 rescue. Execute the corrected chunk for the first forward only, for the dominant input, a contrast input and the full nominal chunk. 30 rollouts, about 30 minutes. Yields whether the correction changes the outcome, and when the episode is decided.

Roughly 60 rollouts and an hour of GPU per perturbation category, plus a second checkpoint (another hour) if the claim is to be about the architecture rather than one fine-tune. Seven categories at that rate is a day. The continuous sweeps are separate: seven rollouts per scene and axis for the bisection form (R-041), or 36 per scene and axis for the probability form with replicates (R-047).

Decision rule for "sure", written before the remaining rows are run An input is the correction mechanism for a category when: restoring it alone recovers at least 0.8 of the first action's displacement on at least 8 of 10 instances; corrupting it alone in the nominal run reproduces at least 0.4 of the effect and more than any other single input; both hold under two further noise seeds; and executing its corrected chunk at forward 0 flips at least half of the failures beyond a no-op drive that re-runs the perturbed chunk. The no-op term was added after R-048 measured a 3-of-7 flip rate from render jitter alone. Start pose passes the first three; its rescue (7 of 7 with the nominal chunk, 4 of 7 with the wrist chunk) predates the no-op design, so the fourth is unconfirmed, though R-050 supports it independently: one corrected chunk halves ‖P−N‖ over the next forwards. Camera passes the first three and fails the fourth (one or two flips above the no-op). If the restoration is high but the corruption is low, the pose is readable from more than one input and the data specification must cover both.

3 · The test that matters

The map is a prediction until a policy is retrained

Everything above localises. None of it fixes anything. The claim that a category's data specification is right is only established by collecting that data, training, and measuring on held-out instances of the same category with repeated seeds. That is R-040 in general and R-046 for the start-pose category, and R-046 is out of scope for now by decision on 2026-09-24. What follows is what running it would take.

4 · Data

How much, and where it comes from

The original LIBERO-Spatial training set is 50 human demonstrations per task, 500 in all. The checkpoint in use was fine-tuned on that. A first retraining for the start-pose category should match that scale: 10 scenes × 5 radius bands (0.1 to 0.5 rad) × 10 demonstrations = 500, with an ablation at 100 and 250 to see how the gain scales. Each demonstration covers the approach, about one second of motion, since R-044 showed the episode is decided in the first forward.

6 · What to expect

Predictions, and what would count as failure

  • Starting point. On the ten mapped start-pose instances the current checkpoint fails 6 to 9 of 10 depending on the noise seed; the nominal control succeeds 10 of 10.
  • Prediction for the frozen arm. Closes at least 70 percent of that gap on held-out perturbed starts. R-044 showed the correct first chunk exists and rescues 7 of 7 (no no-op baseline; R-050 shows it keeps the episode near nominal afterwards); R-045 showed the pose is already in the VLM's output; so the head has the information and only lacks the mapping, which demonstrations supply directly.
  • Prediction for the unfrozen arm. Within 10 points of the frozen arm. If it is 15 or more points better, R-045's post-hoc reading was wrong and the VLM was part of the gap.
  • Regression. Under 5 points on the nominal control. Interference is the main risk of any targeted fine-tune.
  • Mechanism check after training. The wrist arm's transfer on the retrained checkpoint should fall, because a perturbed start no longer moves the first action much. If it does not fall while success rises, the policy learned to recover rather than to start correctly, which is a different fix than intended.
  • What would count as failure. Less than a third of the gap closed, or the frozen and unfrozen arms both flat: the data specification was wrong, and the direct corrective arm of R-040 becomes the comparison to run.
  • Variance. Outcomes flip about 30 percent under render jitter at one seed, so every number above is meaningful only over at least three seeds per instance. Closest approach is the continuous readout to report alongside.

7 · Order of work

If this is picked up

  1. Run the three unmapped categories through the hour-long protocol, language with the pre-VLM text swap. One day.
  2. R-047 overnight for the probability curves on start pose and yaw; the held-out set for training is then drawn from just beyond each scene's 50 percent point.
  3. Mint 500 start-pose demonstrations and write the converter. One day.
  4. Memory smoke of the LoRA fine-tune locally; rent a GPU if it does not fit. Half a day.
  5. Train both arms, evaluate with three seeds, run the wrist arm on the result. One day.
  6. Validate 20 to 50 minted demonstrations against teleoperated ones before extending the claim to real data.
