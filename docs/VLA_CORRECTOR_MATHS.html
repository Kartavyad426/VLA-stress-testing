Direct answers
  • What it is. A runtime layer around a frozen, chunked, flow-matching VLA, with no backbone retraining. Three parts:
    1. a ~40M token-wise MLP Mφ predicts how the policy's own SigLIP image tokens should change k steps after the executed action;
    2. the monitor scores 1 − cos(predicted change, actual change) every env step, and a robust median/MAD state machine decides that drift is persistent;
    3. on a trigger it discards the rest of the chunk and replans once with Online Gradient Guidance (OGG).
  • What OGG actually is code. It is not classifier guidance through the VLA. At guided denoising steps it detaches the velocity v and forms the one-shot clean estimate Â = xτ − τv (openpi time). It feeds only the first action Â[0] into Mφ and takes the gradient of a cosine loss with respect to v. The step is v ← v − η∇vℒ, with η = 1, on every 3rd of π0.5's 10 steps (pi05m.py:241–302). The gradient is non-zero only on row 0 of v derived; the other 49 actions change only indirectly, through attention in later steps.
  • Where the gains come from paper. On MetaWorld with π0.5 at horizon 50, success goes 48.7 → 64.35. Truncation alone (replan sooner, no OGG) gets 60.35, so OGG adds ~4 points and is negative on the Hard split (50.0 → 47.5). The method is mostly an event-triggered adaptive horizon; the gradient part is a modest add-on.
  • What it fixes. Mid-episode drift: 83.7% of truncations fall in grasp or align phases, and the largest real-robot gain is on "object moved mid-task" (+28 pp). Nothing in the paper tests a bad start. The released monitor cannot fire in the first 15 control steps: its threshold is 10.0 until a 15-score bootstrap queue fills (eval_det.py:500–566). Those 15 steps are almost exactly GR00T's forward 0 (16 executed steps).
  • Can our method be framed as a runtime correction in this family? Yes, but not by porting VLA-Corrector. Our failures are decided at forward 0 (R-057, k* = 1), and three things differ:
    • the detector is trivial: the start pose is in proprioception (‖q − qhome‖), so no learned visual monitor is needed;
    • the correction must act on the first chunk, before any drift can be observed;
    • the bar is re-homing (move the arm to its canonical start, then run the policy). That is free, oracle-free and the obvious competitor (Directions review C5).
    Of the three candidates:
    • (a) wrist-image or wrist-token substitution is the most promising mechanism-guided arm. W = 0.88 transfer means showing the policy a nominal wrist view moves its first chunk 88% of the way to nominal (R-042).
    • (b) OGG-style guidance needs a new objective (no drift exists yet at forward 0) and, on GR00T, would edit essentially one action.
    • (c) RTC-style inpainting is the cleanest way to splice a re-homing or bridge prefix into the policy's own chunk, and GR00T already ships native RTC code.
    §8.2 gives a pre-registrable 6-arm test on the R-053 instances plus an R-047 held-out grid, about 3 GPU-hours.

1. The problem it targets: the open-loop blind spot of action chunks

A chunked policy samples At = [at, …, at+C−1] ~ πθ(· | Zt, l), with Zt = E(ot) the visual latent and l the instruction, and executes the first H ≤ C open-loop paper eq. 1. Observations keep arriving during those H steps but are ignored. A disturbance inside the window therefore compounds until the next call. The paper's motivating sweep shows the trade-off: π0.5 on MetaWorld drops from ≈ 64% to below 49% as H grows, with about 4× fewer policy calls (Fig. 2, Table 4). The paper's thesis is that the question is not which fixed H to use, but when to stop trusting the current chunk.

2. The method from first principles

2.1 The latent dynamics corrector Mφ

From demonstration transitions (ot, at, ot+k), passed through the frozen policy's own visual encoder paper eq. 2–4:

∆Z*t+k = E(ot+k) − E(ot),    ∆Ẑt+k = Mφ(Zt, at)(1)
ℒcorr = ‖∆Ẑ − ∆Z*‖²₂ + β [ 1 − cos(∆Ẑ, ∆Z*) ](2)

Why a residual. Predicting the change rather than the next latent removes the static background, which would otherwise dominate an MSE on hundreds of tokens.

What the network is code siglip_dynamics_mlp.py:65–133:

  • The action is embedded once and broadcast to every token. Each token is concatenated with it and mapped by the same residual MLP (width [2048]×4), then projected back to the token width.
  • Tokens are processed independently; there is no cross-token mixing. "40M" is the per-token network. In effect it is a learned optical-flow-like predictor per patch, conditioned on the action.
  • Training uses AdamW 3·10−4, cosine schedule, 30 epochs, batch 512 for the deployed "h1-k10" correctors paper App. C.3. So k = 10 control steps with a history of 1 frame inferred from the name.
  • The deployed models are trained with "a cosine-based training loss", i.e. not exactly (2).

The latent code pi05m.py:361–377: encode_siglip_only runs embed_image (SigLIP vision tower + multimodal projector) on every present camera and concatenates the token blocks. For π0.5 on LIBERO that means agent view and wrist tokens, about 2 × 256 tokens of width 2048 inferred.

2.2 The online inconsistency score

Et = 1 − cos( ∆Zexpt, ∆Zrealt ),   ∆Zexpt = Mφ(Zt−k, at−k),   ∆Zrealt = Zt − Zt−k(3)

Cosine is computed on the flattened token stack, so Et ∈ [0, 2] code eval_det.py:1201–1212.

  • Scale-free. Cosine ignores the magnitude of the change. A correct direction at the wrong speed scores 0. A stalled robot (∆Zreal ≈ 0) gives an ill-defined cosine; the code adds 10−4 noise when ‖∆Zreal‖ < 10−4 (:1202–1205), so a stall scores ≈ 1 at random derived.
  • One action predicts k steps. The prediction uses only at−k, the first of the k actions actually executed. The other k − 1 actions are unseen by Mφ, so it must learn the typical continuation of the demos after at−k. Et measures "is the scene changing the way demos usually change after an action like this", which is an on-manifold test, not a prediction of the commanded effect derived.

2.3 The trigger

Paper version paper eq. 6–7, 12–13, App. B.2:

Ton = Me + λon·MAD,   Toff = Me + λoff·MAD over the last w scores;   ct = ct−1+1 if Et > Ton; 0 if Et < Toff; else ct−1;   fire when ct ≥ p(4)

The stated parameters are w = 15, λon = 3, λoff = 2, p = 5, 5 safe steps to reset, and a 10-step cooldown. The threshold is self-referenced to the episode's own recent scores, so no calibration set and no false-alarm guarantee are involved. Compare FAIL-Detect's conformal band or CheckVLA's conformal threshold.

Code version code eval_det.py:500–610, the state machine actually used in evaluation. It is richer than the paper's:

  • Et is smoothed by an EWMA (α = 0.35) before thresholding.
  • Thresholds are floored at 0.25 (base_noise_floor).
  • No trigger is possible until 15 scores have been collected: the threshold is 10.0, above the maximum of 2, during bootstrap.
  • A jump trigger fires immediately when the EWMA rises by more than 0.06 in one step and is above Ton. This contradicts the paper's "only after p consecutive steps".
  • A hard re-trigger fires after 3 steps 0.08 above Ton, even in cooldown.
  • The trigger margin is 0.02 and the default cooldown is 0 (the paper says 10).

Scores exist only from t ≥ k, so with k = 10 and a 15-score bootstrap the earliest possible interrupt is around env step 25 derived.

2.4 Truncation: the adaptive horizon

On an interrupt the remaining queue is cleared (action_plan.clear(), eval_det.py:1236–1237) and the next call is made immediately. If h actions of the planned H had executed, the realised horizon is Hadaptive = h < H paper. This part alone produces most of the gain (§5).

2.5 Online Gradient Guidance: exactly what is guided

Here the paper and the code agree. The equations are in openpi time (τ = 1 noise, τ → 0 data; see docs/FLOW_MATCHING_MATHS.html §0).

Â0(τ) = Aτ − τvτ,    â = Â0[0],    ∆Ẑact = Mφ(Zt, â)(5)
∆Zcorr = ∆Zexp − ∆Zdev = Mφ(Zt−k, at−k) − (Zt − Zt−k)(6)
ℒOGG = 1 − cos(∆Ẑact, ∆Zcorr),    vguideτ = vτ − η∇vτℒOGG,    Aτ−∆τ = Aτ − ∆τ·vguideτ(7)

code In pi05m.py:241–302, v_in = v_t.detach().requires_grad_(True); x0_hat = x_t − t·v_in; a = x0_hat[:,0,:]; loss.backward(); v_t = v_in − eta·v_in.grad. The target is delta_z_right − delta_z_t, with delta_z_right the corrector's prediction and delta_z_t the real residual at the trigger (eval_det.py:1234–1235), which is (6) exactly. Guidance runs when step % 3 == 0: steps 0, 3, 6, 9 of π0.5's 10. The default η in the config is 0.0, and the paper's η = 1 is set on the command line.

Consequences derived:

  • The gradient lives on one row. â depends on v only through v[0], so ∇vℒ = −τ·(∂ℒ/∂â) on row 0 and zero on rows 1…C−1. The guided step moves the first action's denoising trajectory directly; later actions move only because later denoising steps attend to the changed row 0.
  • The step size shrinks toward the data end. It is η·τ·∂ℒ/∂â: largest at the noise end (τ = 1), and multiplied by τ = 0.1 at the last guided step (step 9). Most of the steering happens early, where it is then partly washed out by subsequent unguided denoising.
  • It is not classifier guidance. Classifier guidance would add ∇x log p(y|xτ) through the denoiser. OGG never differentiates through the VLA. It is a cheap, local edit of the velocity through a 40M MLP, which is why it is "plug-in" for any flow head that exposes v.
  • The target direction is a heuristic. (6) asks the first action's predicted effect to point along "what should have happened minus what did happen", i.e. back toward the expected trajectory. It is a direction-only objective (cosine), so the magnitude of the correction is set by the flow prior, not by the deviation's size.

4. Policies and cost

  • Backbones paper: π0.5 (main), SmolVLA, X-VLA, all flow-matching chunked VLAs fine-tuned per benchmark. No diffusion-policy or autoregressive VLA is tested. GR00T N1 is cited, not tested.
  • Training cost. Only Mφ, on cached frozen features (App. C), on 8×A100-40GB. Mφ sizes: 10M is worse, and 160M is no better than 40M (Table 9).
  • Runtime cost paper App. D.3, MetaWorld:
    • a standard chunk call takes 278 ms, an OGG recovery call 588 ms (2.12×);
    • per episode, wall-clock is 1.62× for π0.5 (2.49 → 4.03 s), with 6.2 calls per episode and about 3.6 OGG events per episode (71.28 per task of 20 episodes);
    • the monitor itself is one vision-tower forward and one Mφ forward per environment step, a cost the paper does not separate out inferred.

6. Critique

  • Most of the gain is the adaptive horizon, not the guidance. Truncation alone gives +11.65 of the +15.65 (Table 6). OGG's extra +4.0 is inside the ±3 pp scale per split, negative on Hard, and its recovery metric is Et falling below Toff: a monitor-defined outcome, not task success paper derived.
  • OGG edits one action. The gradient lives on row 0 of the velocity (§2.5). For a 50-step chunk, "guided replanning" is a nudge to the first action plus whatever the attention layers propagate. That is consistent with a modest effect derived.
  • The truncation comparison is not matched. Without the monitor, the natural control is "replan whenever a cheap trigger fires", e.g. RTC-style continuous replanning or a proprioceptive-error trigger. The only detector ablation is a coupled internal head that also changes the backbone (Table 7). A random-trigger control at matched call count, which would tell whether when to truncate matters or just that replanning happens more in critical phases, is missing inferred.
  • Table 1 and Table 4 disagree for π0.5 at H = 50 (64.35 vs 58.70), and the backbones' horizons in Table 1 look different inferred.
  • The code is richer than the paper: EWMA, floor, bootstrap, jump trigger, cooldown 0 vs 10. Reproduction should use the eval script's state machine, not §3.3's description or circuit_breaker.py code.
  • No false-alarm control. The thresholds are relative to the episode's own scores, so the false-trigger rate on clean episodes is not reported or bounded. It is unknown how often a nominal success is interrupted and what that costs.
  • The monitor needs a domain-matched corrector (Table 10: LIBERO-trained on MetaWorld gives +3.1 vs +10.0). The latent is the policy's own SigLIP tokens of all cameras, so wrist motion dominates the residual during manipulation inferred.
  • Blind at the start, by construction. No score before step k, and no trigger before the bootstrap fills (≈ step 25). It cannot address failures decided in the first chunk, and the paper does not claim to.

8.2 The key question: a deployable, mechanism-guided first-chunk correction

What we know ours:

  • Decided at forward 0 (R-057). Start-pose success with the nominal chunk driven for k forwards is 24 / 22 / 21 / 19 of 30 for k = 1, 2, 4, all, against 6/30 with no correction. So k* = 1.
  • The forward-0 rescue is real but an oracle (R-053). The nominal first chunk flips 20/24 P-failures, against 0/22 for a plain re-run. The nominal chunk comes from the recorded control episode, aligned by forward index (res21's audit; Directions review C5).
  • The wrist camera carries it (R-042). Restoring only the wrist pixels to nominal transfers W = 0.88 of the forward-0 action gap (median, 7 failing instances), against A = 0.17 for the agent view and IT = 0.96 for both token blocks. Stable across seeds at 0.85.
  • Coverage, not capacity, limited retraining. The adapters closed 30% on training starts and 12% held-out.

Two simplifications VLA-Corrector does not have derived:

  • The detector is free. The perturbation is the joint configuration, which proprioception reports exactly. A forward-0 detector is ‖q0 − qhome‖ > ε, or better the D20 achieved gripper offset in cm, which predicts success: ≤ 8 cm 0.93, 8–12 cm 0.65, 12–16 cm 0.22, > 16 cm 0.15. No learned visual monitor is needed, and it has a calibrated meaning.
  • The obvious correction needs no mechanism: re-homing. Drive the arm to qhome with a joint-space controller, then start the policy. It is oracle-free, training-free and costs ~1 s of motion. Any mechanism-guided arm must beat it, or be preferable where re-homing is impossible (cluttered workspace, a real robot holding an object).

    • time-to-success;
    • the nominal control (base ≥ 26/27, the R-055 convention) to show no harm at qhome.
  • Cost: 6 arms × 30 rollouts ≈ 180 rollouts at ~45 s (R-057's rate) ≈ 2.3 GPU-hours, plus a held-out grid of ≈ 1 GPU-hour. No training except fitting δ for (a2).
  • Reading:
    • (a1) ≈ oracle and > re-home → a mechanism-guided runtime fix exists, and the wrist finding becomes actionable;
    • re-home ≈ oracle → the start-pose row is solved by a 1-second manoeuvre, and mechanism work should move to rows without a free fix (camera);
    • (a2) > P on held-out → parameter-only steering generalises where retraining did not.
The gap to keep in view. VLA-Corrector, CheckVLA, Rewind-IL and RTC all address failures that develop during execution, observable as a mismatch between expected and actual change. Ours are decided before any change is observed (k* = 1), are fully visible in proprioception, and have a trivial physical fix. The publishable angle is therefore not "another detector" (Directions review: detection is crowded). It is a measured comparison of a mechanism-specified correction (wrist evidence) against re-homing and against retraining at matched cost. That comparison is only interesting if (a1) or (a2) wins somewhere re-homing cannot be used.
