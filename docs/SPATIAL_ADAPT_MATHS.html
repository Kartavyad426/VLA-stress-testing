"VLA models are more generalizable than you think": the maths, the method, and what holds up

2026-10-06 · res21 · Paper: Li, Zhang, Zhai, Lin, Wang (Sun Yat-sen University / X-Era AI Lab), arXiv:2512.02902v2 (31 Mar 2026), CVPR 2026 · No code released (searched 2026-10-06) · Implementation facts checked against openpi main (pi0_config.py, pi0.py, lora.py, gemma.py, siglip.py). Tags: paper stated in the paper · verified checked by me against code or by arithmetic · inferred my reading where the paper is silent.
Bottom line
  • The claim. When a VLA fails under a new camera viewpoint, the fault is in the vision encoder ("Spatial Modeling"), not in the language model or action expert ("Physical Modeling"). So adapting only the vision side, with one demo per task, should restore performance. On π0.5 in LIBERO with novel cameras: zero-shot 48.5%; a 4K-parameter per-channel affine on the visual tokens (FTM) reaches 87.1%; rank-16 LoRA inside SigLIP (FLA, 4.7M params) reaches 90.8%; the paper's "LoRA" baseline, at 467M params, reaches 90.3%.
  • What holds up. The direction is plausible, and FTM is a striking result: 4,096 numbers, applied identically to every visual token, recover most of the camera gap. That says the drift is largely a global per-channel statistics shift, the kind AdaBN-style recalibration fixes. That is a real and cheap-to-test finding.
  • What doesn't.
    1. The 467M "LoRA" baseline is mostly a full fine-tune of SigLIP (414.8M). openpi's LoRA freeze filter never freezes the vision tower verified. The "99× fewer parameters" comparison is therefore low-rank vision tuning vs full vision tuning plus LoRA elsewhere.
    2. The control that would isolate the claim is missing. That control is to adapt the language model and action expert with vision frozen.
    3. FLA beats LoRA by 0.5 pt, against a ≈1.9 pt 95% interval on a single seed.
    4. The appendix theory proves bounds against a single mean token. It says nothing about closed-loop success.
  • A table error. Table 3 prints FTM's Libero-V average as 90.5%; its own row averages to 93.2%, so the paper under-sells its smallest method verified.
  • For us. Our GR00T retrains (R-053..R-058) adapt the action head and VL self-attention with tune_visual: False. That is exactly the "Physical Modeling" side, the opposite of FLA. R-039 already found that image tokens carry every perturbation category to the action. A training-free version of FTM, moment matching on cached backbone features, is an afternoon's experiment on 8 GB. See §9A (portability) and §10.

1. The claim, and what it would take to prove it

The paper splits a VLA π into two functional halves paper:

Spatial Modelingthe vision encoder fv (SigLIP So400m/14 in π0/π0.5): image → visual tokens z. Supposed to encode positions, orientations, contacts, occlusion.
→
Physical Modelingthe VLM (Gemma 2B) + action expert (Gemma 300M): z, language ℓ, state → action chunk. Supposed to do the task reasoning and control.

The argument is a short syllogism:

  1. A viewpoint shift changes how the scene looks, not what the task is or how the arm moves.
  2. So the Physical half should still be competent; it is being fed tokens from the wrong distribution.
  3. So adapting only fv (or its output) with a tiny number of parameters should recover most of the loss.

Step 3 is evidence consistent with the hypothesis, not proof of it. Proving "the fault is in Spatial, not Physical" needs a 2×2:

Adapt vision onlyAdapt VLM + expert only (vision frozen)
Prediction if the claim is truerecovers most of the gaprecovers little, at matched parameter count and data
In the paper?Yes (FTM, FLA)No. The "LoRA" baseline adapts both, and its vision part is a full fine-tune (§5).

Without the right-hand column, "adapting vision is sufficient" is shown. "The brittleness originates in vision" is not. A large enough network can usually route around a fault from either side. That vision-side adaptation is cheaper is the more defensible reading of the results.

2. π0.5 from first principles

The paper's §3.1 (Eq. 1–2) describes π0.5 as autoregressively emitting discrete action tokens. That is wrong for how π0.5 acts. The appendix (§11) then describes the flow-matching action expert correctly. Here is the model as openpi implements it.

2.1 Architecture

SigLIP So400m/14224×224 → 16×16 = 256 patches per camera; width 1152, 27 layers, MLP 4304 verified
Projector ("head")Dense 1152 → 2048 per token. FTM sits here (§3)
Gemma 2B (VLM)width 2048, depth 18, 8 heads, 1 KV head, head dim 256, MLP 16384 verified
Gemma 300M (action expert)width 1024, depth 18, MLP 4096; shares attention with the VLM layer by layer

The prefix is image tokens from both cameras (agent view and wrist), then language tokens. In π0.5 the discretised state also goes in as text. The prefix uses bidirectional attention. The suffix is H noisy action tokens, attending to the prefix and to each other; the prefix cannot attend to the suffix. The paper's "Physical Modeling" is everything in orange.

2.2 Flow matching, in openpi's convention

Let A = at:t+H be a ground-truth action chunk and ε ~ 𝒩(0, I). openpi interpolates from data at τ = 0 to noise at τ = 1 verified (pi0.py:197–200):

xτ = τ ε + (1 − τ) A,    u = dxτ/dτ = ε − A(2.1)
ℒflow(θ) = 𝔼τ, ε, (o, ℓ, A) ‖ vθ(xτ, τ, o, ℓ) − (ε − A) ‖²(2.2)
τ = 0.999 · s + 0.001,   s ~ Beta(1.5, 1)(2.3)

Beta(1.5, 1) has density ∝ s0.5, mean 0.6, mass piled toward s = 1. In this convention τ = 1 is pure noise, so training over-samples the noisy end. At inference, start at x1 = ε and Euler-integrate backwards with K = 10 steps (dt = −1/K, pi0.py:228):

xτ−1/K = xτ − (1/K) · vθ(xτ, τ, o, ℓ),    τ = 1, 1 − 1/K, …, 1/K(2.4)

Why this works: the conditional field u = ε − A is a straight line from data to noise. The network learns its conditional expectation 𝔼[ε − A | xτ, o]. That marginal field transports noise to the action distribution (Lipman et al. 2023). Integrating it backwards from noise gives a sample from π(A | o).

Corrections to the paper's §11
  • The paper writes aτ = τA + (1−τ)ω (data at τ = 1) and then regresses onto ω − A. With its own interpolation, the derivative is A − ω. It notes the sign ambiguity but does not resolve it. Use (2.1)–(2.2).
  • "Beta(1.5, 1) to emphasise learning at low-noise levels" is backwards in openpi's convention: it emphasises high noise. The π0 paper also states its timestep schedule emphasises noisier timesteps.
  • Eq. 1–2 (autoregressive discrete action tokens) describe π0-FAST or π0.5's FAST co-training head, not the policy that produces actions in these experiments.

2.3 Time conditioning: adaRMSNorm

π0.5 injects τ into the action expert by modulating every RMSNorm paper verified (time_mlp_in/out in pi0.py):

adaRMS(x, τ) = (x / rms(x)) ⊙ (1 + γ(τ)) + β(τ),    [γ(τ), β(τ)] = MLP(sincos(τ))(2.5)

Note the form: (1 + γ) ⊙ · + β. That is exactly FTM's form (§3), except FTM's γ, β are constant rather than functions of τ. FTM is "FiLM with no conditioning input", applied to the visual tokens.

2.4 Attention mask and knowledge insulation

The paper lists three mask rules paper:

  1. The expert attends to the VLM.
  2. The VLM does not attend to the expert.
  3. The expert does not attend to discrete FAST action tokens, so it cannot copy the answer.

The joint pretraining loss is ℒ = ℒdiscrete + α ℒflow. None of this matters for FTM/FLA: during adaptation only the flow loss can be in play, and only the visual parameters receive gradients inferred. The paper does not say which loss the adaptation uses.

3. Feature Token Modulation (FTM)

3.1 The operation

Let F ∈ ℝN×D be the visual tokens for one image, with N = 256 patches and D = 2048. Learn γ, β ∈ ℝD, shared across all tokens, both cameras, all tasks paper:

F̂n,c = (1 + γc) · Fn,c + βc    for every token n, channel c(3.1)

Trainable parameters: 2D = 4,096 verified. Everything else is frozen. The objective is ℒflow (2.2) on one demonstration per task from the new viewpoint. Initialising γ = β = 0 makes the adapted model start as exactly the pretrained one.

Where FTM actually sits

The paper calls D the "hidden dimension of the visual encoder" and sets it to 2048. SigLIP So400m's width is 1152. 2048 is Gemma 2B's width, i.e. the output of the 1152→2048 projector verified (siglip.py widths; pi0.py:83 num_classes=paligemma_config.width). So FTM modulates post-projector tokens, at the boundary between Spatial and Physical. Fig. 4a also shows a dashed "Learnable tokens → MLP" box "used only during training and removed at inference". That suggests γ, β may be produced by a small MLP during training and frozen to constants afterwards. If so, the training-time parameter count exceeds 4K. The text never explains this inferred.

3.2 What a per-channel affine can and cannot fix

Write the map as A(z) = Mz + b with M = diag(1 + γ). Three consequences:

  • It cannot move information between tokens. The same map applies to every patch. A viewpoint change moves the bowl from patch 37 to patch 112; FTM cannot "move it back". The frozen VLM must already be tolerant to where things appear. FTM can only fix how each channel is scaled and centred.
  • It cannot rotate or mix channels. Diagonal M has no off-diagonal terms. A drift that is a rotation in feature space is invisible to it.
  • It has a closed-form, gradient-free version. If the drift were purely a per-channel shift in mean and scale, the optimal γ, β just match first and second moments. With μs, σs the source-view channel means and standard deviations (over tokens and frames), and μt, σt the target-view ones:
1 + γc = σsc / σtc,    βc = μsc − (1 + γc) μtc(3.2)

This is AdaBN (Li et al. 2016) or test-time normalisation, applied at the token boundary. It needs target images only: no actions and no demo. The paper never runs it. If (3.2) gets close to 87%, the "one-shot demonstration" is not what is doing the work. The demo's actions would be irrelevant and only its images matter. This is the single most informative missing baseline, and it is cheap (§10, E1).

FTM's 87.1% therefore says something specific: most of the camera-shift damage, as seen by π0.5, is a global per-channel distribution shift of the visual tokens, not a spatial rearrangement the VLM cannot handle.

4. Feature Linear Adaptation (FLA) and LoRA

4.1 The operation

For a frozen linear map h = Wx, W ∈ ℝdout×din, LoRA (Hu et al. 2022) adds a rank-r update:

h = Wx + (α / r) · BAx,    A ∈ ℝr×din, B ∈ ℝdout×r(4.1)

Trainable parameters per matrix: r(din + dout). The paper's Eq. 5 omits the α/r scale. openpi uses α = r, so the scale is 1, and it initialises both A and B as 𝒩(0, 0.01²) verified (lora.py:20,30). The textbook init is B = 0. With openpi's init the adapted model is not exactly the pretrained one at step 0, but the error is tiny (each entry of BA ~ r·10−4). The paper does not say which LoRA implementation FLA uses for SigLIP.

4.2 Which matrices: the parameter count tells us

The paper says FLA is applied to "the linear layers (27 layers) of the SigLIP ViT encoder", 4.7M parameters at r = 16 and 9.4M at r = 32. Each SigLIP block has six linear maps: Q, K, V, O (1152→1152) and MLP fc1 (1152→4304), fc2 (4304→1152). Counting r(din+dout) × 27 verified:

Target setr = 16r = 32Matches paper?
all six (Q, K, V, O, fc1, fc2)8.70M17.39Mno
attention only (Q, K, V, O)3.98M7.96Mno
MLP only (fc1, fc2)4.71M9.43Myes, both

So FLA is rank-r LoRA on the two MLP matrices of each of SigLIP's 27 blocks, attention untouched. That is useful information the paper doesn't state. It also means FLA cannot change which patches attend to which. It can only re-map what each patch's features become.

4.3 What FLA can do that FTM cannot

FTM is a fixed affine map applied after the encoder. FLA changes the encoder's internal MLPs, so its correction is input-dependent and nonlinear. The update BAx passes through GELU and 26 further blocks. It can mix channels and respond differently to different image content. The rank gives a budget: each layer's correction lives in an r-dimensional subspace of the input features. The paper's Table 8 rank sweep (8 → 64) is flat to within noise for π0.5 (91.05 → 90.8 → 91.2 → 90.6). r = 8, at 2.36M parameters, already saturates. That is consistent with the needed correction being low-dimensional.

5. The baselines, decoded

5.1 The "One-Shot LoRA" baseline is mostly a full SigLIP fine-tune

The paper describes the π0.5 LoRA baseline as Gemma 2B LoRA r = 16 plus Gemma 300M LoRA r = 32, attention and FFN, "keeping all non-LoRA weights frozen via the model-provided freeze filter" paper. openpi's freeze filter is verified (pi0_config.py:88–117):

  • The "99× fewer parameters" claim compares FLA with a full vision fine-tune. 89% of the baseline's trainable parameters are the Spatial module. "FLA matches LoRA" really means rank-16 MLP updates to SigLIP match a full SigLIP update plus LoRA on the rest. That is a fair PEFT result, but not "vision-only beats whole-model".
  • It modestly supports the spatial hypothesis in one direction. Adding LoRA capacity to the Physical half on top of a full vision update buys nothing over FLA (90.3 vs 90.8).
  • It does not supply the missing control (§1): there is no run with SigLIP frozen.

5.2 Prompt learning (0.131M)

Learnable tokens, 𝒩(0, 0.02²) init, are concatenated as a prefix to the multimodal sequence (width 2048) and as a suffix between the state and action tokens (width 1024). The backbone is frozen; "the action head" is also trained paper, presumably the small action in/out projections inferred. Its Libero-V score is 75.1%. That is below π0.5 zero-shot (83.6%), so the prompt-learning baseline makes the model worse. The paper presents this as "modest improvement". A baseline that underperforms doing nothing usually signals a tuning problem.

5.3 GeoAware-VLA and OpenVLA-OFT-m

GeoAware-VLA replaces the encoder with frozen VGGT plus a trained projector, and trains the policy from scratch. Its numbers are copied from its paper paper. OpenVLA-OFT-m is fine-tuned on LIBERO-Plus. Both are zero-shot on the test viewpoint. Every π0/π0.5 row in Tables 1–2 saw a demonstration from the test viewpoint. The headline table therefore ranks zero-shot generalisers against one-shot adapted models.

6. The Libero-V benchmark

6.1 Camera pose

Following Adapt3R, the agent-view camera is moved and the wrist camera left alone paper. Continuous mode orbits the end-effector in the horizontal plane:

r = ‖pxycam − pxyeef‖₂,   p′cam = ( r cos θ + pxeef, r sin θ + pyeef, pzcam ),   R′ = Rz(θ) Rref(6.1)

Two points about (6.1). It places the camera at absolute angle θ around the end-effector, not at θ relative to its original bearing. And composing Rz(θ) with Rref keeps the camera pointed at the workspace only if the original camera already pointed at the end-effector's vertical axis inferred. Discrete mode defines Small / Medium / Large as fixed translation offsets Δp plus fixed quaternions. The offsets themselves are not given, so the levels cannot be reproduced from the paper.

6.2 Lighting, texture, noise

These are taken from LIBERO-Plus paper:

  • Lighting: procedurally regenerated MuJoCo XML, changing diffuse RGB, light direction d ∈ ℝ³, specular and shadows.
  • Texture: PBR floor and wall materials swapped at init; geometry unchanged.
  • Sensor noise: motion blur, Gaussian blur (σ ∈ [1, 10]), zoom blur, fog (plasma fractal), glass blur, each at 10 severities, applied in step().

Our own LIBERO-Plus reading (docs/LIBERO_PLUS_LEVELS.md) found that noise is applied to the agent view only; the wrist camera is clean. The same holds for camera pose. So in every Libero-V perturbation, π0.5 still has an unperturbed wrist view. How much of the adapted policies' recovery leans on the wrist camera is not measured (§9).

6.3 What "one-shot" means here

  • Data: one human demonstration per task, recorded from the new viewpoint. In LIBERO that is 10 demos per suite, 40 for all four suites.
  • Training: batch 32 for 1,500 (FLA) or 5,000 (FTM) steps, with heavy repetition of a single trajectory per task.
  • Not stated:
    • whether one adapted model serves all tasks and suites, or one per suite or per viewpoint level inferred: probably per suite;
    • whether the evaluated viewpoint equals the demo viewpoint;
    • where the "human" demos come from in simulation.
  • Evaluation: 50 trials per task paper.

"Generalizable" in the title therefore means adaptable with one in-domain demo. Zero-shot, π0.5 drops from in-distribution to 48.5% under the camera shift.

7. The theory appendix, restated and checked

7.1 Setup and assumptions

Fix the decoder's other inputs (a<t, ℓ). Write ga|z for the action distribution given visual tokens z. 𝒟s, 𝒟t are the source and target image distributions paper.

  • A1 (Lipschitz): dTV(ga|z, ga|z′) ≤ L ‖z − z′‖ locally.
  • A2 (semantic invariance): the optimal action distribution is the same under 𝒟s and 𝒟t.
  • A3 (affine / low-rank): the map from target tokens to source tokens is approximately affine, or a low-rank linear correction.

7.2 Theorem 1 measures the wrong distance

𝔼v~𝒟t dTV( ga|fv(v), ga|z⋆s ) ≤ L 𝔼v~𝒟t ‖ fv(v) − z⋆s ‖,    z⋆s = 𝔼v~𝒟s fv(v)(7.1)

The algebra is correct; it is A1 plus taking expectations. But z⋆s is one vector, the mean source token, and the bound compares every target observation's action distribution against the action distribution for that average image. A good policy must act differently at different states, so the right-hand side is large for the source distribution too. The adaptation that minimises the bound maps every image to z⋆s, which destroys the policy. The quantity that matters compares each target observation with the same state seen from the source camera.

7.3 Theorem 2 assumes its conclusion

The theorem says: if an affine map achieves 𝔼‖Mfv(v) + b − z⋆s‖² ≤ ε², then 𝔼 dTV ≤ Lε, via Jensen. The step is valid. But the conclusion is the hypothesis multiplied by L. It inherits the mean-token problem of (7.1). It also says nothing about whether a diagonal M, which is what FTM is, can achieve small ε. A3 is called "mild and empirically verifiable", and the paper never verifies it.

7.4 Theorem 3 mixes weight error with feature error

Eckart–Young is correct. If ΔW⋆ has singular values σ₁ ≥ σ₂ ≥ …, the best rank-r approximation has ‖ΔW⋆ − ΔWr‖F² = Σi>r σi². Four problems:

  • No defined target. ΔW⋆, "the optimal linear correction to a vision layer", is never defined. FLA does not approximate a target matrix at all; it is trained end-to-end on the behaviour-cloning loss.
  • The wrong norm for one layer. For a single layer the feature error is ‖(ΔW⋆ − ΔWr)x‖ ≤ σr+1 ‖x‖, the spectral tail, not the Frobenius tail.
  • 27 nonlinear layers, not one. FLA edits 54 matrices across 27 blocks. Errors pass through GELU, LayerNorm and attention, and are amplified by the Lipschitz constants of every later layer. "Propagates linearly" holds for one isolated linear layer only.
  • No closed loop. Every bound is per-step. A policy runs for many steps, and small per-step errors move it into states it never trained on.

7.5 A version that would mean something

Let (vs, vt) be the same simulator state rendered from the source and target cameras. LIBERO makes this coupling free. Define the paired adaptation error and use A1 per state:

εpair = 𝔼s~dπ̂ ‖ A(fv(vt(s))) − fv(vs(s)) ‖,    δ = 𝔼s~dπ̂ dTV(π̂(·|s), πs(·|s)) ≤ L εpair(7.2)

Here π̂ is the adapted policy on the target camera and πs is the original policy on the source camera. For a binary success reward over T policy decisions (chunks), the standard simulation-lemma argument bounds the success gap by the total-variation distance between trajectory distributions, which sums per-step TV under π̂'s own state distribution:

| SR(π̂, target) − SR(πs, source) | ≤ Σk=1T 𝔼sk~dπ̂k dTV(π̂(·|sk), πs(·|sk)) ≤ T · L · εpair(7.3)

The T factor is the cost of being closed-loop. A LIBERO episode of a few hundred control steps, executed in chunks, is tens of decisions. This bound is loose but honest, and its ingredients are measurable. εpair can be computed for zero-shot, FTM, FLA, and the closed form (3.2), on the same paired states. That replaces the t-SNE figure (§9) with a number.

9A.3 Why the DeepStack paths matter

π0.5 has one place where vision becomes language-model input. GR00T has four. An FTM applied only to the merger output leaves three unmodulated visual paths adding raw ViT features into LLM layers 0–2.

  • FTM: a faithful port puts (γ, β) on all four entry points: 4 × 2 × 2048 ≈ 16K parameters inferred, instead of the paper's 4K.
  • FLA: the analogue is LoRA on the Qwen3-VL ViT's MLPs (24 blocks, width 1024). Its parameter count depends on the ViT MLP width, which I did not read inferred. It needs gradients through the ViT, the mergers and 16 LLM layers.

9A.4 What R-037 and R-039 jointly predict

Two verified results verified:

  • R-037: a vision perturbation moves the backbone output strongly (S1: 1.37× nominal nearest-neighbour distance; 46.6% of forwards outside the nominal cloud). The 4-block vl_self_attention removes almost all of that displacement before the DiT (S2: 1.02×; 7.5%).
  • R-039: spliced at S2, the image tokens still carry most of the effect on the action.

Together they predict:

  • The adapter already normalises most of the pooled first- and second-moment shift. What still reaches the action is not a per-channel moment shift inferred (a prediction).
  • E1 (closed-form moment matching) therefore has little to correct at S2. At S1 or pre-LLM, it would correct roughly what the adapter already removes. Predict E1 weak on GR00T. Run it anyway: it is cheap and directly falsifies "it's a statistics shift".
  • E2 (γ, β learned by the flow loss) can move whatever directions the loss rewards, including the action-relevant residual at S2. E2 is the real test of the paper's idea on GR00T.

In π0.5 the expert reads modulated tokens at every layer. In GR00T, a modulation upstream of S2 acts only through what survives 16 LLM layers and the adapter inferred.

9A.5 The theory, restated for GR00T

The paper's Eq. 3, g(a; [A(fv(v)); ℓ]), assumes visual tokens reach the decoder unfused beside language. In GR00T the decoder sees a fused tensor:

a ~ gDiT( a; h( A(fv(v)), ℓ ), s ),    h = vl_self_attn ∘ vlln ∘ LLM16(9A.1)

If A sits upstream of S2, A1's constant becomes a product: dTV ≤ LDiT · Lh · ‖A(fv(v)) − z‖. At S2 it is LDiT alone.

10.2 Proposed experiments, cheapest first

E1 · Training-free moment matching on GR00T (no gradients)

Question: is GR00T's camera-shift damage a per-channel token-statistics shift that closed-form recalibration fixes?

Method:

  1. Cache tokens on nominal and camera-perturbed renders of the same LIBERO-Plus states, at two sites:
    • S1 (LLM layer-16 output, pre-vlln; already tapped);
    • with new hooks, the merger + 3 DeepStack outputs (pre-LLM, per camera).
    Not at S2: the shift there is 1.02× (§9A.2) verified.
  2. Compute per-channel μ, σ for each set and form γ, β by (3.2).
  3. Pre-LLM: apply them to the agent-view tokens only, using R-048's camera finding. At S1, apply them globally to the image positions, because the cameras are partly mixed there inferred.
  4. Re-run the camera-row rollouts.

Report: success rate before and after; εpair before and after; and the first-chunk action deviation, R-050-style.

Cost: inference only, so it fits on 8 GB.

Reading: a large recovery means the "one-shot demo" is unnecessary for camera shift. Little recovery means the drift is not a moment shift on GR00T, which is still informative and rules out the cheapest fix.

Per-camera boundary (resolved, §9A.2): clean only before the LLM, where ViT attention is per image verified; partial at S1; absent at S2 (R-045) verified.

Prior: R-037 + R-039 predict E1 is weak on GR00T. The adapter already removes most of the moment shift, while the action-relevant residual survives (§9A.4) inferred.

E2 · FTM on GR00T (4K-scale parameters, cached features)

Learn γ, β by the flow-matching loss on a handful of demos at a fixed camera variant, with everything else frozen. Two versions (§9A.6):

  • Cheap, local: γ, β on the image-token positions at S2 (or at S1 with the adapter frozen).
    • Backbone outputs are cached once. Training needs only forward and backward through the frozen DiT (plus the adapter for S1), which is cheaper than our R-056 runs.
    • It is global across both cameras, since S2 is mixed verified.
    • S2 is where R-039 shows the action-relevant signal lives verified.
  • Paper-faithful: γ, β on the merger and all three DeepStack outputs (≈16K parameters). The 16 LLM layers sit in the backward pass and nothing can be cached, so probably a rented GPU inferred.

Compare with E1. If FTM ≈ moment matching, the actions in the demo carry no extra information.

Demo source: minted or rescued demos at the perturbed camera, as in R-056's pipeline inferred, or a scripted replay of a nominal demo under the new camera. In simulation the trajectory is the same; only the render differs.

E3 · The 2×2 the paper didn't run

Same demos, same step budget, same camera variants. Four arms:

  1. vision-side only: E2's paper-faithful FTM on the merger + DeepStack, or LoRA on the top Qwen3-VL ViT blocks (rented GPU);
  2. physical-side only: a DiT-only LoRA. Our existing R-056 recipe also LoRAs vl_self_attention, so it mixes in the adapter zone (§9A.6);
  3. both;
  4. neither (zero-shot).

Keep the adapter frozen in all arms, or add it as a fifth arm. Evaluate on the camera row and on nominal, to measure forgetting, and at a held-out camera pose. This decides, on GR00T, whether camera brittleness is a Spatial fault or just cheapest to fix on the Spatial side.

Run it on two rows for a double dissociation inferred:

  • Camera: predict the vision-side arm wins (agent-view pixels carry 0.98, R-048).
  • Start pose: predict the physical-side arm wins (R-045: arm pose decodes from S2 image tokens to ~1.5–2 cm at every radius, so the gap is in the head's mapping).

That crossed outcome would localise faults by row, which the paper's single-row result cannot.

It also bears on the R-056 post-mortem. If action-head-only LoRA cannot fix a visual shift that a 4K-parameter token affine can, part of what our retrains have been asking the action head to do may belong upstream.

E4 · Wrist-camera dependence

For the camera and noise rows, LIBERO-Plus leaves the wrist camera clean. Re-run zero-shot, E1 and E2 with the wrist view replaced by a nominal-episode frame or by noise. This measures how much "robustness" is really the policy falling back on the unperturbed camera. That matters for any real deployment where both cameras drift.

E5 · Drift as a failure signal (links to mining)

R-036 tested whether VL embeddings separate GR00T's failures. It was underpowered on camera, with only n = 2 camera episodes. The per-channel statistics from E1 give a cheap, per-episode drift score ‖(σt/σs − 1, μt − μs)‖, computed from a few frames. Test whether it predicts which camera variants GR00T fails on before rolling out. If it does, it is a pre-rollout OOD triage signal for the miner, and it costs one forward pass.

10.3 What would change our minds

  • E1 recovers ≳ half the camera gap: per-camera feature recalibration becomes a deployment-time knob and should precede any camera retrain.
  • E1 fails but E2 succeeds: the demo's supervision matters. The cheap path is FTM with cached features, not action-head LoRA.
  • E3's physical-only arm ≈ vision-only arm: the paper's localisation claim does not transfer to GR00T, and "where to adapt" is a cost question, not a diagnosis.
  • E4 shows large wrist dependence: the camera-row numbers (ours and the paper's) overstate single-view robustness.
