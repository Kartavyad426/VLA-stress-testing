Verdict: is IDM + VLA "too optimistic"?
  • To raise GR00T's success on our LIBERO tasks soon: yes, mostly too optimistic. In sim, actions are free and exact. An IDM only adds labels to video that has none. Every published performance gain needs one of two things:
    • a video generator that creates new situations, at very large compute: GR00T N1 used about 105k L40 GPU-h for its neural trajectories, and DreamGen RoboCasa about 54 h × 1,500 L40s V;
    • a low-data regime: +4.2 / +8.8 / +6.8 pts on RoboCasa at 30 / 100 / 300 demos V.
    Our start-pose failures are decided at forwards 0–1 (R-044, R-057). R-056 showed that coverage matters, not volume. IDM labels add coverage only if the video adds new situations, and in sim rescue minting or a scripted expert already does that more cheaply and exactly I.
  • As instruments for the mining spec: realistic and cheap. A small IDM trained on our LIBERO data fits the 8 GB card I. It can serve four uses:
    • an E3 without proprioception ("E3b"): commanded vs achieved motion where no robot state is available;
    • gripper events, and so phases, on video-only data (§6c tier 3);
    • a label-quality check: do the labels, replayed, reproduce the video?
    • with latent-action models, an unsupervised segmenter (E6's next step).
  • Policies that predict the future and then apply an IDM (Seer, UWM, DreamZero) would replace GR00T, not add to it. On LIBERO they don't beat current VLAs: Seer scores 87.7% on LIBERO-Long, against UniVLA's 92.0% on Long and 95.2% averaged over the four suites V. There's no reason to switch.
  • Expectation to set: an IDM given the true future frames, run open-loop, reproduced only 55–65% of LIBERO-10 training demos in UWM's test (the policy managed 26–47%) V. Replayed IDM labels will be imperfect even on clean data.
  • VLA vs IDM chunks (c9k, §8): on our own rollouts the difference is commanded-vs-achieved motion plus IDM error, so the IDM adds nothing there. On successful demos it is imitation disagreement, which needs no IDM where actions are recorded. Its clearest direct use is label quality. As a failure predictor it is weak in aggregate and untested per situation (test K1, no IDM, ~1–2 GPU-h).
  • Next step: a gate package of three parts. T0: does replaying the human demos' own actions reproduce them? T1: train a small LIBERO IDM and check that its labels replay. Then E3b on r047. About 3–4 engineer-days, ≤ 6 GPU-h. Nothing else involving an IDM should run before T1 passes (§4, §6).

How this route fails.

  • IDM error compounds when labels are replayed open-loop.
  • Labels are in the IDM's training embodiment, so other robots or human hands need retargeting.
  • Generated video can break physics. Dream.exe (2606.04811, 101 tasks) reports that "visual quality proves a poor predictor of executability" I.
DreamGen's own filter is a VLM judge, not a sim replay V. Replay in sim is the stronger filter, and we have one.

(d) Other uses

  • RL fine-tuning inside a world model. VLA-RFT (2510.00406) V:
    • Setup: a 138M LLaMA-style autoregressive world model on VQGAN tokens, pretrained on LIBERO for 150k steps.
    • Reward: −(L1 + LPIPS) against the expert trajectory, with both rendered through the same world model.
    • Result on a VLA-Adapter base (0.5B): 400 RL steps on 4 × A800 took LIBERO-Spatial 88.4 → 94.4 and the average 86.6 → 91.1. Under ±5 cm object shifts, +4.5.
    • Code: OpenHelix-Team/VLA-RFT.
    HaWMPO (2609.09941) adds hallucination-aware rollouts on LIBERO Object, Spatial and Goal I. RL on a 3B GR00T doesn't fit 8 GB.
  • IDM as a label-quality check. For each demo, compare its recorded actions with what an IDM infers from its frames. A large gap marks a suspect label: a teleop glitch, a minted label hitting the clamp, a frame/action misalignment. Our demos are sim-labelled, so this is mostly for external or minted data I.
  • Latent actions or prediction error as an unsupervised segmenter.
    • SBD (2503.10684, ICCV 2025) detects skill boundaries from spikes in a pretrained action predictor's error I.
    • HiLAM (2601.23156) learns hierarchical latent skills from action-less video by dynamic chunking I.
    Consequence for E1 and E6: the spike SBD uses as a boundary signal is the same "normal replanning at phase transitions" that ROOT_SIGNAL §2 lists as a false alarm for root detection. A root detector built from prediction errors has to subtract the phase-boundary spikes, or calibrate on successes as Sentinel does.

3b · What must be settled before any IDM experiment

  • Action convention. The IDM must output LIBERO's 7-D action (dx, dy, dz, droll, dpitch, dyaw, gripper) in dataset units, the same as runs/r047 action_dims and the cached LeRobot dataset V. Predict the gripper as a binary class, not a regression value I.
  • The clamp. The checkpoint's preprocessor has use_percentiles: true and clip_outliers: true V. In training, actions are min-max normalised to [−1, 1] and then clamped (processor_groot.py ~l.1763). The bounds are q01/q99 for absolute action groups and min/max for relative ones (l.2235) V. Whether the libero_sim groups are relative is unverified; modality_config is empty in the JSON. Consequences:
    • IDM labels beyond the bounds saturate silently, so large IDM errors are hidden in training, not flagged.
    • Genuinely large corrective actions (start-pose rescue) clamp in the same way as minted labels.
    Report the fraction of IDM labels that clamp, next to that fraction for the real labels.
  • The gap k. k = 1 (per step) suits E3b's per-step residual. k = 16 with a 16-step chunk is the GR00T-Dreams default (action_horizon: 16) V, equals GR00T's executed H, and suits c9k. Train both; it's cheap I.
  • Resolution and frames. GR00T sees 256 px. The cached LeRobot dataset is 256 px at 20 fps (432 episodes, 52,970 frames, AV1) V. data/libero_raw is 128 px V, so for 256 px it needs re-rendering from its stored states. runs/r047 saved no frames (video_dir=None, no mp4) V, so E3b needs a render-only replay of the stored actions first. The PoC showed replay reproduces rollouts (4/4, ≤ 2.6 mm drift).
  • D10. Labels from an IDM for another embodiment are a coverage-gap source, not failure-mode data.
  • GPU and joins. Every IDM training run is a gated GPU step (prim → my prim → impl), one model on the card at a time. Never join by rollout_id; use the row index or (scene, radius, direction, noise seed).

8 · VLA vs IDM chunks on the same observations (c9k)

Added 2026-10-09 on the user's request (c9k), as a section of this doc rather than a separate one. The question: GR00T predicts a chunk from ot alone, sees no future, and executes only H = 16 of its C = 40 steps. An IDM sees (ot, ot+k). Is the difference between their chunks useful, and for what?

Bottom line
  • On our own sim rollouts, VLA − IDM ≈ the execution gap (signal c) + IDM error. Proprioception gives (c) exactly, so here the IDM adds only error. Its one use on our rollouts is to validate the IDM (E3b).
  • On successful episodes, it is the imitation disagreement (signal d).
    • Where the actions are recorded (data/libero_raw, the IPEC LeRobot set), no IDM is needed.
    • An IDM version is useful only for successful video-only data, and only if the IDM's error is well below the disagreement being measured. T1 supplies that error budget.
  • The clearest direct use is label quality, not failure detection: IDM vs recorded labels on minted, external or teleop demos.
  • As a predictor of failure or a source of mining targets, open-loop disagreement is weak in aggregate. Validation loss and MSE track checkpoint success poorly (robomimic, data-scaling laws, AutoEval), and policies that deviate from demos often recover (UWM). Per situation it is untested, and that is the cheap test below (K1).
  • How to measure it: as the policy's flow-matching loss of the demo chunk over several fixed (t, ε) draws, which is D20's loss path. Not as an L2 distance to one sampled chunk, which penalises valid alternative ways of doing the task and mixes in sampling noise.

8.1 · What the two chunks are, and what their difference contains

Notation:

  • âπt:t+40 ~ π(· | ot, st, ℓ): GR00T's chunk. Steps t … t+15 are executed after the postprocessor un-normalises and clips V.
  • âIt:t+k = I(ot, ot+k [, st]): the IDM's chunk.
  • aach: the "achieved" action, meaning the action that would explain the motion that actually happened (what a perfect IDM outputs).
  • aD: a demonstrator's recorded action.

Own rollout:   âπ − âI = (âπ − aach) + (aach − âI) = execution gap (c) + IDM error εI(8.1)
Someone else's success:   π(ot) − âI = (π(ot) − aD) + (aD − aach) + (aach − âI) = intention gap (d) + demonstrator's execution gap + εI(8.2)
  • Identifiability. In (8.1), (c) and εI cannot be separated without a second measurement of the motion. Proprioception is that measurement in sim, which is why E3b has to be validated in sim first. Off-sim, any VLA–IDM gap carries an unknown εI, bounded only by T1's held-out error on the same embodiment I.
  • In (8.2), the middle term is small in sim free space but not at contact (grasp, push), which is exactly where roots sit. Disagreement near contact therefore mixes the demonstrator's own slips with the policy's intent I.
  • The unexecuted tail (steps 17–40) has no IDM counterpart unless k = 40. Comparing it with what happened next gives no new signal:
    • against the next chunk's commanded head it is STAC (a);
    • against the achieved motion it is (a) + (c).
    The tail positions are also never supervised by our fine-tunes (FLOW_MATCHING §3.3) V.
  • Compare like with like. Use the post-clip executed action. Compare the gripper as a commanded flip against the realised finger closure, not as a regression value I.

8.4 · Evidence: how weak is open-loop disagreement as a proxy for closed-loop failure?

  • Aggregate, per checkpoint: weak.
    • Robomimic (2108.03298): picking checkpoints by validation loss gave policies 50–100% worse than the best checkpoint, as reported in secondary summaries I.
    • Data Scaling Laws in Imitation Learning (2410.18647): no correlation between MSE and success I.
    • AutoEval (2503.24278): weak correlation I.
  • Why: covariate shift. Disagreement measured on demo states says nothing about the states the policy actually reaches after small errors. Our start-pose failures are off-demo states, decided at forwards 0–1 (R-044, R-057). By construction, a demo-frame signal sees them only where demo and rollout situations overlap I.
  • Deviation is not failure. In UWM's LIBERO tracking test, policies deviate from the reference demos more than the IDM does (0.26–0.47 vs 0.55–0.65 tracking success) and still "eventually recover and solve the tasks" V.
  • Our own data V (FLOW_MATCHING §3.1, RESULTS R-056):
    • D20: the mean flow loss fell about 65% while the held-out sampled first chunk moved 9–12%.
    • R-056 fitted its training starts by 30% of the gap, held-out starts by 12%.
    • The base model's flow loss on IPEC demo frames is heavy-tailed: mean 0.051, median 0.0077.
    So disagreement on demos is concentrated in a few frames. That is good for ranking where to look, and says nothing yet about failure.
  • Multimodality and noise. A successful demo is one of many valid ways to do the task. An L2 distance to one sampled chunk penalises a valid alternative and mixes in flow-matching sampling noise (one draw per forward). Two remedies:
    • (i) the flow-matching loss of the demo chunk at fixed (t, ε) draws: D20's experiments/r056_diag_loss.py path, 8 draws per frame V, a likelihood proxy;
    • (ii) the distance to the nearest of B sampled chunks, or MMD as in STAC.
    The exact likelihood (FLOW_MATCHING §3.3, "untried") is the principled version.
  • Untested: per-situation prediction. Does high disagreement in a situation predict failure for rollouts that reach that situation? I found no paper that tests this. My prior is weak to moderate I.

Disagreement is informative when four conditions hold:

  1. the situation is in-domain (D10);
  2. εI ≪ the disagreement, if an IDM is involved;
  3. it is measured distributionally;
  4. it is used to rank where to look, not as a failure label.
