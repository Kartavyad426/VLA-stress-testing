Verdict
  • No public dataset labels the decision point. Every onset label found marks where the failure shows: "first clearly deviates", obvious_mistake, "earliest commitment action" V. The decision anchor (MINING_SPEC UQ1) can still be measured only by our own correct-at-forward-k interventions (R-044, R-057). External data can give ground truth for the symptom anchor, root-rule tests and a check on our VLM labeller.
  • Top pick: podolinsky's GR00T-N1.7 LIBERO rollouts. These are NVIDIA's own N1.7 LIBERO checkpoints, with 10 action chunks sampled at every inference. Every failure carries a Gemini onset frame and one of 10 modes V. The labels are VLM-made and unchecked. The data is gated and the card states no licence.
  • The ~30-episode hand-labelled set is still needed, but as an audit. Labelling 30 podolinsky onsets by hand under FailTime's onset rules tests their VLM labels and our vlm_label.py in one pass. No external set covers our libero_spatial checkpoint under our perturbations, so it cannot replace labels on our own rollouts I.

2 · Top picks

1 · podolinsky GR00T-N1.7 LIBERO v3

This is the only source that pairs our policy family with per-failure onset frames and the per-inference action samples (K=10) our dispersion signals need, so E5's timing questions can run without any GPU V. The weakness is the labels: one VLM made them, with no human audit stated, and the onset mixes symptom types (obvious_mistake 1,088 vs operator_intervention 167 in LIBERO-X; the card does not say what the second means here I). Plan: request access, hand-audit 30 failures under FailTime's rules, then use the dataset only where the audit agrees. The audit costs about as much as the planned hand-labelled set but checks both their labels and our labeller. The licence is unstated, so treat it as research-only until the author confirms I.

2 · FailTime

This is the only open, human-made onset ground truth with measured agreement (50 ms), and it gives a per-category onset rule we can copy instead of inventing our own V. It is another embodiment, so it says nothing about our policy's failures. Its role is to calibrate: score vlm_label.py (NIM VLMs) on onset accuracy@0.5 s against these human labels. ProTracer's own best is 71% Short and 40% Long V, which sets the bar. Caveat: most of the Short split comes from ViFailback, where 4,995 of 5,202 trajectories are teleoperated, deliberately staged failures V. So prefer the Long split (real policy-length episodes) for the headline number I.

3 · LIBERO-Recover

On paper this is the ideal fixture: our simulator, natural failures including GR00T's, VLM labels checked by humans, and recovery demos V. But the labels are not released, and nobody has answered the request for them V. It is worth a check each week. If the labels arrive, it outranks rank 1 because humans verified them.

3 · Checked and not picked

  • ViFailback (2512.02787): real ALOHA, keyframe and correction labels, but 4,995 of 5,202 trajectories are teleoperated, deliberately staged failures V. Reach it through FailTime instead.
  • RoboMIND failure_data/: mostly rejected teleop demos, episode-level (DATA_AND_BENCHMARKS §6.3) V.
  • REFLECT / RoboFail (2306.15724): small; AI2-THOR plus a UR5. The details were not on the abstract page I.
  • Sentinel / FAIL-Detect, AutoEval logs: episode-level success only, as far as known; not re-checked I.
  • FailureSpot (2609.04277): frame-labelled LIBERO-10 rollouts (π0, π0-FAST, OpenVLA) under a "first clearly deviates" rule, but only about 27 trajectories, with no release stated V.
  • Early-warning OpenVLA (2606.29699): heuristic onsets (wrong-object grasp, drop at >0.06 fall, stall at <0.015 per 25 steps) that the authors call "unaudited". The code is CC-BY-4.0; the rollouts are not released V. Its thresholds are a useful cross-check for our rules.
  • LabRobFail (2607.23704): injected faults with temporal labels, but a chemistry-lab domain and still under review V.
  • robotwin-failure-recovery: has fault_detected_frame, but it is an unlicensed backup (DATA_AND_BENCHMARKS §6.2) V.
  • REPAIR-Bench (2606.29937): a human–robot interaction study, not manipulation failures V.
