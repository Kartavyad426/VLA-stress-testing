Verdict
  • The quick win is to keep LIBERO's tasks and change what happens while they run. Our four GR00T N1.7 LIBERO checkpoints, our harness and our MuJoCo stack all carry over unchanged. Two 2026 benchmarks do exactly this, and both are new to our docs:
    • LIBERO-MAX (2609.36518): 8,000 paired cases. In each, the same rollout prefix is replayed and then one event fires mid-task: the target or the receptacle moves, distractors appear, or an obstacle is inserted. It already reports GR00T N1.7: 69.3% Base vs 50.0% Dynamic V. That gives us a published number to reproduce, which no other new-failure-mode benchmark offers for our policy.
    • LIBERO-Recover (2609.05178): it starts the robot from states saved at real failures of six policies, GR00T among them (a grasp that knocked the bowl over, a blocked drawer), and asks for recovery. Scene files are on ModelScope under Apache-2.0, and the full release is promised for 20 Oct V.
  • Cheap add-ons on the same footing: LIBERO-Para (paraphrased instructions; failures are wrong plans from the first step) and LIBERO-Occ (static occluders). Both reuse LIBERO tasks, so no retraining is needed I.
  • Anything with new tasks needs a fine-tune first, whatever its simulator: LIBERO-Safety, VLA-Arena, LIBERO-X, RoboCerebra, MimicGen, RoboCasa365. On 8 GB that is the expensive part, as the R-053..R-058 pilot showed. These are the second wave.
  • The qualitatively better option is SimplerEnv with NVIDIA's own GR00T N1.7 SimplerEnv checkpoints.
    • Published per-task numbers, including natural failures such as put eggplant in sink at 2/100 and place in closed drawer at 7/100 V.
    • A different robot, and sim results that are known to correlate with real ones.
    • The cost is a new SAPIEN adapter and new privileged-state work.
  • Ranked shortlist (§8):
    1. LIBERO-MAX Lite, in-scope events;
    2. LIBERO-Recover;
    3. LIBERO-Para plus LIBERO-Occ;
    4. SimplerEnv with GR00T N1.7;
    5. a fine-tune-gated wave led by RoboCasa365 (articulated objects and appliances) or LIBERO-Safety.

0 · Why move beyond LIBERO

docs/FAILURE_MINING_RELEVANCE.html makes the case. Here is what it gives this doc:

  • The unit a client cares about is (policy, task, condition) inside their deployment. That is the operational design domain (ODD), weighted by how often each condition occurs. LIBERO fixes the task set at 40 tabletop pick-and-place variants. So every failure boundary we have measured is a boundary for those tasks only. FAILURE_MINING_RELEVANCE's "which unit is right" table spells out what each unit hides.
  • LIBERO success does not carry over to other task sets. ROBOGATE: a GR00T N1.6 LIBERO-Spatial fine-tune scores 97.65% on LIBERO and 0% on 68 industrial Isaac scenes V (2603.22126). SIM_ALTERNATIVES §2f's caveat stands: that is a scene gap plus a simulator gap. FATE-VLA: failure-aware search takes GR00T N1.6 from 64.4% to 34.7% V (2606.02307).
  • LIBERO is structurally thin for several failure families.
    • One instruction per task, so language failures are degenerate.
    • Grasp points cluster within 1.1–2.6 cm per task (DATA_AND_BENCHMARKS §1.2) V.
    • No grasp predicate in BDDL.
    • Every episode starts from a clean reset, so recovery is never exercised (LIBERO-Recover's motivation).
  • Our own failures so far are all reset-time perturbations: start pose, camera and lighting (R-039..R-058). This doc looks for failure modes that are not perturbations: mid-task change, recovery from a bad state, occlusion, language, safety, long horizon, articulated objects, contact, memory and bimanual work.

2 · Tier A: same tasks, new failure condition (no retraining)

These keep LIBERO's 40 tasks and BDDL predicates, so the four nvidia/gr00t17-lerobot-libero_*-640 checkpoints apply as they are. MINERVA applies too. The work is in the env wrapper, not in a new policy.

A1 · LIBERO-MAX: a mid-task event, paired with a no-event twin
  • What it is.
    • 8,000 Base/Dynamic pairs, plus an 800-pair Lite subset that tracks Max within 2.4 pts and keeps 88 of 91 pairwise orderings V.
    • 8 events, 1,000 pairs each: target relocation, receptacle relocation, distractor burst, obstacle insertion (in scope), plus camera shift, sensor-noise onset, illumination switch and visual-theme switch (out of scope here, but they come free).
    • Source cases: 5,600 from LIBERO-Plus and 2,400 from LIBERO-PRO, over all four suites V.
  • Why it fits us better than anything else found. The protocol is our paired counterfactual:
    • Same initial state, seed and executed action prefix, then one frozen event. Adapters must verify that the prefix replays exactly V.
    • Outcomes go into four cells, (1,1) (0,1) (1,0) (0,0), plus "conditional regression", i.e. McNemar-ready.
    • It pins the same LIBERO-Plus SHA we use (4976dc3) and LIBERO-PRO at 2b910b5, with robosuite 1.4.0 and MuJoCo 3.2.6 V.
  • GR00T N1.7 is one of its 14 policies: 69.3% Base, 50.0% Dynamic V. Other policies range from 11.0 to 25.7 pt drops. This is a reproduction gate for our policy on a new failure mode, which nothing else here offers.
  • Caveats.
    • Base cases are already LIBERO-Plus/PRO-perturbed, so Base is not nominal LIBERO.
    • The repo has no licence file V; check before anything client-facing.
    • It ships adapters for X-VLA, openpi, OpenVLA-OFT, VLA-JEPA, Cosmos, FastWAM and LingBot, but no GR00T runner script V. Which GR00T checkpoint and serving cadence they used must be read from their result artefacts first I.
    • MuJoCo 3.2.6 against our 3.3.2. Both are below the 3.4.0 break, but renders differ around the 3.3.3 change (DATA_AND_BENCHMARKS §8) I.
  • Harness work I:
    • a step-time event hook in libero_env.py, since today's perturbations apply at reset;
    • a trigger on end-effector-to-entity distance (we already log _gt_eef_to_object);
    • prefix replay, which our nominal pairing already approximates.
    Our localisation methods transfer naturally: "decided-at-forward-k" becomes "first post-event forward".
A2 · LIBERO-Recover: start from a real failure, measure recovery
  • What it is.
    • Failure states were extracted from simulator checkpoints of six policies (π0, π0.5, OpenVLA, GR00T, Wan-Policy, Cosmos-Policy). Qwen3.5-27B localised the failures in time; humans then teleoperated recovery demos V.
    • Four levels: retry → adapt → restore the object's state → clear the environment. Five failure categories (§1).
    • The paper counts 2,178 scenarios over 130 subtasks. The README's released scene library holds 880: spatial 168, goal 91, object 77, libero_10 207, libero_90 337 V. The two counts differ; the full release is due 2026-10-20.
  • Data. ataier/LIBERO_Recovery_Assets (scenes as .bddl + _sim_state.npy), LIBERO_Recovery_Expert (recovery demos, LeRobot format) and LIBERO_10_LL (episodes cut just before failure), all Apache-2.0 on ModelScope V. The GitHub repo has no licence file.
  • Why it matters. These are natural policy failures on our stack, labelled by category: the "75% of real failures are natural" point from FailBench, on our simulator. The LIBERO_10_LL pre-failure data is the closest public analogue to our failure-onset question (DATA_AND_BENCHMARKS §6.2).
  • Caveats.
    • Half the default trials start with the gripper closed. That is a start-state perturbation, so report the open split for this doc's scope I.
    • The client env pins MuJoCo 3.2.3. Whether a flattened sim_state saved there loads identically on 3.3.2 is I untested; check it with a zero-action settle.
    • Published policy numbers are for GR00T N1.5, not N1.7.
  • Harness work I: set the init state from .npy (our env already restores init states) and parse the instruction from the BDDL. libero_90 scenes need a libero_90 policy, which we don't have.
A3 · LIBERO-Para: paraphrase robustness · A4 · LIBERO-Occ: occlusion
  • LIBERO-Para (2603.28301, MIT, code public).
    • It varies action wording and object references independently, on LIBERO-Goal tasks.
    • Drops of 22–52 pt across seven configurations, driven by object synonyms ("the range" for "the stove").
    • Models were trained on LIBERO-Goal demos, so our libero_goal-640 checkpoint is the matching case I. GR00T is not in their table V.
    • Harness: an instruction swap only. R-038 and R-049 already do input-level language swaps. The cheapest item in this doc.
  • LIBERO-Occ (2606.10862, MIT).
    • Occluded BDDL and init files for all four suites. Occluders are static objects added at generation, and executability was checked by replaying the demos V.
    • Three types (target, receptacle, both) and three severities.
    • Baselines were "fine-tuned on the same demonstration data". I read that as standard LIBERO demos, so our checkpoints apply I.
    • No GR00T row. Clean failure mode: the evidence is simply not in the image, which our wrist-vs-agent split (R-042) can localise.
  • LIBERO-PRO (2510.03827, MIT). Object swaps and task-instruction changes within LIBERO scenes; models above 90% collapse to 0% V. It is half of LIBERO-MAX's source cases, so running A1 exercises it. Its own initial-state and environment axes are perturbations and out of scope.

3 · Tier A′: same simulator, new tasks (fine-tune first)

These run on MuJoCo/robosuite, so the privileged-state and _check_grasp work transfers. But the tasks are new, so no existing GR00T LIBERO checkpoint is meaningful on them. Each needs a GR00T LoRA fine-tune on their data, and our one 8 GB card and the SFT pilot's cost (hours per run) are the binding constraint.

4 · Tier B: qualitatively better, new stack

B1 · SimplerEnv with NVIDIA's GR00T N1.7 checkpoints: the best new-stack option
  • Checkpoints exist with published per-task numbers V (Isaac-GR00T examples/SimplerEnv).
    • nvidia/GR00T-N1.7-SimplerEnv-Bridge (WidowX), average 62.3%.
    • …-Fractal (Google Robot), average 72.5%.
    • Each ships processor_config.json plus 6.44 GiB of safetensors, the same size as our LIBERO checkpoint, so the 8 GB fit should match R-021 I.
  • Natural failure modes, no perturbation needed: eggplant in sink 2/100, place in closed drawer 7/100, stack cube 48/100, carrot on plate 58/100, against open drawer 100/100 V. Those are contact, precision and articulated failures on a real-robot task set.
  • Why it is qualitatively better.
    • A second embodiment.
    • Tasks drawn from real datasets (Bridge, Fractal).
    • The sim-real correlation is published (2405.05941), so a failure found here is more likely to mean something on hardware.
  • Loading path. Our LeRobot's GR00T processor maps simpler_env_google: 0 and simpler_env_widowx: 1. Its docs say N1.7 "provides checkpoints for SimplerEnv, DROID, and LIBERO". So these may load through our existing groot policy type I, untested. If not, use the Isaac-GR00T native server in its own venv (R7).
  • Cost. No LeRobot env. SAPIEN/ManiSkill2 needs its own version audit and its own privileged-state extraction (DATA_AND_BENCHMARKS §3.1, §8). Forks are fragmented, so use the one Isaac-GR00T's setup script pins I.
