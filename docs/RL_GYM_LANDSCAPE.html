RL gyms and closed-loop improvement for robot VLA policies

Survey + positioning · written 2026-09-24 · companion to docs/LANDSCAPE.md (238-source prior-art survey) and docs/WHAT_OUR_CODE_DOES.md

Three findings up front.
  1. "RL gym" names three different products. An environment suite (LIBERO, RoboCasa, Isaac Lab-Arena), an RL training substrate (RLinf-VLA, SimpleVLA-RL), and a commercial environments-as-a-service market (Surge, Mercor, Prime Intellect). Only the first two currently exist for robots.
  2. The commercial RL-environment market is almost entirely non-robotic. A 2026 directory of 38 RL-environment vendors contains zero robotics or VLA specialists — it is coding agents, browser use and enterprise SaaS. Robotics has its own, separate supplier set (NVIDIA, Lightwheel, the data foundries).
  3. Nobody ships the complete autonomous closed loop. The closest published attempt (AgenticRobotics, Aug 2026) ran it unattended on LIBERO and reports a negative headline: taking the final checkpoint beat every measurement-driven selector it tried, including its own. Every deployed system breaks the loop at the same seam — diagnosis, and deciding what data to collect. That seam is exactly where our harness lives.

2.2 RL training substrates (sense B)

  • RLinf / RLinf-VLA — "RL infrastructure for embodied and agentic AI". Unified RL training of VLAs across architectures, algorithms and simulators. The nearest thing to a general-purpose robot RL gym stack.
  • SimpleVLA-RL (ICLR 2026) — veRL-based; VLA-specific trajectory sampling, multi-env rendering. Took OpenVLA-OFT to 97.6 on LIBERO-Long; from 17.3 → 91.7 with one cold-start trajectory per task.
  • RIPT-VLA, RL-VLA3, on-the-fly test-time RL — variants on the same idea: replace more demonstrations with interaction.

These matter to us for one reason: they all need a reward/verifier, and today that reward is almost always the binary task-success bit. See §6.

3. Enterprise options

3.1 The "RL environments" market — and why robots aren't in it

2025–26 saw a real market form around RL environments for agents. Structure, as of 2026:

  • Human-data incumbents that added environments: Scale AI, Surge AI (EnterpriseBench / CoreCraft), Mercor (acquired Sepal AI, Feb 2026), Turing, Centific. Scale, Surge, Mercor and Handshake reportedly split >75% of labelling revenue.
  • Environment-native startups: Mechanize (code, "replication training"), Fleet AI, HUD, Veris AI, Plato, Bespoke Labs — mostly simulating proprietary SaaS (Salesforce, Slack, ServiceNow) with SOC-2 and turnkey env+data+eval bundles.
  • Open ecosystems: Prime Intellect's Environments Hub (2,500+ community environments, bring-your-own, distributed compute), General Reasoning, Chakra Labs.
The load-bearing observation: a 2026 directory of 38 RL-environment vendors — 25 commercial, 3 open-source, 4 incumbent, 6 infrastructure — lists zero robotics/VLA specialists. Focus areas are coding agents (21), computer-use (16), enterprise workflows (17). This is a clean market-segmentation boundary, not an oversight: physical-world environments need physics, assets and embodiments, which is a different supply chain.

4. Is anyone running the complete autonomous closed loop?

Decompose the loop into six stages and ask who automates each:

#StageState of the artAutomated end-to-end?
1Run the policy, get a scoreSolved many times over: Isaac Lab-Arena (thousands of envs in parallel), RoboFinals, lerobot-eval, AutoEval on real hardware.Yes
2Find failures / stress itPerturbation benchmarks (LIBERO-Plus, COLOSSEUM, VLATest); adaptive search (ROBOGATE, two-stage boundary-focused sampling, emits a "failure dictionary", far fewer evaluations than uniform sweeps).Yes
3Diagnose why it failedRoboFAC, AHA, Sentinel, FIPER, verifier surveys ("No Free Checker"). Labels are largely unvalidated; robotics-specialised VLMs underperform their base models on failure benchmarks (FailBench).No — this is the seam
4Decide what data would fix itDemonstration scoring exists (CUPID, Demo-SCORE — rank demos by closed-loop impact). A costed, evidence-carrying specification of missing data does not.No — this is the seam
5Mine / generate that dataMimicGen-family, GR00T-Mimic/Dreams, world-model rollouts (World-VLA-Loop, RISE), teleop foundries.Partly — generation is cheap, targeting is not
6Retrain and re-measure on a frozen setRL substrates (§2.2) and plain SFT. LIBERO-Plus §6.2 did it once by hand: camera-viewpoint robustness 55.6% → 92.8% with 20k remediation trajectories.Mechanically yes; as a governed, non-regressing gate, rarely

The two data points that should calibrate expectations

AgenticRobotics (arXiv:2608.07555, Aug 2026) — the most complete public attempt. It ports the coding-agent architecture (main agent + subagents) to policy improvement: an LLM controller driving disposable workers through durable Bind→Analyze→Act→Measure→Score→Commit transactions, with an immutable objective, controller-owned measurement, commit-keyed crash recovery, an evidence-graded skill library, and a tool registry where every tool's measured quality is recorded and expired when the artifact behind it changes. Backends (policy, trainer, evaluator, recorder, dataset editor, calibrator) are all swappable. Run on LIBERO-10 against π₀.₅, SmolVLA, MolmoAct2, GR00T N1.7, X-VLA.

Its own headline is negative: "on the measured lineage, taking the final checkpoint beat every measurement-driven selector, ours included." Campaign 2 targeted ≥70% on ten tasks and stopped at 56.5% after 26 rounds. What it did demonstrate is the infrastructure discipline: zero lost or duplicated effects under kill injection, 6/6 tampering classes detected, false-promotion rate cut from 0.005–0.021 to 0.001.

Read that as: the engineering of an autonomous loop is tractable; the decision quality inside it is not yet better than a human's crude default.

The Pebblous negative result (Jul 2026, vendor blog, unverified) — 93 demonstrations targeted at known weaknesses dropped closed-loop success from 73% → 43%, with no offline signal predicting it. Targeted data is not automatically good data. Any loop that does not re-measure closed-loop on a frozen regression set, including non-targeted cells, can make the policy worse and report success.

Conclusion. Stages 1, 2 and 6 are commodity. Stages 3 and 4 are unsolved, and stage 5 is only as good as stage 4. So no — there is no autonomous, closed, VLA-to-data-to-retraining loop in production, open or commercial. What exists is a stack of well-built components with a human judgement call wedged between evaluation and data collection.

The shape of the difference: a gym answers how well and which policy is better. Our harness answers where, why, through which pathway, at what cost, and what data would fix it. RoboArena is the clean precedent for that framing — it answers "which policy is better", we answer "where does this one break". Those are complementary, not competing.

5.2 Where we are genuinely ahead

Three things survived the 238-source survey, and the gym landscape does not threaten any of them:

  1. The Data Gap Manifest as a costed artifact — severity, fixability, discriminators, validation status per row. The ideas are published; the artifact is not.
  2. Counterfactual attribution of policy failure to environment factors — formalised for LLM agents, essentially unexploited in robot policy evaluation. And it is not retrofittable onto a gym's output.
  3. Failure-cost weighting — no perturbation benchmark found weights failures by consequence.

5.3 Where we are behind, honestly

  • Scale. Arena evaluates across thousands of envs; we queue on one shared card. Any claim that needs n in the thousands is not ours to make today.
  • Validation. Failure-family labels are unvalidated (adjudication sheet 0 of 80); VLM labels named the wrong grasped object on their first real test. A gym that ships a wrong success predicate is embarrassing; a diagnostic layer that ships wrong diagnoses is worthless.
  • The loop is not closed even once. No frozen regression set, no remediation arm run. Until that lands we are a measurement instrument, not a loop.
  • Breadth. One benchmark, one embodiment, two policies (GR00T N1.7, MINERVA).

6. Could we extend into a gym? Three options, ranked

Direct answer: we should not try to become sense-A. Competing with Isaac Lab-Arena and RoboFinals on environment authoring, assets and throughput is a losing fight against NVIDIA's supply chain, and it would trade away the only three things that are ours. But "gym" has two other meanings, and one of them is a strong fit.

Option A — Ship as an EnvHub environment cheapest

Package the perturbation + privileged-trace wrapper as a LeRobot EnvHub entry, so make_env("<org>/vla_stress:envs/libero_plus_perturbed.py") yields a Gymnasium env that emits our Rollout contract instead of a success bit. Distribution, not new capability.

  • For: EnvHub is the real distribution channel now; Isaac Lab-Arena and LeIsaac both route through it. Low effort — our Env protocol is already reset(seed, spec) / step(action).
  • Against: gives away the wrapper without the mining layer, which is where the value is. Adds no science.
  • Blockers: none technical. Licence check on LIBERO-Plus redistribution.

Option B — Become the verifier/reward layer for an RL gym highest value

Every RL substrate in §2.2 — RLinf-VLA, SimpleVLA-RL, RIPT-VLA — trains against a binary task-success bit. That is the weakest possible reward, and it is exactly the signal our harness replaces. We already compute, per step and per episode: phase segmentation, divergence from a nominal reference, failure family, and failure cost (benign / disruptive / safety). Expose those as a verifier and you get:

  • Dense, phased reward instead of a terminal bit — reward shaped by where the rollout broke, not just that it did.
  • Cost-weighted reward — a safety failure and a benign timeout stop being worth the same. No published VLA-RL work does this.
  • A verifier that survives audit — deterministic rules first, LLM adjudication only for the residue, never overriding a deterministic verdict. The "No Free Checker" survey is the argument for why this is scarce.
  • For: uses the two-fifths of the code that is the contribution; the RL gyms are complements, not competitors; it is a publishable claim (cost-weighted, phase-aware verifier beats binary success at equal interaction budget) that sits alongside the existing open claim (targeted collection beats broad collection at equal budget).
  • Against: needs a second GPU at minimum — RL fine-tuning on one shared 8 GB card is not viable.
  • Blockers, in order: (1) family labels must be validated — an unvalidated taxonomy used as a reward function trains the policy on our mistakes; (2) the 0-of-80 adjudication sheet; (3) compute (PENDING #25).

Option C — Curriculum gym: turn the manifest into scenario generation the natural extension

This is the sense-A-adjacent version that does play to our strengths. find_boundary() plus the (still unbuilt) adaptive_sweep() already locate the edge of competence per factor; ROBOGATE independently validated that boundary-focused sampling finds failures far cheaper than uniform sweeps. Extend that from measurement to emission: the manifest row becomes a machine-readable scenario spec, and scene_descriptor (already in physical units, already bench-rebuildable) becomes the collection instruction — in sim, or handed to a teleop foundry.

  • For: it closes stages 4→5, the exact seam nobody has closed. It is the only option that makes the loop autonomous rather than better-instrumented. It also makes the manifest executable instead of a report.
  • Against: the Pebblous result is a direct warning — targeted data made a policy worse, 73%→43%, with no offline signal. Emitting a curriculum without a frozen regression set and non-targeted-cell regression reporting is how you ship a loop that degrades policies confidently.
  • Blockers: (1) RegressionSet must be frozen before any remediation data — it is built but unfrozen; (2) adaptive_sweep() and the coverage_required validator are the two remaining OPEN items in ARCHITECTURE §0; (3) fixability, discriminators, failure_cost must actually be emitted.

Recommended sequencing

  1. Do not start any of them until the family labels are validated. Everything downstream — reward, curriculum, manifest row — inherits that error.
  2. Then C, because it needs nothing we don't have on one GPU and it closes the seam that defines the project.
  3. Then B, when a rented GPU exists. It is the larger claim and the better story, and it converts the mining layer from a report generator into an RL component.
  4. A whenever, as distribution — but after B or C exists, so what ships is the diagnostic layer and not just a perturbation wrapper.

7. What would invalidate this reading

  • Isaac Lab-Arena shipping per-step privileged state and paired counterfactual probes in a later alpha. It is at v0.3 and explicitly moving toward "agent-ready and agent-injected workflows" — that is the direction of travel, and it is NVIDIA's to take.
  • Lightwheel's "bottleneck diagnosis" turning out to mean per-factor attribution rather than per-task scores. Worth a direct look at RoboFinals output format.
  • A follow-up to AgenticRobotics reporting a measurement-driven selector that does beat last-checkpoint. That would mean the loop's decision layer is solved and the remaining gap is only diagnosis quality.
