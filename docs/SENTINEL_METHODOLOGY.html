Part I · The claim

What Sentinel actually claims

One sentence: stop asking whether the state is unusual, and start asking whether the policy's own action distribution is contradicting itself over time — then hand everything that contradiction misses to a VLM watching the video.

The paper's organising move is a partition of failure, not a new score. It splits policy failure into two categories defined as complements of each other, and assigns a detector to each with deliberately mismatched latency budgets:

CategoryWhat it looks likeDetectorLatency need
Erratic failurePolicy jitters between action modes, thrashes, dives into unrecoverable statesSTAC — statistical temporal action consistencyFast. Must fire in seconds; the damage is physical.
Task-progression failurePolicy behaves smoothly and confidently, and is simply doing the wrong thing — stalls, drifts, places the object in the wrong spotVLM chain-of-thought video QASlow is fine. GPT-4o mean response 14.0 s. Nothing urgent is happening.

Because the second category is defined as "failures that are temporally consistent", the two detectors are near-disjoint by construction, and combining them with a logical OR is safe: each has a high true-negative rate, so the union's false-alarm cost stays bounded. That is the entire system-level argument, and it is why the paper reports +18% failures detected over either detector alone.

Part II · The math

Problem setup and notation

A policy π(a|s) acts in a finite-horizon MDP of length H. Rolling it out for t steps gives a trajectory τt = (s0, a0, …, st). The object being built is a detector

f(τt) → {ok, failure}

evaluated at every timestep, using only the past. A true positive is: the detector fires at any point in a trajectory that ends in failure, and the detection time is the earliest such firing. A true negative is: the detector never fires in a trajectory that succeeds. That asymmetry matters — TNR is an any-time property, and it is the reason the calibration argument in §5 has to be an any-time argument too.

Action chunking is the structure everything hangs off

The policy is a chunked generative policy: at inference step t it samples a sequence of h actions,

at := (at|t, at+1|t, …, at+h−1|t) ∼ π(·|st)

where at+i|t is the action predicted for time t+i at time t. It executes the first k < h of them, then re-plans at t+k. So two consecutive chunks make predictions about the same h−k future timesteps. That overlap is the entire signal.

Formally, write the two marginals over the overlapping slice:

π̄t := π(at+k:t+h−1|t | st)     π̃t+k := π(at+k:t+h−1|t+k | st+k)

Both live on ℝ(h−k)×|A|.

Why a distribution and not a vector

The obvious thing — compare the two sampled chunks directly — fails on multimodal policies. A diffusion policy may legitimately plan around an obstacle on the left at t and on the right at t+k; both are competent, and their L2 distance is enormous. The question is not "did the sample change" but "is the new distribution still covered by the old one". That is why the comparison is distributional, and it is the difference between STAC and the Temporal Non-Distributional Minimum baseline that scores 0.27 TNR out of distribution.

The calibration set

Sentinel assumes a post-training validation phase yielding Dτ = {τi}M of successful rollouts only. M = 50 in simulation, M = 10 on hardware. No failure data is ever required. This is the same reference-from-successes posture our ood_selfref already takes, and it is the single most portable idea in the paper.

STAC: the temporal-consistency detector

Define a per-step consistency score as a statistical distance between the two marginals, and accumulate it along the trajectory. At inference timestep t = jk:

Equation 1 — the STAC score
ηt := Σi=0j−1 D̂( π̄ik , π̃(i+1)k ) (1)

Raise a warning the first time ηt > γ.

Two properties are being bought here, and the paper states both:

  • Sensitivity to chronic, small deviations. A policy that is marginally more inconsistent than nominal at every step — jitter — never trips a per-step threshold but accumulates.
  • Sensitivity to frequency. A policy that is badly inconsistent more often than nominal also accumulates, even if each individual spike is within range.

The third property is not advertised in the body text but is the load-bearing one: ηt is monotone non-decreasing, because every summand is a distance and therefore ≥ 0. §5 shows that this single fact is what converts an ordinary split-conformal quantile into an any-time false-positive bound.

The hypothesis underneath

§A.1 states the mechanism the authors believe they are measuring. A predictive policy behaves as though it has an internal world model. At t it has a belief about what st+k will be. If that belief is right — which it should be in distribution — then the actions it would choose at st+k are already well represented in π̄t, and the distance is small. Out of distribution, the implicit world model is wrong, the states diverge from prediction, and π̃t+k lands somewhere π̄t gave little mass.

Read carefully, STAC is a one-step prediction-error probe on the policy's own latent dynamics model, measured in action space because that is the only place the world model is observable. This framing matters for transfer: it means STAC is not really an "action statistic", it is a self-consistency statistic, and self-consistency can in principle be measured at any tap where a model commits to something it can later be held to.

The statistical distances, as implemented

The true D(π̄t, π̃t+k) is not computable: it would require marginalising out both the non-overlapping actions and every intermediate denoising step. So D̂ is empirical — sample a batch of B chunks from each distribution (parallel on GPU) and compute a two-sample statistic.

MMD with an RBF kernel — the default

D̂(π̄t, π̃t+k) = 𝔼a,a′∼π̄[k(a,a′)] + 𝔼b,b′∼π̃[k(b,b′)] − 2·𝔼a∼π̄, b∼π̃[k(a,b)]

with k(x,y) = exp(−‖x−y‖² / β1), the kernel acting on whole flattened action sequences in ℝ(h−k)|A| — not on individual actions.

Bandwidth

Two regimes, chosen per domain:

  • Low multimodality (Close Box, Cover Object): β1 = 1/|A| fixed, B = 32.
  • High multimodality (PushT, Push Chair): the median heuristic, B = 256. In code this is gamma = 1/(2·median{‖zi−zj‖² : >0}) over the pooled sample z = [x; y] — i.e. the bandwidth is re-estimated at every timestep from the data being compared.
Implementation detail worth knowing

The docstring in compute_mmd_rbf says the kernel is exp(-gamma*||x-y||^2 / 2), but it calls sklearn.metrics.pairwise.rbf_kernel, which computes exp(-gamma*||x-y||^2). The factor of 2 is absorbed into the bandwidth, so results are unaffected — but anyone reimplementing from the docstring will get a silently different bandwidth. Take the kernel from the call, not the comment.

KL divergence via KDE — the alternatives

Fit Gaussian KDEs to both sample sets and take a plug-in estimate. Forward:

D̂ = 𝔼a∼π̃t+k[ log p(a) / q(a) ]     (p = KDE of π̃t+k, q = KDE of π̄t)

Reverse swaps which distribution supplies the samples. Bandwidth β2 is either 1.0 or √λmax(Cov(at+k:t+h−1|·)) for multimodal domains — i.e. scaled to the dominant principal axis of the overlapping actions.

Forward vs reverse is not cosmetic. Forward KL is mode-covering: it explodes when π̃t+k puts mass where π̄t put none — exactly "the policy has discovered an option it previously did not consider", which is the erratic signature. Reverse KL is mode-seeking and punishes collapse instead. Empirically in Table 1 forward KL takes a higher TPR and a lower TNR than reverse; that asymmetry is the expected one.

A gap in the guarantee

Proposition 2's proof asserts D̂(·,·) ≥ 0 "because it is a statistical distance". That holds for the MMD V-statistic by construction. It does not hold for a KDE plug-in KL estimate, which is a difference of two noisy log-density averages and can be negative in finite samples. In practice it essentially never is, but with STAC KL the any-time bound is empirical rather than proved. If you port one distance, port MMD.

Preprocessing the actions

Not incidental. Before any distance is computed (compute_temporal_error):

  1. Slice to the overlap. prev[:, k:h] against curr[:, :h−k]. An off-by-one here silently compares non-corresponding timesteps and the score still looks plausible.
  2. Drop the binary gripper channel. A discrete 0/1 dimension inside a continuous RBF kernel contributes a fixed quantum whenever it flips and nothing otherwise; it degrades the kernel. Optionally drop rotations too.
  3. Optionally integrate velocities into trajectories — (action·Δt).cumsum(axis=−2) — so the comparison happens in position space, where a persistent small velocity offset becomes a growing position gap. This is what the ATE error function operates on.

Calibration: why the cumulative sum is the whole trick

This section is the one piece of Sentinel that is genuinely a theorem rather than a heuristic, and it is the piece most worth stealing.

The goal is a threshold γ such that on an in-distribution success the detector never fires, with probability ≥ 1 − δ. The obstacle: split conformal prediction needs i.i.d. calibration and test points, and the per-timestep scores within one trajectory are violently autocorrelated. Applying a conformal quantile at each of H/k timesteps would need a union bound and cost a factor of H/k in δ.

Theorem 1 · split conformal (standard)

For i.i.d. calibration points X1…XM and test point Xtest, and any score function η, setting

γ := the ⌈(M+1)(1−δ)⌉ / M empirical quantile of {η(Xi)}

gives ℙ[η(Xtest) ≤ γ] ≥ 1 − δ. Distribution-free; no assumptions on the score.

Sentinel's move: calibrate on the terminal score of each trajectory. Across trajectories, ηiHi are i.i.d. — the correlation lives inside a trajectory, not between them. Then monotonicity converts a statement about the endpoint into a statement about the whole path.

Proposition 2 · STAC has low FPR

Let Dτ = {τi} be i.i.d. successful rollouts from the closed-loop nominal distribution Pτ, and set γ to the ⌈(M+1)(1−δ)⌉/M empirical quantile of the terminal scores {ηiHi}. Then

FPR := ℙPτ[ ∃ 0 ≤ t ≤ H′ s.t. ηt > γ ] ≤ δ

Every summand D̂ ≥ 0, so η0 ≤ ηk ≤ … ≤ ηH′. Therefore the event "η crosses γ at some point" is identical to the event "ηH′ > γ" — not merely implied by it. Theorem 1 applied to the i.i.d. terminal scores bounds the latter by δ. ∎

The three honest caveats, from the paper's own §D remarks

(a) Only FPR is bounded. Detection is not guaranteed, and cannot be without failure data or distributional assumptions on the OOD regime. (b) The guarantee holds for trajectories i.i.d. with Dτ; under distribution shift there is no guarantee — which is the point, but also means the bound says nothing about false alarms on OOD successes, which is where the embedding baselines die. (c) Taking the OR of two detectors can, worst case, add their false-alarm rates.

The published numbers use the 95th quantile, i.e. δ = 0.05.

The VLM detector and the OR

Deliberately simple. The formulation is chain-of-thought video question answering:

  • Input: a subsampled image history I0:t = (I0, Iνk, I2νk, …, It), where ν sets frame frequency relative to the execution horizon k.
  • Prompt: a task description, the VLM's role as runtime monitor, and — critically — the elapsed time t and the time limit H. Without this a slow-but-progressing robot is indistinguishable from a stalled one.
  • Output: CoT reasoning, then a classification in {ok, failure}. temperature = 0.0, max_tokens = 1000. Prompts are YAML templates per domain; images may be centre-cropped and resized per domain/model.
  • Optional: a reference video of a successful rollout, and goal images, gated by template settings flags.

The measured lesson is blunt and replicated in Table 1: a single image gives GPT-4o a TNR of 0.00. Given one frame it calls everything a failure. Given the video it reaches 0.89–0.95 TNR. Temporal context is not an enhancement here, it is the difference between a usable detector and a constant.

Combination is a logical OR over the two warnings. Nothing cleverer — and the paper flags that as a limitation.

The finding that should change what we do

Look at the OOD TNR column. All three embedding-similarity detectors score 0.00. When the scenario is out of distribution and the policy succeeds anyway, they fire every single time. They are not detecting failure; they are detecting novelty, and novelty is not failure — a 41% success rate OOD means the policy generalises in four cases out of ten, and the embedding detector calls all four a failure.

This is the same shape as our own R-036 negative: GR00T's VL embeddings did not separate its own failures. Sentinel found this in 2024 with three different encoders, including CLIP and ResNet, on a domain where the same detector achieves 0.95 TNR in distribution. It is not a property of a weak encoder. It is a property of the question.

Two more results worth carrying:

  • Task-progression failures break STAC and the VLM saves it. On shifts that produce smooth-but-wrong behaviour, STAC gets 44% TPR. The VLM lifts the combination to 93% TPR for +7% FPR. The failure category determines which detector works — this is the paper's actual thesis and it is well supported.
  • Real world, 10 successes / 10 failures, M = 10 calibration rollouts: STAC 0.80/0.90, GPT-4o Video QA 0.90/1.00, Sentinel 1.00/0.90, 95% accuracy. Ten calibration trajectories is a strikingly small budget for a conformal threshold — at M=10, δ=0.05, the quantile index ⌈11·0.95⌉ = 11 > M, so the threshold is effectively the maximum observed score and the nominal guarantee is at its boundary.

The repo is structured as two decoupled stages: generate offline datasets of policy rollouts (optional — they publish ~319 GB of them), then evaluate detectors on those stored datasets. Dataset naming encodes the design: ca in-distribution calibration, na in-distribution test, hh OOD erratic failures, ss OOD task-progression failures. That separation is exactly our trace-store-then-analyse shape, and it is why their detector code is portable while their policy code is not.

Where the method is soft

  1. The cumulative score is partly a clock. ηt is a sum of non-negative terms, so it grows with episode length whether or not anything is wrong. Calibrating γ on the terminal scores of successes means any sufficiently long rollout crosses eventually. Sentinel is protected because its erratic failures are fast and its horizons are fixed — detection times of 5–8 s against much longer episodes. It is not a defect there. It is a defect for us; see §11.
  2. The guarantee covers the wrong error. FPR ≤ δ holds only on i.i.d.-with-Dτ trajectories. Every interesting deployment is by definition not that. The bound is a statement about nominal operation, not about the regime the monitor exists for.
  3. Batch size is a hidden capacity knob. B = 32 vs 256 is chosen per domain by multimodality. MMD's variance scales roughly as 1/B, and the V-statistic's positive bias as O(1/B) — so the score's floor moves with B. Comparing scores across settings with different B is not meaningful.
  4. The median heuristic re-estimates bandwidth per timestep. Convenient, but it means D̂ is computed under a different kernel at every step, and the sum in Eq. 1 adds quantities from different metrics. Defensible in practice; not clean.
  5. Two detectors, one OR, no arbitration. The paper says so. There is no notion of which detector is more trustworthy in a given context, and no confidence attached to either.
  6. Nothing localises. STAC says the policy is contradicting itself; the VLM says the robot is not progressing. Neither says which part of the policy is responsible. Sentinel is a detection paper, full stop — a point worth stating plainly before we try to bend it to a mechanism question.

Part IV · Transfer

Transfer: our pipeline, our two questions

We already have what Sentinel spends its first stage producing: rollouts with labelled outcomes, including failures, in a trace store. What we want is downstream of anything the paper does — localise the failure to a mechanism, and/or decide whether the current task is out of distribution. Here is what survives, what dies, and what changes shape.

How architecture-agnostic is it, really?

The paper's claim is narrow and correct: "STAC is agnostic to the generative policy formulation, whereas the DDPM loss baseline is specific to diffusion policies." But the framework has three layers with different answers, and conflating them overstates what transfers.

Nobody has run this on a VLA

The paper's future work names monitoring "high-capacity policies [2, 23]" — Octo and OpenVLA. Every result in it is on a diffusion policy or EquiBot, in domains with 7–14 DoF and hand-specified action spaces. Running STAC on GR00T N1.7 is not a reproduction; it is the extension the authors flagged and did not do. Worth pricing as such — including the risk that it simply does not transfer.

The blocking structural fact: we have no chunk overlap

GR00T N1.7 on our eval path runs with RTC off and executes all 16 actions of each chunk, so k = h = 16 and the overlap band in F2 has width zero. STAC's D̂(π̄t, π̃t+k) is undefined — the repo's own compute_temporal_error asserts pred_horizon - exec_horizon > 0 and would refuse to run. STAC's core signal is not available to us as the pipeline currently stands. Two routes restore it:

RouteWhat it costsWhat it preserves
Reduce k at eval — set n_action_steps to e.g. 4, giving overlap 12A new campaign; and it is a different controller, so existing failure cases do not transferTrue STAC, online, cheapest per episode

Shadow inference is the better first move: it is post-hoc forensics on stored traces, which is the posture the rest of our programme already takes, and it does not invalidate the failure set.

S4 is not STAC — and Sentinel already measured what S4 is worth

Our S4 tap (call the head k times from the same conditioning, take the spread) is not a temporal-consistency statistic. It is spread across draws at one moment, which is precisely Sentinel's Diffusion Output Variance baseline.

That baseline is the worst-performing method in Table 1: TPR 0.33 in distribution, 0.28 OOD, 0.72 combined accuracy. It is beaten by every temporal method and by the pretrained-encoder embeddings. This is a direct, published, apples-to-apples measurement of the quantity S4 computes, and it says S4 should be budgeted as a weak covariate, not as a detector. The ~20–30% capture surcharge for k=4 resampling should be priced against that expectation.

What ports unchanged

  1. Reference-from-successes-only. No negative class anywhere. We already do this in ood_selfref; Sentinel confirms it is sufficient and standard.
  2. Split-conformal calibration with δ as a design parameter. Our leave-one-out percentile is the same idea; adopting the exact ⌈(M+1)(1−δ)⌉/M index makes the false-positive rate a stated guarantee rather than an empirical percentile, at no cost.
  3. Leave-trajectory-out, not leave-frame-out. Sentinel computes calibration scores for a frame against trajectories other than its own. This is the same invariant our ood_selfref header is shouting about — and Sentinel's version is stricter, because it excludes the whole episode, not just the point.
  4. Episode-level roll-up from per-frame scores. np.any over per-frame predictions, detection time = first firing, TN = never fired. This is §3 of our methodology doc — score per frame, aggregate per episode, test where the label was measured — implemented.
  5. The complementary-category architecture. Not the specific detectors: the discipline of defining failure categories as complements and assigning a detector with a matched latency budget to each.

What must be adapted: the length confound

Our failures time out at 281 steps; our successes finish around 113. Sentinel's ηt is monotone increasing by construction. Import it naively and we build a detector whose leading signal is duration.

So: our existing choice of a per-episode OOD fraction trades Proposition 2's any-time guarantee for length-robustness. That is the correct trade for our data, and it should be stated as a deliberate departure rather than an oversight. If we want both, the route is ep_iid_cum_per_t: a cumulative score with a threshold calibrated separately at each forward index — Sentinel's own fifth variant, which exists in the repo and not in the paper.

A second caution, from Sentinel's weakest baseline

The Temporal Non-Distributional Minimum baseline scores a chunk by its distance to the nearest sample from the comparison set — and collapses to 0.27 TNR out of distribution because a min over samples throws away the shape of the distribution. Our ood_selfref scores a frame by nearest-neighbour distance to the reference cloud. The two are not the same use (ours is a cross-episode manifold distance, theirs a chunk-to-chunk comparison), so this is not a refutation. But it is a reason to check the distributional alternative — Mahalanobis against the reference cloud, which is Sentinel's Eq. 3 and is already implemented in error_utils.mahal_embeddings — as a cheap robustness check on the 6.9× number.

A concrete design, and its kill switches

Question A — is the current task OOD?

Sentinel's answer is uncomfortable and worth taking seriously: this is the wrong question to ask of a detector, because state atypicality and policy failure are different events, and the paper measures a domain where they come apart completely (41% of OOD scenarios succeed; the embedding detectors call all of them failures).

The useful reformulation is not a scalar but a 2×2. Keep the state-embedding detector — it is a legitimate measurement of novelty — and cross it with the outcome we already have:

Concretely: run the embedding detector at each tap, report the 2×2 counts per perturbation category, and never report a bare OOD rate without the outcome cross-tabulation beside it. R-036's result — that the state signal separated failures at a similar rate in every perturbation category — is exactly the signature of a detector living on the wrong axis of this table.

Question B — which mechanism?

Sentinel does not answer this, but it supplies the one piece of machinery that makes a cross-tap comparison legitimate. The mechanism claim in our methodology doc §5 is relative: at forward j, are the VLM-side taps inside the manifold while the action-side taps are outside? That comparison is only meaningful if every tap's threshold was set to the same false-positive rate. Otherwise "S1 fired and S3 did not" may mean nothing more than that S1's threshold was looser.

The design

For each tap s ∈ {S0, S0e, S1, S2, S3}:

  1. Score. S(s)j = Mahalanobis (or top-k) distance of tap s at forward j to the success-reference cloud for that tap, built leave-trajectory-out. Do not pool across embodiments — S0e comes from a per-embodiment CategorySpecificLinear.
  2. Calibrate. One threshold per tap, per forward index, at a common δ, using the ⌈(M+1)(1−δ)⌉/M conformal index on the reference trajectories. Common δ is what makes step 3 legible.
  3. Read the pattern, not the OR. The output per episode is the indicator vector over taps at the earliest forward any of them fires, plus the onset index per tap. Sentinel's OR would destroy exactly the information the mechanism question needs.
  4. Verdict. First firing in pathway order S1 → S2 → S0e → S3 nominates the stage. Upstream fault ⇒ source more diverse scenes. Downstream fault ⇒ source more diverse trajectories within existing scenes.

And if shadow inference (§11) restores chunk overlap, STAC itself becomes a sixth signal in that vector — an action-side self-consistency measure that is, per Table 1, substantially stronger than anything the embedding taps provide. That would be the single highest-value addition to the panel.

Kill switches — run these before believing any of it

  1. Success-vs-success split. Run the whole panel on two halves of the successful episodes. If the taps separate, the method is reading scene identity or episode length, not failure. Cheapest possible invalidation; run it first.
  2. Common-δ audit. Confirm each tap's threshold fires at the nominal rate on the reference itself. Sentinel's leave-trajectory-out calibration exists for this; a tap whose reference rate is far from δ is mis-calibrated and its cross-tap comparison is void.
  3. Matched-index comparison. Never compare aggregates over episodes of different length (F6). Fix the forward index.
  4. Architectural-phase control. If the onset ordering is computed across DiT depth, the AlternateVLDiT block structure imposes a period-4 pattern by construction. The null is not "no periodicity" but "periodicity at exactly the architectural phase" — and the text-attending blocks are idx % 4 == 0 while the image-attending ones are idx % 4 == 2. Take the period from the forward pass, never from the config.
  5. Shuffled labels. Permute episode outcomes; the separation must vanish. Catches leakage through duration.
