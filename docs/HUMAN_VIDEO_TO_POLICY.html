Method & capability argument · drafted 2026-09-25

From one captioned human video to a policy

We hold egocentric footage of an operator working an assembly line, cut into captioned stages, and no robot actions at all. This file argues what that asset is worth, which of the four published routes out of it survives contact with our constraints, and what has to be measured before any of it is believed.

Bottom line

From human video, the actions transfer and the pixels do not. Our own R-039 found that every perturbation category reaches the action through the image tokens; a head-mounted human view is the largest camera-and-embodiment perturbation we could construct on purpose. Pre-training a policy on those pixels is Mind's route (§7 P1) and it is affordable at their scale, not ours.

So the recommendation inverts the usual pipeline: spend the engineering on recovering an action stream from the hands, use the captions to specify and segment, and render the observations from simulation or the robot — never from the human footage. That is route B′ below, and it is the only route whose error can be measured before a single frame of real footage is processed.

Part I · What the asset is worth

1 · What we hold, stated precisely

A long egocentric recording from a head-worn camera; the operator's hands are visible throughout; the recording has been cut into stages, one stage per step of the assembly, each carrying a dense caption. This is, almost exactly, the substrate Mind Robotics says it collects — video data at industrial scale, capturing live, high-variability work as it happens on the line, gathered on wearable head- and body-worn capture rigs — and it comes with the same structural hole.

PresentAbsent
RGB frames of expert, in-distribution workJoint commands, end-effector poses, gripper state
Stage boundaries (a temporal segmentation)Metric scale — monocular video does not carry it
Dense per-stage natural languageFailures. An expert at takt time mostly succeeds
A camera that moves with attentionA camera that matches any robot's viewpoint

Three of those four absences are recoverable with work. The fourth — failures — is not recoverable from expert footage at all, and is worth flagging early because it is the same gap Mind's teleoperation cockpit exists to fill (§7 P6). A policy trained only on this asset will have no model of what a failed seat looks like or how to recover from one.

2 · The three things a captioned human video can actually buy

A motor prior

What the hand does, as a trajectory. This is the expensive one and the only one that teaches the policy how. It requires inverting the video into actions — §5.

A task specification

What to do, and in what order. The captions give this for free, and it is not nothing: R-038 found GR00T scores 50/100 with an empty prompt against 100/100 with the instruction, and 0/10 on the drawer scene where it grabs the wrong object.

A curriculum

The stage cuts are a decomposition of a long-horizon job into atomic units. Every route in the field — GEN-1.5's atomic tasks, Mind's inferred two-level policy (§7 P3), our own perturbation rows — operates on atomic units. Somebody has to produce that decomposition, and here it already exists and is human-authored rather than VLM-guessed.

That last point deserves its weight. Our own experiments/vlm_label.py is unvalidated, and on the first real failure both working models detected the grasp and named the wrong object; FailBench (arXiv:2609.03611) finds robotics-specialised VLMs underperform their base models. Mind's P3 assumes machine-generated episode labels because hand annotation does not scale to industrial hours. A human-authored dense caption track is therefore the part of this asset that the large labs explicitly cannot afford, and the part whose value is easiest to defend commercially.

3 · Where annotation converts into capability

Ordered by how directly each label type becomes model behaviour, not by how hard it is to produce.

Read commercially: the rows a model cannot produce for itself are the product. Hand keypoints are estimable and therefore weak as a service; grasp frames, contact events, retry marks and a single known dimension in the scene are cheap for a human, unreliable for a VLM, and each one unblocks a specific downstream channel.

Part II · Method

4 · The four routes, and which survive

A · Caption layer only. runs today Use the captions as subtask language and the cuts as boundaries. Feeds GR00T N1.7 with no new machinery. Teaches the policy what to do and never how. Necessary, not sufficient.

B · Latent actions from action-free video. scale-gated Learn a VQ codebook or inverse-dynamics model over consecutive frames, pretrain to predict latent actions conditioned on the caption, then decode latents to actuator commands with a small paired robot set. This is LAPA's recipe, GR00T N1.x's human-video branch, and Mind's inferred P1. The confound is specific to our footage: on a head-mounted camera most inter-frame pixel motion is the operator's head. A codebook trained naively spends its capacity modelling gaze. GEN-0's ossification finding — models below ~7B stop absorbing pretraining data — also says this route wants a scale we do not have.

B′ · Explicit hand-pose retargeting. recommended Hands are visible throughout, so do not learn the action space — extract it. Detailed in §5. Converts the asset into supervised action data with a measurable error bar.

C · Demo-as-prompt, no training. blocked Put the clip in context: GEN-1.5 §2, RoboTTT, BPP. GR00T is n_obs_steps: 1 — there is no history slot, and adding one means retraining the backbone. BPP's released checkpoints were trained with ignore_prompt_action: False, so an action-free prompt needs a retrained prompt encoder, and BPP training wants ~46 GB against our 8. RoboTTT is the right shape — GR00T N1.7 plus TTT layers, one-shot imitation from a single human video at 6/10 on unseen configurations — and has released no weights. Watch item, not a plan.

5 · The recommended pipeline

Per frame, recover:

  • Wrist pose in the camera frame from a 3D hand-pose estimator.
  • Camera ego-motion from visual-inertial odometry or SLAM over the same footage, and compose it with the wrist pose. This is the step that kills the head-motion confound in route B: a trajectory expressed in the world frame no longer moves when the operator merely looks away.
  • Grip aperture as a continuous scalar from thumb–index separation, thresholded into open/closed using the human-marked grasp and release frames (§3) rather than a learned cutoff.
action_t = [ p_xyz (3) , R_6d (6) , g (1) ] # world frame, resampled to the control rate p_xyz = T_world←cam(t) · p_wrist_cam(t) · s s = metric scale, fixed once from a known fixture dimension

That is the same action space BPP already consumes — [L, 20, 10], pos3 + rot6 + grip1 — and it is adjacent to GR00T's LIBERO 8-D. The human video stops being action-free.

The scale trap

Monocular video does not carry metric scale; a hand-pose estimator returns a trajectory up to an unknown multiplier. Everything downstream — grasp offsets, approach distances, the whole error budget in §6 — is in centimetres, so s must come from somewhere real: a stereo head rig, a depth channel, or one measured dimension of a fixture that appears in the footage. The last is the cheapest and is an annotation task, not a research problem. If scale cannot be fixed, B′ degrades to B and the recommendation changes.

6 · The validation gate

The dossier's verdict on Mind is that it is a claims ledger, not a results ledger. A file from us proposing the same pipeline with no verification step earns the same line. So the method's spine is a round trip against ground truth we already own:

The round trip

Take a sim rollout whose true action sequence is recorded. Re-render it from a simulated head-mounted viewpoint with a hand model in place of the gripper. Run the full B′ extractor over that render. Compare the recovered action stream to the actions we already have.

This yields a number before any real footage is touched, and it satisfies the roster's reproducibility rule in the only form available here — there is no published figure to reproduce, so the pipeline has to reproduce itself. Proposed thresholds, set by what LIBERO grasps actually tolerate:

ChannelBudgetWhy that number
wrist position< 1 cm RMSR-038's failing episodes miss by 25.2 cm against a 4.4 cm control; a grasp tolerance of order 1–2 cm is the relevant scale
wrist rotation< 5° RMSApproach-axis error compounds over a 16-step chunk
grip transition< 100 msEarly release drops the part; late close collides. Timing, not value, is what matters
scale s< 3% 3% of a 30 cm reach is ~1 cm, i.e. the position budget

Two honest caveats on the gate. A sim render is an easy case for a pose estimator — clean lighting, no motion blur, a hand model rather than a hand — so passing it is necessary and not sufficient; the real-footage number will be worse and must be bounded separately against a small hand-annotated keypoint set. And R-039 measured 23/30 agreement at render-jitter level, so any downstream success-rate comparison built on this needs ≥50 rollouts per cell regardless of how clean the extraction looks.

7 · Staged plan

S0 · Caption layer

Ingest the stage cuts and captions into the episode record schema. Score the existing VLM labeller against the human captions on the same stages — this is the first validated measurement of vlm_label.py we would have, and it is free. Output: a human-vs-VLM agreement number, and an instruction set.

S1 · Extractor, validated in sim

Build the B′ extractor and run the §6 round trip. Gate: all four budgets met on sim renders before any real footage is processed. Kill condition: if position error exceeds ~3 cm, retargeting cannot support grasping and the programme falls back to B or to teleop-only.

S2 · Mechanism proof on LIBERO

Does a retargeted action stream plus caption conditioning beat the language baseline on held-out instances? Run it where we already know the noise floor, the perturbation rows and the failure buckets. This is the cheap, falsifiable half, and it does not need the assembly cell to exist.

S3 · The assembly cell

Only now: build the cell in sim or on hardware, collect the paired teleop set that grounds the decoder, and test transfer. This is where Mind's M5 — co-designing fixtures and presentation around the robot rather than imitating the human motion path — becomes available to us too, and it quietly lowers the bar S2's policy has to clear.

8 · Falsifiable predictions

  1. The extractor passes in sim and degrades on real footage by more than 2×. Motion blur, occlusion by parts and tools, and glove texture are all absent from the render. If it degrades by less, the sim gate is a stronger instrument than claimed here.
  2. Caption conditioning alone (route A) moves nothing on perturbation rows. R-039 says perturbations reach the action through image tokens; language cannot repair perception. It should, however, close R-038's empty-prompt and wrong-object gaps by construction.
  3. Human pixels used as observations underperform sim-rendered observations at matched action data. This is the thesis. If it fails — if training on the human frames helps — then the pixel channel carries transferable information at our scale, and Mind's P1 is cheaper than argued here.
  4. Occlusion-heavy stages (fastening, connectors) fail the extractor first. Same stages Mind names as hardest. Expect route B as the fallback exactly there, and expect it to need the paired set it does not have.

9 · Open decisions

  • Where metric scale comes from. Stereo rig, depth, or an annotated fixture dimension. Blocks S1.
  • Which policy S2 runs on. GR00T N1.7 is the roster priority and has the harness; BPP already speaks this action space and has published numbers to reproduce, but cannot be trained here.
  • Whether failures exist anywhere in the footage. If the operator never fumbles, the corrective distribution has to come from teleop intervention later — Mind's P6 — and that is an S3 cost, not an S1 one.
  • Sim or hardware for the cell. Mind's P5 reads simulation as a release gate and not a data source, which is the correct call for contact-rich work; if S3 goes to sim, that tension has to be argued rather than assumed.
