Written at the user's request ("go over the metrics … what they mean, give reasons on how each metric is used or what it shows, what are the trends in it, why it could be useful, make a separate doc"). The user is the checker for now; no metric gates anything (user, 10-26): they are reported and discussed. Values come from the existing run files (runs/real2sim/…, batch summaries read 10-26) and SIM_FIXES; all exploratory, UNVERIFIED. Everything so far is on one scene (orange) and one policy (GR00T-N1.7-DROID); c3 is the only second scene. Companion: SCENE_V1_MECHANISMS (how each step works).

Short version.
  1. Success alone is too sparse to steer by. With 33 variants the 95% intervals of our batches overlap almost completely: 4/33 is 5–27%, 9/33 is 15–44%, 13/33 is 25–56%. Only the geometry fixes and the new wrist renderer moved anything clearly, and they showed up first in closes, lifts and blocked arms, not in success.
  2. The most informative policy numbers so far are the stage funnel (reached → closed → lifted → placed), the blocked-arm count, and the image-swap planned-height gap (cm, continuous, needs no rollouts).
  3. Build metrics (table height, calibration, object size, settle, expert solve rate) are what v1 can measure on every scene without a policy. Across 15–20 scenes their pass rates per step show which step generalises.
  4. Proposed headline set for discussion in §6.

1 · How to read a number here

  • Counts out of small n. A batch is 33 variants of one scene (9–10 for the partial ones). A 95% Wilson interval is shown next to rates; two batches whose intervals overlap heavily are not shown to differ.
  • Paired comparisons. Batches since D2 replay the same layouts (--layouts_from), so the same 33 episodes can be compared one to one; the p-value is McNemar's on the episodes that flipped. 4 flips each way = no evidence of change.
  • Run-to-run noise is not measured. GR00T isn't seeded in our rollouts; the same batch twice would flip some episodes by chance (checklist 6.6, ~50 min GPU). Until it's run, per-episode differences under ~4–5 flips can be noise.
  • One scene. Every trend below is from the orange scene; it says what changed on that scene, not what generalises. The 15–20 scenes are what turn these into generalisation evidence.
  • Threshold ≠ gate. Values like "≤ 1.5 cm" turn a measurement into PASS/FAIL in a report. Nothing in the pipeline stops on them (checked 10-26 in scene_checks.py; see SCENE_V1_MECHANISMS §12b).
