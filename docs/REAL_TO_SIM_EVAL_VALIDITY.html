Real-to-sim evaluation: is it shown to be valid, and do our scenes qualify?

0 · Verdict

Is real-to-sim policy evaluation shown to be valid? Partly. It has been shown for one thing: ranking several policies or checkpoints against each other. Ten studies ran learned policies in scenes rebuilt from real captures and on the real robot (§1). Seven report a headline sim–real Pearson r between 0.89 and 0.96; the lowest single task or environment is 0.81. An eighth (YUBI) got 8 of 9 pairwise rankings right [V]. Each compared only 1–6 policies or checkpoints; two of the seven correlate across tasks or training stages of one policy, not across policies.

What it has not been shown to do: predict a policy's absolute success rate, predict whether a single episode succeeds, or reproduce where a policy fails.

  • Absolute success rates: sim–real gaps of 0–66 points even where rankings hold (SimFoundry: π0 succeeds 100% real vs 34% sim on one task; YUBI: mean absolute error 31 points while 8/9 rankings were right) [V].
  • Single episodes: SureSim's per-episode sim–real correlation ranged from 0.70 down to −0.05 [V].
  • Failure stages (grasp, carry, release): nobody measures them. Only one paper says qualitatively that failure modes "look similar" [V].

Under what conditions does ranking hold? Six, and the studies that report good correlation meet all six:

  1. The scene is built from a dedicated multi-view scan or video of the real workspace.
  2. Every image the policy sees, including the wrist view and the robot itself, looks like the real one. Visual mismatch is the main error source (PolaRiS: sim images cost about 25% success, sim dynamics cost nothing).
  3. The robot controller is system-identified against real trajectories.
  4. Object layouts and initial states match the real evaluation.
  5. Rigid objects, mostly pick-and-place.
  6. The scores being compared are rankings, not absolute rates.
Whether the policy needs a light fine-tune on sim data first is disputed. PolaRiS needed it: off-the-shelf DROID VLAs correlated "too low to accurately rank". SimFoundry did not need it.

Do our scenes meet these conditions today? No.

  • They are built from one recorded episode, not a dedicated scan. The published method closest to ours, RobotArena∞ (one frame from a fixed camera, LaMa-inpainted background, robot-based camera calibration, DROIDSim), checked against the real robot on one task with three policies and reports no correlation.
  • Our robot is a plain sim render, the wrist view is stitched together from other images, and the controller is not system-identified.
  • The generated task variants have no real counterpart.
  • Above all, there is not one real-robot outcome of any policy on any of our scenes. The real episode was driven by a human operator.
Our geometry checks (stereo table height, grasp and release positions from forward kinematics) are stronger than what most papers report. The test of GR00T on the real recorded frames (it closes at frame 70 vs the operator's 75 and releases at ~120 vs ~126) is a good sanity check. Neither one makes the eval sets valid.

What would make them count: paired real outcomes. We have no robot cell (user, 10-13). Real outcomes are still available without one: RoboArena publishes its real DROID evaluations (MIT licence; 3,883 sessions and 10,783 policy episodes in the 2026-07-17 snapshot, each with exterior and wrist video, proprioception and success scores) [V]. Rebuilding a set of those scenes with our pipeline, running the same open-source policies, and correlating against the recorded real scores is the minimum experiment (§7). It validates our pipeline with DROID policies. It does not validate GR00T directly, because GR00T is not in RoboArena's policy index [V].

Until then: label every result from our real-to-sim eval sets "sim-only, not validated against real". Report comparisons and rankings, never absolute success rates.

3 · What makes sim track real

ConditionEvidence
C1 · What the policy sees must match, including the robot and the wrist view. This is the main error source.PolaRiS hardware-in-the-loop (π0-FAST, joint position, 3 tasks): real images + sim dynamics gave no drop; sim images + real dynamics gave about a 25% drop. "Visual mismatch is indeed the dominant source of error" [V]. SIMPLER: green screen, object textures and robot textures help only all together (MMRV 0.087 → 0.050); Octo-Base gets 0% in sim with an untuned arm colour vs 29.3% tuned [V]. Soft-body paper: dropping colour alignment cuts r on every task (push-T 0.915 → 0.529) [V]. ReVeal: DINOv2 real-vs-render similarity predicts agreement (r 0.795) [V]. PolaRiS: splats beat ray-traced and untextured renders even after co-training [V].
C2 · Geometry of the surfaces the task touchesReVeal: completeness and roughness of task-relevant planes predict agreement (r 0.759 / 0.731) [V]. SIMPLER aligns table edges and the gripper at initialisation [V]. YUBI: background holes and floaters flip 1 of 9 rankings [V].
C3 · System-identified control; joint-position actionsSIMPLER Table II: system-identified PD gains give MMRV 0.031 vs 0.070 / 0.100 for two hand settings [V]. REALM, ReVeal and RobotArena∞ all calibrate the controller against real trajectories [V]. PolaRiS chose joint-position actions because they "suffer from less accumulating error" in sim [V].
C4 · Physics matters for contact-rich and deformable tasksSoft-body paper: without physics identification, rope r falls 0.901 → 0.832 and toy packing 0.944 → 0.694 [V]. SIMPLER: physical parameters move success by up to ~15% but keep rankings [V]. Everyone else is rigid pick-and-place.
C5 · Initial states and the task distribution match the real evaluationSureSim pairs each real trial with sim runs within 2 × 2 cm of the real object position [V]. YUBI and the soft-body paper use matched initial layouts [V]. ReVeal samples sim initial states inside the real demo ranges [V]. In SureSim, changing which initial conditions were used moved per-episode ρ from 0.59 to −0.05 [V].
C6 · Policy adaptation to sim (disputed)PolaRiS: DROID VLAs without sim co-training correlate "too low to accurately rank". About 1k steps on ~350 out-of-domain sim demos fixes it; longer training hurts [V]. Practical Recipe: a light fine-tune raises REALM's Spearman from 0.700 to 0.875 and is non-monotonic in data (10 demos beat 20) [V]. SimFoundry: zero-shot works (r 0.911) [V]. Caveat: a co-trained policy is no longer the checkpoint you deploy. R2S-Eval goes further and evaluates sim-trained counterparts [V].
C7 · What transfers: rankings, not ratesEvery study claims rankings or correlation, not equality. SIMPLER: the goal is "not to obtain a 1:1 reproduction" [V]. PolaRiS "does not entirely replace real-world evaluation" [V]. Mean |sim − real| across SimFoundry Table G.1: 17.2 points over 29 cells, with sim lower than real on most fine-tuned tasks (computed [I]). Perturbation-sensitivity ordering transferred for REALM but not for SIMPLER or VLA-Arena [V].
C8 · Sample sizeThe correlations are over 2–6 policies per study (SIMPLER Bridge: 3). An r over 3–6 points has wide uncertainty [I]. SIMPLER itself notes r "does not reflect the range of values it is computed over" (per SIM_TO_REAL_TRANSFER §1.1 [I]). Real-side noise: 20 hardware trials gave 55–75% for the same policy in MetaFine [V].

Reported cases where sim misled

  • Same tasks, different pipeline: SimFoundry's tasks run in PolaRiS gave per-task r from −0.396 (Cup in Bowl) to 0.822, and PolaRiS "consistently underperformed" real [V]. Without co-training, PolaRiS's own correlation was too low to rank [V].
  • Big absolute gaps despite correct ranks: SimFoundry: π0 Stack Dishware 100% real vs 34% sim; GR00T N1.6 40% real vs 0% sim [V]. YUBI: MAE 31 points with 8/9 ranks right [V].
  • Ranking inversions: YUBI Flat Cup (real 40 vs 35%, sim 37.5 vs 73.75%) [V]. SIMPLER Bridge "light orange" task [V].
  • Per-episode prediction failed: SureSim π0, ρ ≈ −0.05 [V].
  • Hand-built and video-model simulators rank poorly: Libero scores 90–95% for policies that span the full real range; Ctrl-World MMRV 0.22 (both from PolaRiS) [V].
  • Robot appearance alone: Octo-Base 0% in sim with the default arm colour vs 29.3% tuned (SIMPLER) [V].

Bottom line. Before our eval sets count as valid we need, in order:

  1. Paired real outcomes on scenes built by our pipeline (§7). Nothing replaces this.
  2. A numeric visual-fidelity gate on held-out real frames for both cameras (D20/D21, DINOv2/SigLIP similarity).
  3. Controller system ID from the episode's own joint trajectories.
  4. Robot appearance matched (texture, or a composited real arm where possible).
  5. Reporting limited to rankings and comparisons.
Items 2–4 are what the evidence says drives agreement. They are cheap and run on CPU, but they are not validation by themselves.

6 · Our open case: GR00T releases ~13 cm short

Is a sim-vs-real release or placement bias reported for DROID-trained policies? No. I found none. Searches covered early or premature release, placement and per-stage sim-vs-real comparisons for DROID / π0 / GR00T real-to-sim evaluation. The papers in §2 report success, progress or preferences, not release position. The nearest facts:

  • Sim is often pessimistic for DROID policies. In SimFoundry most fine-tuned-task cells have sim below real (e.g. GR00T N1.6 Stack Dishware 40 → 0) [V]. PolaRiS policies "consistently underperformed" real (SimFoundry's comparison) [V]. That matches the direction of ours (0/2 in sim) without explaining the mechanism.
  • Visuals, not dynamics, produced PolaRiS's 25% drop [V]. Our own checks rule out execution lag (commanded vs achieved 1–3 cm), block slip and object misplacement in the exterior image. Joint-head vs hand-head differences of 1.2–1.5 cm cannot explain 13 cm (commit 816e510) [I].
  • On real frames the release is right: GR00T releases at ~120 vs the operator's ~126 (G1, df2cc9f) [I]. So the policy can time the release from real images. The short release appears in our sim.
  • Real robots also fail at release: SureSim's real scoring has a "failed release" grade (0.75) [V]. Release failures happen on real hardware too, so a sim release failure is not automatically an artefact.

Hypotheses, ranked by what the evidence predicts (all [I]):

  1. H1 · Visual cue mismatch at the release decision (plain-render robot or bowl, or the stitched wrist view, makes the bowl look closer). This is the literature's main error source. Test: D20 image swap at the release frames of the real episode (real exterior + sim wrist, and the reverse). See whether the predicted gripper-open step moves.
  2. H2 · The variant asks for a longer carry than the policy produces. The rollouts needed 33.4 / 30.9 cm; GR00T carried 24.0 / 18.8 cm; the real operator carried 28.3 cm in the recorded layout (816e510). Test with the running sweep: regress release position on the required carry distance across variants. If release distance stays roughly constant, the policy is not tracking the bowl. Then run the original recorded layout as the control.
  3. H3 · Residual scale error. Check A5 scale 0.907 means about 10% of a ~30 cm carry, i.e. about 3 cm, far below 13 cm. Unlikely to be the main cause.

7 · Recommended validity protocol for our eval sets

7.0 · Today, on CPU: per-scene gates (necessary, not sufficient)

  • Visual fidelity on held-out real frames, both cameras: frames not used to build the scene, rendered from the recorded pose. Report DINOv2 and SigLIP cosine plus LPIPS (as in ReVeal), and D20/D21.
  • Controller system ID from the episode's recorded joint trajectory (as in SIMPLER and REALM).
  • Run every rollout on the original recorded layout as well as the variants. It is the only variant with a real reference, even a human one.

7.1 · The minimum experiment: paired validation on RoboArena data (no robot needed)

Why RoboArena. The HF data dumps (RoboArena/DataDump_07-17-2026, MIT) hold real DROID policy rollouts. Each session has one instruction in one real scene, 2+ policies, per-policy binary_success and partial_success, exterior video (left/right "when available"), wrist video and a proprioception/action npz [V]. That is exactly our pipeline's input, plus real outcomes for several policies in the same scene.

  1. Select: ≥10 sessions, rigid pick-and-place, where ≥2 open-source joint-position policies ran. The index marks MolmoAct2-DROID, Cosmos3-Nano-Policy, the floppy_bagel checkpoints and others as open and joint-position [V]. Checkpoint availability and whether each fits the 8 GB GPU are unchecked [I].
  2. Rebuild each scene from one of its episodes with our unchanged pipeline, and run the §7.0 gates.
  3. Run each policy 20–50 times in sim from the session's initial layout (SureSim-style 2 × 2 cm jitter).
  4. Compare, pre-registered:
    • per-policy mean success and progress across sessions: Pearson r and MMRV;
    • within-session pairwise ranking agreement (YUBI's metric);
    • per-episode paired correlation (SureSim).
  5. Pass bar: r ≥ 0.8 and MMRV ≤ 0.12 (the published range, §2), and ≥ 80% pairwise agreement. Below the bar, eval sets stay "sim-only".

Cost. Sample episode files are ~0.4 MB exterior, 1.3–1.6 MB wrist and ~48 KB npz [V]. So 10 sessions × 2–3 policies is well under 1 GB [I]. GPU: 10 scenes × 3 policies × 20 rollouts = 600 rollouts. At G2's pace (~1 min per rollout, inferred from its 13:18–13:20 window) that is ~10 GPU-hours [I]. Scene builds are res31's time. The policy servers for non-GR00T checkpoints are new work.

Limits of this design.

  • It validates the pipeline, not GR00T: GR00T is not in RoboArena's index [V].
  • The real side has one rollout per policy per session, so outcomes must be pooled across sessions, which needs more sessions.
  • Our stereo depth fix (LESSONS #12) needs the ZED stereo pair. Whether RoboArena's "left/right" videos are stereo pairs or two cameras is unchecked [I].
  • The npz holds pickled objects. I did not load it, so its fields are unchecked. Load it only in an isolated environment.
  • Session metadata includes evaluator e-mail addresses: don't copy them into our docs.

7.2 · Extending to GR00T and to generated variants

  • GR00T: no robot-free path to real GR00T outcomes found. Options: (a) evidence by transfer (SimFoundry GR00T N1.7 tracked real within 0–20 points on 4 DROID tasks); (b) a real cell or a partner (the user decides); (c) whether RoboArena accepts external policy submissions is unchecked [I].
  • Generated variants: no paper validates layouts outside the real evaluation's range. Claim validity only for variants inside the real scene's object set and layout range; flag the rest as extrapolation.

7.3 · Gold standard, if a robot ever becomes available

The PolaRiS / ReVeal protocol: ≥4 policies or checkpoints × ≥5 scenes × 20 real rollouts = ≥400 real rollouts, with sim rollouts from matched initial states. Report r, MMRV and MAE, plus SureSim's combined estimate to cut the real budget by 20–25% [V].
