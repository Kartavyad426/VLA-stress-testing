1 · What was done

  • The bug. lerobot_train builds its Accelerator with step_scheduler_with_optimizer=False (third_party/lerobot/src/lerobot/configs/accelerator.py:239) and steps the scheduler every micro-batch. R-056's first run built a cosine over optimizer steps, so with accumulation 8 its LR cycled. That run is kept, not deleted: runs/r056_r16_cyclic_train, runs/r056_r16_cyclic_eval and runs/r056_r16_cyclic_train_report.json, each with a MOVED.txt.
  • F1 schedule. vla_harness/training/schedule.py holds a LambdaLR with lr·L(s // accum), stepped per micro-batch, where L is diffusers' lambda over optimizer steps. It is exact at every update. SFT's suggested micro-count construction lags by (accum−1)/accum of a step: 8.75e-7 at 2000 steps, but 4.4e-5 on a 40-step run.
  • F2 guard. experiments/r056_train.py:
    • At startup, a CPU subprocess drives the run's construction through lerobot's own AcceleratorConfig and update_policy, and aborts if any applied LR is more than 1e-6 from the closed form.
    • An optimizer pre-hook records the LR of every real update. The trace is re-checked at each checkpoint, and schedule_verified plus the LR go into vla_train_meta.json.
    • The cached-features loop gets the same.
    • --scheduler-builder legacy_scheduler exists only for the negative test.
  • F3 memory. _from_pretrained_cast in vla_harness/policies/lerobot_policy.py streams the checkpoint in bf16 in chunks of ≤512 MiB, and turns GR00T's model_params_fp32 off during the build (restored afterwards). experiments/r056_eval.py refuses adapters without schedule_verified, logs RSS (anon/file) and CUDA after every load, frees between loads (del, gc, empty_cache, malloc_trim), and has a loadcheck phase.
  • F4/F6 units. experiments/launch_r056_fixed.sh wraps a command in systemd-run --user --unit=$UNIT --collect plus systemd-inhibit. The queues are experiments/queue_r056_fixed.sh (unit vla-r056, launched 09-29 19:37) and experiments/queue_r056_cont.sh (unit vla-r056-cont, 19:51). The continuation:
    • stopped vla-r056 at 23:32 via experiments/r056_cut.sh, only once the chain was blocked on the lock with no python in its cgroup;
    • meters every step with experiments/stepmeter.py, writing runs/overnight/<step>.peaks.json;
    • appends headlines via experiments/r056fix_summary.py to runs/overnight/SUMMARY_r056fix.txt.
  • Small additions: experiments/r054_mint.py --start-attempt (default 0, so behaviour is unchanged), for the minting extension. tests/test_schedule.py has 10 CPU tests.
  • Relaunching the remainder (skip-safe: finished steps are detected and partial ones resume; do not run before D18/D19 are decided):
    UNIT=vla-r056-cont bash experiments/launch_r056_fixed.sh -- bash experiments/queue_r056_cont.sh

6 · Known bugs, risks and gotchas

  • RAM / OOM. 30 GB RAM, 7 GB swap, and systemd-oomd watches user@1000.service at 50% pressure. A GR00T load peaks ~16 GB total RSS (7.5 GiB anon). Eval processes reach 17–18 GiB RSS tree. The r64 cached loop reached 19.9 GB and was kernel-OOM-killed. Never run two GPU or GR00T loads at once. The VS Code scope was oomd-killed on 09-29 17:23, which is why runs go in separate units.
  • GPU. An 8 GB card: r64 resident training does not fit (7.4 GiB in use, then OOM). Every job takes flock /tmp/vla_gpu.lock; r056_cut.sh relies on that lock.
  • AC power. The 09-29 launch was on battery (94%). systemd-inhibit blocks sleep and lid-switch but cannot stop a flat battery. Plug in before any relaunch.
  • Render nondeterminism. LIBERO renders differ between runs on some instances (313 and 281 at forward 0), so action-level comparisons need frozen observations. Old vs old differed by up to 0.0098.
  • rollout_id ignores the noise seed. runner._cell_id(env, policy, seed, spec) has no noise term, so rollouts that differ only by noise seed share an id. r056_eval's exploratory offset breakdown looks start states up by rollout_id. The start state depends only on the env seed and spec, so the offset values should be unaffected, but any per-rollout join on that id across noise seeds is ambiguous. Not fixed (D25).
  • Directory names under checkpoints/ count lerobot micro-steps (004000 = opt 500); vla_train_meta.json has the optimizer step. A manifest's adapter paths inside the cyclic dirs still say runs/r056_r16_train.
  • accelerate warns "lr_scheduler.step() before optimizer.step()" every run. This is expected with accumulation and harmless.

SAE

owner: SAE · SAE-on-VLA paper analysis

This session was discussion only. The user wanted to understand the paper arXiv:2603.19183 (Swann et al., Stanford, v2 of 6 Jun 2026, "Sparse Autoencoders Reveal Interpretable and Steerable Features in VLA Models"). Nothing was decided, no runs were launched, and nothing else in the repo was edited.

1. The paper in brief

  • Models. π0.5-LIBERO and π0.5-DROID: both the PaliGemma backbone (PG) and the action expert (AE). Also OpenVLA fine-tuned on LIBERO.
  • Activations. The residual stream at layers 0, 5, 11 and 17. At each timestep, all tokens are averaged into one vector. That means about 768 image tokens plus about 20 text and state tokens.
  • SAE. TopK with AuxK (the Gao et al. recipe), one SAE per layer. The expansion ratio is 1: 2048 features with k = 100 on PG, and k = 64 on AE. Larger ratios gave many dead features on these small datasets: 273k timesteps for LIBERO and 567k for the 2k-episode DROID subset.
  • Generality metrics. Four numbers per feature: episode coverage c, mean onset count ō, mean peak activation ā, and relative run length ℓ̄r. A logistic regression trained on 30 hand-labelled features (15 general, 15 memorized) classifies every feature as general or memorized.
  • Claims.
    • 79% of 120 sampled SAE features are interpretable, against 6/20 FFN neurons.
    • Share of features classified as general: 2.6% (π0.5-LIBERO), 10.8% (π0.5-DROID), 0.45% (OpenVLA).
  • Causal tests.
    • On the real robot, removing the 4 most general features drops success from 39/40 to 0/40. Removing 4 random memorized features gives 37/40, and 4 random general features give 26/40.
    • The LIBERO single-feature ablation sweep covers 135 features and 20,250 episodes. Success stays at the baseline (about 95%) until P(general) reaches about 0.5, then drops.
    • With an empty instruction, steering one object feature shifts target-object choice by +9 to +21 out of 25.
    • Steering feature F586 closes the gripper (mean closure 0.65), where the prompt "close the gripper" gives 0.005.

2. Critique

  • The ablation result is confounded by how often and how strongly a feature fires. "General" features have high coverage and high activation by construction. "Memorized" features barely fire on held-out tasks, so removing them does almost nothing. There is no control that removes a matched amount of signal, such as a random direction or a frequently firing non-general feature. So Table 2 and Fig. 10 could partly just show that removing a heavily used direction breaks the policy.
  • The labels and the classifier partly check themselves. Manual labelling criterion 3 (Stage 3) already uses c, ō and ā, and the classifier is trained on the same metrics. So 100% leave-one-out accuracy on 30 labels is weak evidence.
  • The ablation doesn't quite match the SAE. The removal is y − (yᵀv)v on the raw residual stream. But the SAE subtracts a learned bias (b_pre) and normalizes each input, and its decoder directions are not orthogonal. The removal is also applied to every token and every denoising step.
  • Averaging over tokens washes out language. About 97% of the averaged vector comes from image tokens, so the features are mostly about the scene and the task phase. The authors' own per-token SAEs were less interpretable.
  • The steering comparisons are small and tilted toward the SAE.
    • The steering strength α is tuned per task.
    • The paper does not say how the object features (F1784, F8, F1727, F39) were chosen.
    • "Success" only means getting close enough to grasp.
    • The gripper test compares the best SAE feature against the average of 3 FFN neurons, with n = 5 each.
    • α is never stated relative to the residual stream's norm.
  • "The features emerge during fine-tuning" uses the wrong control. The comparison is with frozen SigLIP. The right baseline is the π0.5 base checkpoint before fine-tuning.
  • The authors admit the classifier mistakes rarity for memorization. One example is the lid-grasp feature F1381: it fires in 86% of lid episodes but is still labelled memorized. So the classifier ranks features; it does not split them into "memorizes" and "generalizes".
  • What is solid:
    • the LIBERO ablation curve, with the confound above;
    • SAEs trained from 6 different seeds recover the same top features (shown only visually);
    • the action expert's features follow the manipulation phases;
    • the recipe is cheap: averaged activations, a 1× dictionary, and no rollouts needed for the metrics.

3. Concepts explained to the user

  • Feature. A fixed direction v_i in a layer's activation space. The model is assumed to represent each timestep as a sparse sum x ≈ Σ z_i·v_i, with only about k terms nonzero. The coefficient z_i is that feature's activation. This is the superposition hypothesis.
  • Dictionary. The set of all v_i, which are the columns of W_dec. Think of it as a vocabulary: each activation vector is "spelled" with a few entries from it.
  • Learning. The encoder computes z = TopK(ReLU(W_enc·x)) and the decoder computes x̂ = W_dec·z. The loss is ‖x − x̂‖², with no labels. Sparsity plus a dictionary shared across all timesteps pushes the SAE to learn directions that recur.
  • One SAE learns all the features of one layer. Each layer gets its own SAE, and a feature index such as "F586" only means something within that SAE. Training a new seed or fine-tuning the policy reshuffles the indices.
  • Observation is not causation. A feature's meaning is assigned by a human who looks at its highest-activating frames. Whether pushing it changes behaviour has to be tested with rollouts. The paper's own limitations section says many clean features do not steer reliably, and F399 behaves in a way the label wouldn't predict: it causes early lift-off.

4. Suggested workflow for finding a feature that steers

  • Rank candidates from data. Use frames labelled with the target event. Rank features by how differently they fire at those frames than elsewhere. Optionally, rank by attribution instead: the gradient of the action output with respect to z_i, or single-feature patching on logged observations.
  • Screen offline. Replay recorded observations with +α·v_i added and measure how far the predicted action chunk moves, sweeping α. No rollouts are needed for this step.
  • Confirm in closed loop. Roll out only the few survivors. This is the only step that establishes a change in behaviour.
  • The framing given to the user: the SAE narrows the search to candidates, but it is not a control panel.

5. Idea, not adopted: diagnose R-056 with SAE features

The idea: compare SAE features before and after a fine-tune (base GR00T against the R-056 r16 checkpoint) on scene 1327 against the other scenes. The question is whether the grasp regression on 1327 shows up as a grasp-phase feature that disappeared, moved, or was replaced by a feature specific to that scene.

  • Key constraint: R-056 trained LoRA on the action head with the VLM frozen (see start-pose-retrain-pilot). On the same input, the backbone activations should therefore be identical before and after fine-tuning. The comparison only means something on the action-head (DiT) layers, or on backbone features driven by the different states the policy visits in closed loop. That second case is a confound, not a mechanism.
  • What it would need:
    • forward hooks on GR00T action-head blocks (a new adapter; no SAE code exists in vla_harness/);
    • activation collection on a fixed set of observations: the logged 1327 rollouts plus a nominal control, run through both checkpoints;
    • one SAE trained on the pooled activations, so both checkpoints share one dictionary;
    • the paper's four metrics, plus per-feature activation deltas around the grasp window.
  • Estimated cost (not measured):
    • collection is a few hours of forward passes on the 8 GB laptop GPU and has to share the flock;
    • SAE training at a 1× expansion ratio on averaged vectors takes minutes;
    • the main cost is the engineering for the hooks and adapter, about 1 day for impl.
  • What it would and wouldn't tell you:
    • it only produces candidates;
    • any causal claim needs the matched-ablation control from §2;
    • it is unvalidated on GR00T, since the paper only used π0.5 and OpenVLA.
  • Next to existing work: the existing post-mortem (docs/R056_POSTMORTEM.html) already gives behavioural causes: the gripper closes about 5.5 cm high, the dose was weak, and there is a coverage gap. This would be a mechanistic complement, not a replacement.
