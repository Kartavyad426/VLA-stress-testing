Direct answers
  • Mechanism. There are two stages, and neither uses failure data. (1) Score. Fit a small, separate network to the policy's own observation embeddings Ot (and, for some scorers, its action chunks At) on nominal data. It outputs one scalar per policy forward, and that scalar rises when the input is off-distribution. (2) Threshold. Functional conformal prediction over a few hundred successful rollouts gives a time-varying upper band. A rollout is flagged as failing at the first forward where its score crosses the band.
  • The score they recommend, logpZO, is one Euler step of a flow-matching model that maps Ot to Gaussian noise. The score is the squared norm of the noise it lands on: s = ‖O + fθ(O, 0)‖². This equals −2 log pZ(z) up to a constant. It is a density-style OOD score on Ot alone, and the code never passes actions to it (§3).
  • Can we use it with a checkpoint we will not retrain? Yes. The policy stays frozen, and their own pipeline loads latest.ckpt and only reads it. What you do train is the scorer, a separate small model. Three things are needed: access to the policy's internal embedding (we have this: vla_harness/capture/groot_features.py), a nominal dataset of Ot to fit the scorer, and roughly 20 to 1,000 successful rollouts to calibrate the band.
  • The catch for GR00T, from our own results. Which tensor you use as Ot matters a great deal. The one the action head actually conditions on (S2, vl_adapted) does not move under vision perturbations (R-037: 1.02×), while pre-adapter S1 does (1.37×). And in R-037 even S1 detected the perturbation without predicting failure within the vision arm. logpZO is an observation-only OOD score, so expect it to inherit that limit: it is an alarm for "off-distribution", not for "about to fail" (§11).

1. The problem, and the one design decision that shapes everything

An imitation-learning policy g(At | Ot) emits an action chunk At = (At|t, …, At+H−1|t) from an observation Ot. The goal is a runtime decision function D(τt) ∈ {0, 1} on the trajectory so far, which flags failure before the episode ends paper.

The design decision is to have no failure data at training or calibration time. Everything is fitted to successes and nominal demonstrations. That leaves two sub-problems:

  1. a scalar score DM(At, Ot; θ) that is small on the training distribution and large away from it; and
  2. a threshold that controls false alarms on successful rollouts, with a guarantee that does not depend on how the score is distributed.

The paper's bet is that "far from what the policy was trained on" is a good proxy for "about to fail", because an imitation policy has no competence outside its data.

2. What Ot and At actually are in their code

  • Ot is the policy's own conditioning vector, global_cond. That is the output of obs_encoder (ResNet features for each camera, concatenated with robot state) over n_obs_steps = 2 frames, flattened. For Square that is 274-d, and 548-d for Transport code train_diffusion_unet_hybrid_workspace_get_data.py:86-88.
  • At is the normalised demonstration action chunk, horizon 16 × action dim 10 (Square), flattened to 160 code :79, :83.
  • The scorer's training set is the policy's training demonstrations, not rollouts. save_data.py loads the checkpoint and walks train_dataloader with shuffle=False through the frozen ema_model code. So "nominal" here means "what the policy was trained on".
  • At runtime, Ot is the rollout's global_cond and At is the policy's sampled action_pred, scored once per forward, every 8 env steps (n_action_steps = 8) code robomimic_image_runner_FAIL-Detect.py:407-458.
  • A layout hack worth knowing about. The scorers reuse Diffusion Policy's 1-D temporal U-Net. To feed it a flat vector, adjust_xshape zero-pads 274 → 280 and reshapes to a fake sequence of 28 "tokens" of width 10, padded to a multiple of 4 code UQ_baselines/data_loader.py. The convolution runs across chunks of an embedding that have no temporal order. This is a convenience, not part of the method.

3. logpZO, the recommended score

3.1 Fit a flow from observations to noise

This is a standard rectified-flow / flow-matching objective, with data at s = 0 and noise at s = 1:

O[s] = O + s(Z − O),   Z ~ 𝒩(0, I),   s ~ U[0, 1](1)
ℒ(θ) = 𝔼O, Z, s ‖ fθ(O[s], s) − (Z − O) ‖²(2)
f
velocity network (unconditional 1-D U-Net, 256/512/1024)
O
observation embedding only; A is loaded and ignored

code UQ_baselines/logpZO/train.py: x0 = observation; x1 = randn; vtrue = x1 - x0; xnow = x0 + t*vtrue; loss = (vhat - vtrue)^2. Time enters as the integer long(100·s). It trains for 200 epochs, Adam 1e-4, batch 128.

3.2 Map a test observation to noise in one step

If the flow is straight (which is what the objective encourages), a single Euler step from s = 0 to s = 1 lands on the noise sample:

ZO = O + fθ(O, 0)(3)

If O is in distribution, ZO should look like a draw from 𝒩(0, I). If it is not, the network extrapolates and ZO lands somewhere unlikely, usually at a large norm.

3.3 The score, and where the name comes from

log pZ(ZO) = −d⁄2 log 2π − ½‖ZO‖²   ⟹   slogpZO(O) = ‖O + fθ(O, 0)‖²₂(4)

"logpZO" means log p of Z given O. It is the Gaussian log-likelihood of the latent code, not the likelihood of O. By change of variables the two differ by the Jacobian term:

log p(O) = log pZ(ZO) + log |det ∂ZO/∂O| = log pZ(ZO) + log |det(I + Jf)|(5)

logpZO simply drops the Jacobian term. That makes it cheap: one network call, and 0.033 to 0.04 s per step against STAC's 1.45 s, a 36 to 44× speedup paper. It also makes it the best-performing score in their benchmark (§9). The version that keeps the Jacobian term is logpO (§3.4). It exists in the code and lost.

# UQ_test/eval_load_baseline.py : logpZO_UQ   (action_pred is never passed at the call site)
pred_v = net(O, t=0)
Z      = O + pred_v
score  = Z.reshape(B, -1).pow(2).sum(-1)

The padded zero dimensions from adjust_xshape are included in the norm. Their target is 0, so they add only the network's residual there, which is small in distribution inferred.

3.4 logpO, the version with the Jacobian (a baseline in their table)

Because Jf is small for a near-identity map, they use log det(I + J) ≈ tr(J) and estimate the trace with a single-probe Hutchinson estimator and a finite difference:

tr(Jf) ≈ ε⊤ [ fθ(O + σε, 0) − fθ(O, 0) ] ⁄ σ,   σ = 10−3/√d,   ε ~ 𝒩(0, I) fixed(6)
slogpO(O) = −[ log 𝒩(ZO; 0, I) + tr(Jf) ](7)

code logpO_UQ. The score is negated in plot_with_CP_band.py so that higher means more anomalous. It reuses the logpZO network, so there is no separate training.

4. RND (random network distillation): the only one of the five that uses the action

There are two identical conditional 1-D U-Nets over the action chunk, each conditioned on O: a frozen random target fT and a trained predictor fθ. The predictor learns to imitate the target only on training pairs, so its error grows off-distribution:

ℒ(θ) = 𝔼(A, O) [ 1⁄H Σk=1..H ‖ fT(A, O)k − fθ(A, O)k ‖₂ ](8)
sRND(At, Ot) = Σk=1..H ‖ fT(At, Ot)k − fθ(At, Ot)k ‖₂(9)
k
index within the H = 16 action chunk; the norm is per step over the action width
At
train: demonstration chunk · test: the policy's own sampled action_pred

code UQ_baselines/RND/net.py: nn.PairwiseDistance(p=2) over the last axis, .mean() in training, .sum(dim=1) at test. The paper writes the objective with a squared norm; the code uses the un-squared L2 code. The difference is harmless for ranking.

Why it matters to us. RND scores the pair. It can fire when the observation looks normal but the sampled action is unusual for that observation. No O-only score can do that. In their table it is top-3 in 9/16 sim cases, against 14/16 for logpZO paper.

5. CFM (consistency flow matching): a curvature score

This is the same flow as logpZO, with a consistency penalty added to make the trajectories straight. The penalty asks that the predicted endpoint be the same from two nearby times:

Fs(x) = x + (1 − s) fθ(x, s),    ℒ = ℒFM + β ‖Fs(O[s]) − sg[Fs+δ(O[s+δ])]‖²,   δ = 5·10−3(10)

β is rescaled every batch so that β·ℒf = 0.01·ℒFM (both read as .item(), so β carries no gradient) code UQ_baselines/CFM/train.py. sg means stop-gradient.

The score integrates 5 Euler steps and measures how much the velocity changes along the path. On a well-learned, straight flow the velocity is constant, and off-distribution it bends:

sCFM(O) = 1⁄d Σj=1..d stdi=1..5 [ fθ(xi, si) ]j,   x1 = O,   xi+1 = xi − fθ(xi, si)/5,   s = 1, .8, .6, .4, .2(11)

code CFM_UQ. Oddity: the test-time integration puts O at s = 1 (the noise end) and steps toward s = 0, which is the reverse of the training convention in (1). A commented-out line in train.py shows the direction was once flipped. As a curvature probe it still yields a number, but it is not the path the model was trained on inferred. If we reimplement, integrate from s = 0 upward.

6. NatPN (natural posterior network): density in a latent space, with pseudo-labels

NatPN needs a classification or regression target, and there are no labels here. So they invent one: k-means with K = 64 clusters on O gives each training point a cluster id y code UQ_baselines/NatPN/train.py. Then:

z = encφ(O),   n(O) = NH · pψ(z),   αpost = αprior + n(O) · χφ(z)(12)
enc
"tabular" MLP encoder
pψ
16-layer normalising flow over z
χ
predicted class probabilities over the 64 clusters
α
Dirichlet concentration

Training minimises the Bayesian (expected-NLL plus entropy) loss of the Dirichlet posterior against the cluster labels. The evidence n can only be large where the flow density pψ(z) is high, so low density means low evidence, which means uncertain. The score used is the flow's log density, negated:

sNatPN(O) = −log pψ(encφ(O))(13)

code NatPN_UQ returns log_prob from posterior_update (the flow.forward(z) term). It is negated in plot_with_CP_band.py. The 64 k-means classes only shape the encoder; the score is a learned-latent density. Note that z is learned, so this density is on a space trained to separate clusters, not on O itself.

7. DER (multivariate deep evidential regression): epistemic variance of the action

This regresses the (normalised, flattened) action chunk Y ∈ ℝp, p = 160, from O, predicting the parameters of a Normal-Inverse-Wishart prior over the Gaussian's mean and covariance:

(μ, ν, κ, L) = head(U-Net(O)),   ν = softplus(·) + p + 1,   κ = softplus(·) + 1,   L lower-triangular with softplus diagonal(14)

Training uses the NIW negative log-likelihood (the marginal is a multivariate Student-t) with AdamW and a one-cycle schedule code DER/train.py, losses.py:NIW_NLL. The score is the trace of the epistemic covariance:

Σalea = LL⊤ ⁄ (ν − p − 1),    Σepi = Σalea ⁄ ν,    sDER(O) = tr Σepi(15)

code networks_chen.py:get_prediction. The code divides by ν. In the textbook NIW, the variance of the mean is E[Σ]/κ, so κ is what it should divide by, and κ is predicted but unused in the score. Treat (15) as "what they ran", not a canonical definition inferred. It uses only O as input. The action is the regression target, not an input.

8. The conformal band: from a score to a decision

All scorers share this stage, and it is the part with a guarantee. It is functional conformal prediction (Diquigiovanni et al., "The Importance of Being a Band", arXiv:2102.06746). The whole score trajectory is treated as one sample, so the guarantee covers every timestep at once, not each step separately.

8.1 Data

Run N rollouts in the nominal environment and keep the successful ones. Each one gives a score trajectory si = (si1, …, siT) of the same length T. Split them in the ratio num_train : num_cal = 300 : 700 into a shaping set 𝒜 (size N1) and a calibration set ℬ (size N2) code plot_with_CP_band.py, defaults --num_train 300 --num_cal 700.

8.2 Shape: a mean and a modulation, from 𝒜

μt = 1⁄N1 Σi∈𝒜 sit,    rit = |sit − μt|,    Ri = maxt rit(16)
γ = R(⌈(N1+1)(1−α)⌉),    mt = maxi∈𝒜 : Ri ≤ γ rit + 10−8(17)

In words, mt ("Tfunc" modulation) is the per-timestep envelope of the shaping residuals once the most extreme α-fraction of trajectories is dropped. It makes the band wide where successes are naturally noisy and narrow where they are consistent. That is the reason the band varies over time instead of being a flat line. If ⌈(N1+1)(1−α)⌉ > N1, all trajectories are kept code functional_predictor.py:_get_modulation_trajectory.

8.3 Calibrate: one number per calibration trajectory, from ℬ

Dj = maxt ( sjt − μt ) ⁄ mt,   j ∈ ℬ      h = Quantile1−α{Dj}(18)
uppert = μt + h · mt(19)

The max over t in (18) is what makes the band simultaneous. A calibration trajectory's "badness" is its worst-normalised excursion anywhere, so h is chosen so that only an α-fraction of successful trajectories ever touch the band.

8.4 Decide

D(τt) = 𝟙[ ∃ t′ ≤ t : st′ ≥ uppert′ ],    detection time = 8 · (first such t′)(20)

It fires once and latches. The metrics reported are TPR, TNR, balanced accuracy (TPR+TNR)/2, and the mean detection time over true positives code get_detection_with_plot, get_metric.

8.5 The guarantee, precisely, and what it is not

What it says. If a new successful rollout is exchangeable with the calibration successes (same environment, same policy, same distribution), then

P( st ≤ uppert for all t ) ≥ 1 − α   ⟺   false-alarm rate on nominal successes ≤ α(21)

What it does not say:

  • Nothing about detecting failures. TPR is purely empirical. The guarantee is one-sided and only about successes.
  • Nothing about successes in a shifted environment. Those are not exchangeable with the nominal calibration set. An OOD rollout that succeeds anyway is likely to be flagged, and the α bound does not apply to it. The paper lists this as a limitation for hardware paper.
  • The code's quantile is not the finite-sample one. Exact split CP takes the ⌈(N2+1)(1−α)⌉-th order statistic. The code uses np.quantile(·, 1−α), with the exact line commented out beside it code functional_predictor.py:get_one_sided_prediction_band. At N2 = 700 the difference is negligible. At our scale (N2 ≈ 20) it matters, so use the exact form. That form also requires N2 ≥ ⌈1/α⌉ − 1 (19 for α = 0.05) before the band is finite at all.
  • α is tuned per task in the code, not fixed at 0.05. For flow policies it is 0.025, or 0.01 on Transport; for diffusion policies 0.0125, or 0.05 on Transport code get_alpha(). Keep this in mind when reading their operating points.

10.2 The GR00T-specific decisions, in the order they bite

  1. Which tensor is Ot. The literal analogue of global_cond is what GR00T's action head conditions on, [pool(S2) ‖ S0e]. Our results say that is the wrong choice for vision. R-037 found S2 flat under camera, lighting and noise perturbations (median NN distance ratio 1.02×), because vl_self_attention removes the signal, while pre-adapter S1 moves 1.37× ours. The recommended Ot is [vl_encoder_img_mean (S1, image tokens) ‖ state_encoded (S0e)], 2048 + 1536 = 3,584-d. Keep the S2 variant as a control, because the result lives in the difference, as it did in R-037.
  2. Dimension and network. Their flow net sees 274-d through a U-Net hack. At 3,584-d with fp16 activations that can peak at 15k (S1 is pre-LayerNorm; R-036 fp16 overflow note), use this recipe: standardise per dimension in fp32, apply PCA to about 128–256, then use an MLP velocity net with time embedding for logpZO and CFM, and an MLP pair for RND inferred. None of these choices change the maths in §3–§5.
  3. What "nominal" data fits the scorer. The faithful choice is the policy's LIBERO-spatial fine-tuning demos, run once through the frozen backbone. This costs one backbone forward per frame and needs no simulator. The cheaper choice is successful nominal rollouts. Either way, the fitting set and the calibration set must be disjoint, or (18) is scored on points the scorer was fitted to. This is the same leave-one-out trap that produced a 4.9e7× artefact in R-036 ours.
  4. Cadence. One score per model forward, every n_action_steps = 16 env steps, so about 8 per episode. The band is indexed by forward index, not env step. Their resolution is every 8 steps, so ours is half as fine.
  5. Variable length. (16)–(18) need a common T. There are two options. (a) Run the nominal calibration episodes to a fixed horizon without success termination. That is closest to their protocol, but it changes what a "success trajectory" looks like after the task is done. (b) Pad each finished trajectory with its last score up to Tmax. That is cheap, but it makes the band's late section depend on episodes that already ended inferred. This choice is yours to make before any run.
  6. Labels come from the capture run. GR00T's denoise starts from an unseeded torch.randn, so outcomes do not reproduce across runs. Successes for calibration, and failures for testing, must come from the run that produced the scores ours (R-036 convention).
  7. Calibration size. α = 0.05 with the exact quantile needs N2 ≥ 19 successes, plus N1 for shaping. Their hardware setting (20–50) is the realistic target, so that means about 50–70 nominal successes for 𝒜 ∪ ℬ, plus the fitting set.

10.3 Cost, only where measured

  • Capture: 2.208 s per forward at k = 4 resampling, 26.3 KB per forward (R-036, measured) ours. FAIL-Detect needs k = 1, so it should cost less; that figure is not measured.
  • Scorer training: a small MLP on at most tens of thousands of 256-d vectors. This is CPU/GPU minutes in principle, not measured.
  • Runtime cost of logpZO: one small MLP call per forward, negligible next to GR00T's 2B backbone inferred.
  • A CPU-only dry run is possible today on R-037's stored npz files (all needed keys are present: vl_encoder_img_mean, state_encoded, action_pred). It would validate shapes and plumbing, but it is not a result: 163 nominal forwards cannot fit a 256-d density, and 20 episodes cannot be split into shaping and calibration sets.

11. What to expect, honestly

  • logpZO on GR00T is our R-037 nearest-neighbour detector with a better density model and a proper temporal threshold. Both are observation-only OOD scores on the same embeddings. R-037 found S1 detects vision perturbations (46.6% of forwards outside the nominal cloud against a 3.0% self-rate) but does not predict failure within them (pass/fail separation 0.00×, p = 0.53) ours. A sharper density does not obviously fix a signal that was not there. The functional band then adds exactly what R-036/R-037 lacked: a false-alarm rate with a guarantee, and a time-varying threshold.
  • The state pathway will likely dominate, as the paper warns. The paper's limitations section says learned scores "focus on simpler robot state information over visual features" paper. Our R-036 found the same: S0e separated at 12.95× and the VL taps were flat ours. R-036 also noted the causation caveat, that a failing episode reaches unusual states almost by definition. An S0e-driven alarm may fire after the failure has begun, which is detection, not prediction. Report detection time against failure onset, not only TPR.
  • RND is the only one of the five that can see "normal scene, wrong action". R-041's headline was "no boundary in the action; a wide one in the outcome" ours, so I would not expect much from the action side either. It is still the experiment that tests something R-036/R-037 did not.
  • Under the perturbations we care about, the guarantee covers less than it appears to. The α bound is for nominal successes. On LIBERO-Plus-style shifted scenes, where our failures live, successes are not exchangeable with the calibration set, so the false-alarm rate there is empirical.
