SIM fixes: how a sim that looked right turned out not to be, and how we found each fault

res31, 10-14 → 10-20 (updated 10-20 evening: D2, placement split, seed bug, yellow block, stacking, redaction), internal. Scenes rebuilt from single DROID episodes (FR3 + Robotiq 2F-85, exterior ZED 2 + wrist ZED Mini). Policy under test: GR00T-N1.7-DROID (internal research only; licence open). All numbers exploratory and UNVERIFIED (not through val). Commit hashes in brackets; pages linked at the end.

The short version. The sim passed every check we had: composites matched the real camera, objects settled, a scripted expert succeeded on 91/105 episodes. The first policy run failed, and taking it apart found four separate faults in the sim and one in the policy:
  1. Table and objects ~6 cm too high (orange scene; ~8 cm in c3). Fixed with stereo depth.
  2. Exterior camera pose off (~6–7 cm sideways at the objects). The old depth error had hidden it. Fixed with metric anchors from the wrist camera.
  3. Bowl built floating (dropped 2.2–2.6 cm on load). Code bug, fixed.
  4. Wrist-camera image misleads the policy: with our wrist render GR00T plans its grasp ~4 cm lower than with the real image, so it pushes its fingers into the table. Fully explained by G6: three things must match the real wrist camera: the table texture, the light/shadow on the table, and the gripper's look (with camera blur). With all three, GR00T plans exactly as on the real image. 10-20: a renderer that needs no real frame (wrist-video table, real gripper image, wrist-fitted light) recovers 2.6 of the 3.9 cm; ~1.3 cm is still open (G7, G8).
  5. Policy: GR00T releases the block ~13 cm short of the bowl, and does the same on real frames, so that one is the policy's own.
After fixes 1–3: GR00T went from "never grasps" (0 closes) to 4/33 successes on the orange scene, with 32/33 reaching and 17/33 closing at the block.

1 · The sim we thought was good (10-14 → 10-15)

From one DROID video per scene we built a MuJoCo scene: objects detected and masked in frame 0 (Grounding DINO + SAM), depth from a monocular model (MoGe-2) scaled by matching the robot's own depth, the exterior camera calibrated by fitting the sim robot's outline to the real robot, the real background composited behind the sim (LaMa-inpainted plate), and later a projectively textured wrist view. Tasks were generated automatically and solved by a scripted expert.

2 · How it kept breaking, and what isolated each fault

10-15
smoke1
First policy run fails. GR00T-DROID, "put the green block in the bowl": it reaches the right block in ~4 s, hovers 30 s, never closes (gripper closed 1.3% of steps) (4c56957).
Test: compare the hover with the expert's grasp and with the real operator's grasp, all in robot coordinates.
Found: GR00T commands its fingertips 4.5 cm below our table top (the arm is stopped ~1 cm in, 3.5 cm tracking error); the real operator's own grasp puts the fingertips 5.0 cm below our block's centre. An edge test (real fingertips must be above the real table) puts the real table ≥4.5 cm below ours. Either the table is too high or our gripper too long.
Built: the pre-deploy checklist, 23 checks (5db8dcf).
10-16
table vs gripper
Gripper length ruled out: sim finger pads overlay the real ones in the wrist video; a 4.5 cm length error would move them 144–184 px.
Table height measured independently with stereo (DROID ships rectified stereo video; baselines from the camera specs: ZED 2 120 mm, ZED Mini 63 mm): exterior stereo −0.086 m, wrist stereo (pose from the robot's joints, so independent of our calibration) −0.092…−0.095 m, our scene −0.029 m. Table ~6 cm too high (c3 ~8 cm) (2c7247a).
Cause: the monocular depth was scaled by matching the robot, then carried to frame 0 through table pixels; the result was ~15% short (scale 0.904 vs 1.055 from stereo). Fault 1.
10-16
stereo build
Fixing depth exposed a second fault. Scaling the depth from stereo fixed the vertical error (real grasp 5.0 → 0.0 cm) but the horizontal error went 0.7 → 6.6 cm. The old wrong scale had been compensating a wrong exterior camera pose sideways. Fault 2.
Two of my own bugs on the way: the first stereo build mixed stereo and monocular pixels (table only half fixed, blocks shrank), and a comparison overlay used the old scene's object positions.
10-16/19
anchored calibration
Metric anchors for the camera: block positions measured by the wrist camera's stereo (pose from the robot's joints) must project onto the blocks in the exterior image, and the stereo table must sit at the measured height. Anchor error 44.8 → 4.9 px, table error 1.5 → 0 cm, robot-outline IoU 0.488 → 0.495 (b783f5f).
Rebuilt orange scene (df2cc9f): table −0.094 m vs stereo −0.093; blocks 0.7–1.2 cm from the wrist anchors; real grasp vertical +1.4 cm, horizontal 1.7 cm (threshold 1.5: marginal); release inside the bowl; distance scale 0.907.
Bowl bug found by the settle check: the bowl's bottom was computed from box centre − largest half-size, ignoring rotation, so it was built floating. Fixed: 2.55 → 0.10 cm. Fault 3.
10-19
G1
Is our plumbing to the policy right? GR00T on the real episode's frames + recorded state: it predicts the close at frame 70 (operator 75) and the release at ~120 (operator ~126), path within 2–3.4 cm around the grasp. The interface (state, gripper convention, action decoding) works; the failure was in the sim (df2cc9f).
10-19
P6, G2
New rollout instrumentation: command tracking (flags an arm held back by something) and stage scoring (reached / closed / lifted / placed), regression-tested by replaying smoke1 exactly (18dcde5).
G2 in the rebuilt scene: GR00T now reaches, closes and lifts the instructed block (green and orange), but drops it short of the bowl (7469cb2).
10-19
release analysis, test A
Why it misses the bowl: GR00T itself opens the gripper ~13 cm from the bowl centre (rim 8.5 cm), 9–15 cm up. Ruled out: execution lag (commanded vs achieved 1–3 cm), block slip (none), a misplaced bowl in the images (sim vs real objects IoU 0.67–0.82, centres ≤0.3 cm), running the joint output instead of the hand output (both release as far) (816e510).
Test A on real frames: GR00T also plans to open early there, 5–23 cm from the bowl centre (mostly 11–17). Policy behaviour, not the sim. Fault 5.
10-19
slot A
All tasks on the orange scene (33 expert-solved variants): 4/33 success; reached 32, closed at the block 17, lifted 11, placed 4. The sim does not fail everywhere (2d749a3).
Found on the way: a do-nothing policy "solved" one variant (task true at the start); such variants are now excluded (fde6ff4).
New pattern: in 17/33 episodes GR00T pushes its fingertips ~2.6 cm into the table during grasp attempts; on real frames it never aims below +0.5 cm.
10-19
grasp compare
What differs in the approach (page linked below): on real frames GR00T plans like the operator, gripper nearly upright (5° vs 7°) and stopping above the table (≥ +1.9 cm; operator closed at +3.1). In sim it comes in tilted 12–20° and too low (−0.8…−1.1 cm at the close, commanded down to −5.4). Speed is not the difference (fae4e50).
10-19
G3
Image swap: same recorded state, different images. Real wrist image → plan +2.4 cm above the table, whether the exterior is real or ours. Our wrist render → −1.3/−1.4 cm, whether the exterior is real or ours. The state we compute in sim equals the recorded one exactly. The wrist image is the cause; geometry and state are not (fd011a2).
10-19
G4
Which part of the wrist image: swapping one part at a time. Real wrist + our gripper +1.3 cm (−1.1); real wrist + our blocks +1.9 (−0.5); our wrist + real gripper −1.2 (no recovery); no shadows −1.5; real block colours −2.0. Everything built on the real wrist background stays above the table; everything built on ours goes below. Main driver: the projected table texture (blurry, smeared at grazing angles, inpainted); the rendered gripper adds ~1 cm. Fault 4 (e6f2f5e).
10-19
texture source
Why the table texture was wrong: the wrist camera moves, so it has no background image of its own. We put texture on the 3D table and re-viewed it from the wrist, but ~95% of that texture came from the exterior camera, which sees the table from ~0.5 m at a shallow angle; stretched to the wrist's 20 cm close-up it is blurred and smeared. The exterior view itself is fine (G3): a fixed camera uses its own real pixels.
10-19
G5
Table texture from the wrist camera instead: a top-down mosaic of the table from the 36 real wrist frames before the first grasp (closest, most head-on samples; robot and object pixels masked; exposure-matched; holes filled from the mosaic itself). 26 s per scene, once; no extra cost per frame. Ghosts of the real blocks and edge speckles found by eye and fixed.
Result: our render with the live real table pixels plans at +1.0 cm (table confirmed: 2.5 of the 3.9 cm gap); with the new texture −0.7 cm (only 0.8 cm recovered). A sharp but static texture is not enough (250eeae).
10-19
camera check
Our sim camera is an ideal pinhole; the real ZED Mini is not. Measured at GR00T's 320×180 input: our gripper outline is ~6× sharper than real (65 vs 10: the real gripper is out of focus and the real frame is shrunk by area-averaging; we render directly at 320×180 with hard edges). Inside the gripper ours is too flat (2.8 vs 4.5); our table texture is softer than real (3.0 vs 4.8). Also unmodelled: motion blur, H.264 compression, auto-exposure, sensor noise. The real gripper is warm brownish-black with silver bolt heads; ours is neutral grey-black and flat.

S1
A renderer that needs no real frame (WristRenderV2, opt-in): renders the wrist view at 640×360 with the wrist-video table texture, replaces our gripper with the real open gripper cut from the wrist video (rigid w.r.t. the camera: the median over open frames is a clean gripper picture; closed openings rejected, held objects baked in), keeps our blurred gripper when closing, and shrinks to 320×180 like real frames (2b8c37e).

S3/S4
The light/shadow factor, measured: exposure and vignetting negligible; the real robot shadow is 20–25% darker and converges on the gripper as it descends. A first reading ("shadow on the wrong side") was wrong, a real black bowl counted as shadow; corrected with object-aware masks. Light refitted from the wrist view (near overhead, darker) and used for the wrist render only (54bafb0, e70feeb).

yellow block
GR00T closes on air next to it: 2.4–3.9 cm from its centre (the expert: 0.3–0.5 cm, 38 N grip). Our "closed at the block" score had counted those as closes (3 cm radius); now it needs both pads on the block for 0.5 s. 10-20
stacking
Clean drops stay: green dropped on orange from up to 6.5 cm and 1 cm off-centre stays every time, so the drop physics is fine. In the failed stacks the block was already tumbling at release (18–64 cm/s, spinning 50–940°/s, tilted 17–169°) while the gripper moved slowly: it rotates out of the grasp during the carry. Our finger pads use Menagerie defaults (sliding friction 0.6–0.7, no twist resistance, pyramidal cone, impratio 1). Replaying the recorded actions with firmer grip physics is not a valid test (the actions were reactions to the old physics); that needs a closed-loop batch (38cd20e).
10-23
(a) firm grip
Closed-loop firm pad contact (pads condim 4 + twist friction 0.02; scene-wide elliptic cone + impratio 10), 33 variants on D2's exact layouts (new --layouts_from; checked by replay, 0.0 m): success 9 → 13 (paired p = 0.29), lifted 24 → 29 (p = 0.23); bowl tasks 5/14 → 10/14; stacking still 0/10. Correction: the pads' "0.6–0.7 sliding friction" above was not in effect: MuJoCo uses the larger of the pair, and table and blocks are 1.0 (44c14d8).

Grip is not the main stacking failure. At the gripper opening (replay_probe.py --mode stack, D2 + firm + yfit vs the scripted expert's 11 successes): GR00T releases 0.5–3.5 cm off-centre (expert ≤ 0.6), at the same height; turning just before release overlaps with the expert's for green on orange (17–181 vs 12–130 °/s). Green-on-orange offsets point the same way in 9 of 10 (+y): a systematic aim error, policy or our images not separated.

review pages
The user reviews the scene by eye. New pages (runs/real2sim/review/index.html): the real episode replayed in our scene, real | ours | real with our outlines at 1280×720, absolute and relative distances, and an issue list (issues.json); rollout pages with what GR00T saw at each key moment. 10-23
no-shadow run
Does the image swap predict closed-loop behaviour? G7 had GR00T plan ~0.5 cm lower without our shadows. 10 bowl episodes without shadows vs D2: lifted 10 → 9, success 5 → 6, approach time with fingertips commanded below the table 29% → 41% (8 of 10), lift 9.9 → 12.2 s. The direction matches; outcomes don't move at n = 10. Also corrected the shadow plan (5b): G7's real-shading upper bound (+1.1 vs ours +1.2) says shadows are not the remaining 1.3 cm.
10-23
wrist blur bug
Mine. V2's closed-gripper focus blur used "closer than 20 cm" instead of "is the gripper": near the table that is the whole image, so every GR00T batch up to 10-23 (D2, firm, yfit, no-shadow) saw a blurred wrist image at each grasp and place. At 320×180, sharpness away from the gripper at the grasp: old 3, fixed 174–186, real 63–65. Fixed (gripper pixels only), checked by the user, default from 10-23 (--wrist_blur gripper); the orange batch re-run with it (33 variants, D2 layouts, 10-26): no measurable change (success 9 → 9, 4 flips each way, p 1.0; lifted 24 → 25; blocked 2 → 4, p 0.69). The blur did not drive the earlier results; the D2/firm/yfit/no-shadow numbers stand, though they were measured with the blurred view.

3 · Why the sim could not have been accurate from the start

  1. We validated geometry from the camera it was estimated from. That camera is blind to errors along its own rays; a 6 cm table offset looked perfect. Rule now: check from a second viewpoint (wrist camera, pose from the robot's joints) and against real robot coordinates (recorded grasps) — scene_checks.py.
  2. Metric scale came from the robot, carried through monocular depth. Monocular depth error is not one global scale (it varied 0.91–1.06 across depth bands); real stereo was available in DROID all along.
  3. The camera pose came from the robot outline alone. An outline fit leaves the pose loose along the viewing direction; it needed metric anchors.
  4. The scripted expert was our success signal, but it uses the sim's exact object positions and no images, so it cannot catch a geometry or visual error.
  5. We judged visuals by eye. A wrist image that looked plausible to us moves the policy's plan by ~4 cm. Only swapping images at a fixed state (G3/G4) measures this.
  6. The wrist view has no real background of its own. The camera moves, so we projected the exterior camera's background onto the table; the exterior camera sees the table at a grazing angle, so the texture is blurry where the wrist camera looks closest.
  7. The sim camera is an ideal pinhole: no focus blur, no motion blur, no compression, no auto-exposure, and rendered straight at the policy's 320×180 instead of shrunk from 1280×720 like the real frames. Gripper edges came out ~6× too sharp (G6: camera effects + gripper look worth ~1–1.3 cm).
  8. The robot is a sim render (Menagerie FR3 + Robotiq 2F-85: same kinematics as the real robot, verified to 0 mm, but simplified meshes and flat materials); it costs ~1 cm in G4.
  9. Not yet addressed: the controller (MuJoCo position actuators, not DROID's controller; never system-identified), the ±90° mount sign, and no real-robot reference for this checkpoint on these scenes. The eval-validity review (re412) says real-to-sim ranks policies only with matched visuals including the wrist view and robot, a matched controller and matched layouts; our scenes do not meet that yet.
