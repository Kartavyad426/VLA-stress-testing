Reference · R-042 · start-pose perturbations

How the wrist camera was found

The intervention, the arithmetic, the ten numbers it rests on, and the other ways the same claim could be checked, with which of them have actually been run.

2026-09-23RESULTS.md R-039, R-042general splice maths

1 · The claim

What is being asserted, and what is not

When the robot starts from a perturbed joint configuration, the change in the policy's first action is carried by what the wrist camera sees, not by the eight-number proprioception and not by the third-person view of the arm. The claim is about which input the action head reads the pose from. It is not a claim about what the vision encoder represents, nor about later forwards, nor about any checkpoint other than this one.

2 · The intervention

Hybrid observations through the whole stack

A start-pose instance has a perturbed run p and a control run n of the same scene under the same seed and the same fixed noise. At forward 0 both are at their reset state, so the comparison is exact. Write V for the vision-language backbone followed by the adapter, E for the state encoder, h for the action head, and an observation as (agent image, wrist image, state).

The two per-camera arms
aP = h( V(agentp, wristp), E(statep); ε ) aN = h( V(agentn, wristn), E(staten); ε ) aA = h( V(agentn, wristp), E(statep); ε )    aW = h( V(agentp, wristn), E(statep); ε ) The nominal pixels of one camera are put in before the backbone, so everything downstream, including the text positions that R-039 showed carry image content, is recomputed. The other camera and the state stay perturbed. This is an interchange intervention on one input at the pixel level (Geiger et al. 2021), run as denoising (Heimersheim & Nanda 2024).
Transfer, as in R-039
τX = 1 − ‖aX − aN‖ / ‖aP − aN‖,   X ∈ {A, W} The share of the perturbed-to-nominal gap in the 280-number action chunk that restoring one camera alone closes. The control of the state token, r0 = 0.10 (R-039), is the third leg: restoring the state alone closes 4 percent, deleting it changes the action by 10 percent of the gap.

4 · Why this is causal and not a correlation

What rules out the alternatives

  • "The wrist image simply changes more under a pose change, so of course restoring it does more." Magnitude of the pixel change is not what τ measures. τ measures how much the action moves when that input is set to nominal with the rest held. A large pixel change that the head ignored would give τ ≈ 0. And A is the counter-case: the agent-view image also changes, and restoring it moves the action 17 percent.
  • "The proprioception carries it and the cameras are incidental." S = 0.04 with the state restored alone, and deleting the state token outright changes the action by 10 percent of the gap. The training config's state dropout of 0.2 is the mechanism.
  • "It is an artefact of the hybrid observation." The same hybrid built on camera-viewpoint and sensor-noise instances, where only the agent view is perturbed by construction, gave W = 0.00 ± 0.02 on all twenty and A = 0.95 to 0.99. The wrist arm reads zero exactly when it should.
  • "Noise." The five arms share one seeded ε per forward; the perturbed and nominal chunks replicate R-039's to 0.03; the per-instance spread of W is 0.66 to 0.92.

6 · Scope

What it does not say

Ten instances, one seed each, one checkpoint, forward 0 exact and later forwards a bound. Two instances (430, 485) fall to 0.25 and −0.19 on W at their anchor forward while IT stays above 0.9, so later in those episodes the wrist is no longer the whole story and the recorded source is off-manifold; those are reported as bounds, not results. The claim is about where the head reads its pose at the first decision, which is the decision the data specification has to cover.

7 · Reading the number

What W is, exactly

Operator order
W = 1 − ( ‖aW − aN‖ / ‖aP − aN‖ ) The division happens first, then the subtraction from 1. The denominator belongs to the fraction alone. Task 468: 0.63 / 5.21 = 0.12, then 1 − 0.12 = 0.88. The other bracketing, (1 − 0.63) / 5.21 = 0.07, is not what the tables show.
  • Not a cosine. Both norms are Euclidean distances on the 280-number chunk (40 steps × 7 live action dims) in the policy's normalised action units. Cosine would ignore magnitude, and for delta-pose commands magnitude is most of the content; that is why the published activation-injection paper's cosine numbers read 0.99 where ours read 0.88 for comparable effects.
  • Not rescaled to 0–1. Bounded above by 1 (a distance cannot be negative) and unbounded below. W = 1: the wrist-restored chunk lands on the nominal chunk. W = 0: no closer to nominal than the fully perturbed chunk. W < 0: restoring the wrist moved the action further away, which a few rows show at −0.03 and −0.19.
  • Higher is closer to nominal.
  • Units. Normalised action space, the units the head is trained in. A gap of 4.0 for a start pose and 0.3 for lighting are comparable to each other but do not convert to centimetres.

8 · What follows for data

More pairs, and what to retrain

The product of this line of work is a map from perturbation to the input that carries it, and from that input to a data specification. Four LIBERO-Plus categories are mapped. The pairs still worth finding, in order of value:

  • The three categories not yet run: object layout, background texture, language. Language is the only one where the text arm would be a finding rather than leakage (R-038).
  • Within the wrist frame for start poses: the gripper's own appearance versus the near scene seen from the new pose. This decides whether demonstrations need the gripper visible or scene coverage.
  • The reverse-direction check (section 5), to rule out the pose being readable from either camera.
  • Lighting, the one interaction, where neither camera alone explains the effect.

Two corrections to the natural reading of the result. First, the finding is not about perturbations of the wrist camera. The perturbation is the robot's start pose in joint space; the policy reads that pose through the wrist camera. So the data needed is demonstrations that start from those poses, and the wrist view they contain is what makes them work. Rendering only the third-person view at new poses would reach 17 percent of the effect.

Second, what the pixel swap does and does not say about the VLM. Corrected 2026-09-24. Restoring the nominal pixels before the vision-language model gives back the nominal action, which proves the VLM and adapter are in the causal path and transmit the difference between the two images into the head. It does not show that the VLM's representation of the new pose is adequate. Two stories give identical numbers: the VLM encodes the new pose correctly and the head has never learned the action for that encoding; or the VLM encodes the new pose poorly, because those views lie outside what it was tuned on, and the head is handed a representation it cannot use. The swap cannot separate them. What is established is only that the failure enters through the images, not the state pathway.

What would separate them: (a) a pose probe on the adapter output, fitting a linear decoder from features at forward 0 to the simulator's true end-effector pose on canonical starts and testing it on perturbed starts; equal decodability puts the gap in the head, decodability falling with radius puts part of it in the VLM; about a hundred forwards, no rollouts. (b) R-040's frozen-versus-unfrozen arm, already in the design: if training only the head on new-pose demonstrations closes the gap, the representation was sufficient. Until one of these runs, training with the VLM frozen is a default justified by cost and risk (half the parameters, the pretrained grounding, no room for gradients on this GPU), not a conclusion from this evidence.

What the three establish together

The wrist claim survives all three. It is necessary (nothing else reproduces the effect), sufficient at the action level (0.88, stable to 0.15 across noise seeds, wrist above agent view on 30 of 30 instance-seeds), and sufficient at the outcome level for 4 of 7 failures with one corrected chunk. Sharpened wording: at a new start pose the policy reads where it is from the wrist camera, checks it against the agent view, and commits in the first chunk. Demonstrations must contain both views from the new pose, and the approach phase, roughly the first second, is what they must cover. Full scoring in RESULTS.md R-044; run metadata under runs/r044_*.

Not run: a second checkpoint (whether this is the checkpoint's habit or the architecture's), and the per-region split inside the wrist frame.
