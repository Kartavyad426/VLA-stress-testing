GEN-1.5: one-shot learning, and what it means for us

2026-09-23 · Source: Generalist AI blog, "GEN-1.5: Embodied Foundation Models are One-Shot Learners" (2026-08-19), its predecessors, and the papers it cites · Compared against this repo's GR00T N1.7 / LIBERO-Plus work (R-026 to R-042)
Summary
  • What it is. GEN-1.5 is a closed, unreleased robot foundation model. You give it a 3 to 12 second demonstration of a new task in its context window, and it does the task with no weight updates. Across 10 short tasks it averages 59% success. With 1 to 10 gradient steps on 1 to 5 minutes of demos it reaches 83%.
  • Imitation or generalisation? It infers the task, not the exact trajectory. It runs closed-loop from what the cameras currently see, so object positions, object sizes and which hand it uses can differ from the demo. The strong generalisation examples (new tools, obstacles, unseen jars) come from the fine-tuned models, not the in-context ones. Generalist itself calls the in-context skills "more brittle".
  • Static or retrained? There are three regimes. In-context: weights frozen. Few-step: 1 to 10 gradient steps that change the weights by less than 0.15%. Full post-training: GEN-1-style, about 1 hour of robot data plus RL, for 99%+ success. The one-shot headline refers to the first regime.
  • How they made it work. By their own account, scale alone: more than 500k hours of real human-worn-gripper interaction data, models above ~7B parameters, and 30 s of video memory. They did no meta-learning and added no in-context-learning objective. The ability appeared during 8+ months of continued pretraining.
  • Relevance to us. The thesis matches ours: failures are coverage gaps, and a broad enough prior makes closing them cheap. We cannot replicate in-context learning with GR00T N1.7 as it stands. Our checkpoint sees one frame (n_obs_steps: 1), so there is no place to put a demo. We can run the few-step analogue now as an add-on to R-040. RoboTTT from NVIDIA (built on GR00T N1.7) and BPP from Stanford (open code, with a LIBERO-based benchmark) are the realistic paths to a true in-context version.

  • Model. A "large multimodal model". It takes video with 30 s of memory, plus other sensors, language and proprioception, and outputs 100 Hz action trajectories. Architecture and parameter count are not disclosed. GEN-0 was scaled to 10B+ parameters.
  • Data. Real physical interaction only, from homes, warehouses and factories. The data is mostly recorded by people using handheld grippers, i.e. wearable UMI-style devices. GEN-1 reported "over half a million hours" of this data and "no robot data" in pretraining. GEN-1.5 has no simulation data. It was pretrained on "randomly sampled continuous spans" with no special packing of demo-then-execute examples.
  • Tasks. Short-horizon atomic tasks: unzipping a pencil pouch, twisting a lid off a jar, brushing a cube into a bowl, removing a vacuum pad, taking money out of a wallet, putting a marker into a cup, pouring bolts.
  • Other claimed capabilities. Chaining two independently recorded prompts into one behaviour. Zero-shot sim-to-real: a demo recorded in simulation prompts the real robot. Human-hand-to-robot imitation "in some cases".

2. How the one-shot learning works

Mechanism: "physical prompting"

It works the way GPT-3's one-shot prompting does. A demonstration is placed in the model's context window as sensor data plus action trajectory, and the rest of the window holds the robot's live observations. The model then keeps predicting actions. Because the prompt has just shown a task, the most likely continuation is to do that task again in the current scene.

physical prompt: 3–12 s demo (obs + actions)
rolling live observations
now → act
t − 30 s30 s context windowt

The demo is selected in a drag-and-drop UI and inserted into the buffer. The join between demo and live stream is a discontinuous jump in time, which the model never saw in pretraining. It works anyway.

Why it emerges, according to Generalist

  • They did not train for it. The post rules out architectural changes, a meta-learning inner or outer loop, and auxiliary objectives.
  • Hypothesis 1: data distribution. Physical data may be "bursty" and Zipfian, the same distributional properties that Chan et al. 2022 linked to in-context learning emerging in transformers.
  • Hypothesis 2: repetition. Physical work has repeating cycles, such as the same pick done over and over within one 30 s span. A long-context model learns to "detect and extend" the pattern, as LLMs do (Mirchandani et al. 2023). Read this way, "do what the last few seconds showed, but in the current scene" is an ordinary next-action prediction.
  • Precondition: scale. GEN-0 found "ossification" below ~7B parameters: small models stop absorbing pretraining data. The few-step ability was seen to get stronger as pretraining went on, from hundreds of steps to tens to 1. Only after that did they try zero steps.

What the blog does not show is an ablation that would confirm either hypothesis. Both are offered as guesses.

Answer: it is not a trajectory replay. The policy is closed-loop at 100 Hz on live camera input, so it has to re-ground the task in whatever scene it is in. The demo tells it what to do. The pretrained prior supplies how. The tasks are atomic, though, and the post never states how different the test scene is from the demo scene. The claims about strategy-level generalisation (new tools, obstacles) belong to the fine-tuned models. The honest reading is "the task generalises to nearby variations, and the in-context version is fragile". It does not show open-ended generalisation from one demo.

Proposed: R-040 extension, a gradient-step response curve

  1. Take one R-026 failure bucket with enough instances. Robot-initial-state is the largest.
  2. Collect k ∈ {1, 5, 10, 50} corrective demos on mined failed instances (arm D data).
  3. Fine-tune from the released checkpoint for s ∈ {1, 10, 100, 1000} steps with the R-040 recipe. Record ‖Δθ‖/‖θ‖ for each run.
  4. Evaluate on (a) the exact failed instances, (b) held-out instances of the same category, and (c) the R-029 nominal control for regression. Use ≥50 rollouts per cell, given R-039's 23/30 agreement.
  5. Pre-registered prediction: GR00T N1.7-LIBERO is far below GEN-1.5's scale, so s ≤ 10 will not move (b) outside noise, and the curve will look like an ordinary fine-tune. If s ≤ 10 does move (b), GR00T's pretrained prior already covers the gap, and the manifest's cost estimates should shrink sharply.

Cost: 16 fine-tunes, each tiny except s = 1000, plus 16 × 3 evaluation sets. This fits the current GPU queue (flock + announce).

8. Caveats

  • This is a blog post, not a paper. The architecture, trial counts, per-task results, success criteria and test-scene protocol are not given, and nothing is compared against π0, GR00T or anything else. The "±10%" is a standard deviation across tasks, not a confidence interval.
  • "Tasks not engineered into pretraining" was checked only by a nearest-neighbour language search over 1.89M scenes. A similar motion could still be in the corpus.
  • Our rule is that a policy stays on the roster only if it reproduces its published number. GEN-1.5 cannot be checked against that rule, so treat its figures as directional.
  • Human-to-robot transfer works only "in some cases", and no numbers are given.
Part II · Follow-up Q&A · 2026-09-23

Getting in-context prompting into our stack: BPP, RoboTTT, and how VLA "context" works

This part answers the follow-up questions from the same session. BPP facts come from its paper (arXiv:2606.30457) and from reading its code at real-stanford/behavior_prompting@main (pushed 2026-09-01). GR00T facts come from our checkpoint's config.json.

II.1 Is GEN-1.5's in-context learning really emergent?

It is emergent in the sense that nobody trained for it. The only objective was next-action prediction on random 30 s spans. There were no demo-then-execute training pairs, no meta-learning and no auxiliary loss. Three qualifications:

  • The data probably teaches it implicitly. Physical work repeats within 30 s (the same pick, again and again). So "continue the pattern from the last few seconds in the current scene" is already rewarded by next-action prediction. This is the same data-driven mechanism as in LLMs (Chan et al. 2022). It is not magic.
  • No emergence curve is shown. There is no plot of one-shot success against model size, data or compute. They also mention undisclosed "surgical architectural and algorithmic changes" made during training.
  • The contrast with other work is real. BPP and RoboTTT get prompting by training for it explicitly. GEN-1.5 is the first to claim it without doing so, across a broad range of tasks.

What this means for us: if the ability comes from scale, breadth and long context, it cannot be added to GR00T N1.7 afterwards. The workable route is to train for it explicitly, which is what BPP does.

II.3 Will BPP survive perturbations if it gets a good prompt?

Prediction (not yet tested)

Probably not for camera or robot-initial-state failures. Possibly yes for language and task-ambiguity failures.

  • A prompt specifies the task. It does not fix perception. R-039 found every perturbation category reaches the action through image tokens, meaning the policy misreads the current scene. A demo tells the policy what to do, but it still has to see a shifted camera correctly.
  • A nominal prompt may even hurt. BPP's attention works as a "prompt lookup", matching current frames to milestones in the demo. A clean-camera demo won't match a rotated live view.
  • No robustness evidence. BPP is ~208M parameters trained only on LIBERO. The paper reports no camera, lighting, distractor or initial-pose experiments, and lists "occasionally completes a different task than instructed" as a failure mode.
  • Where it could help. Language perturbations and R-038's empty prompt: the demo replaces the text, so these vanish by construction. The drawer/bowl wrong-object failure: the demo shows which bowl. Initial state is uncertain: BPP uses end-effector pose with a 2-step history plus the wrist camera, so its sensitivity may differ from GR00T's, which ignores its state input.

Proposed experiment: clean prompt vs matched prompt

ArmConditioningQuestion it answers
1libero_language.ckptBaseline with the same architecture and data

3BPP, matched prompt (a successful demo recorded under the same perturbation)Does one demo from the new condition adapt the policy with no training? This is GEN-1.5 in miniature.

Matched demos are cheap: 468 of R-026's 623 hard variants were GR00T successes. Their actions convert mechanically to BPP's format. If 3 beats 2 on held-out instances, in-context adaptation works at this scale and competes directly with R-040's fine-tuning arms. If 3 ≈ 2 ≈ 1, prompting only specifies the task, and our failures are perception gaps that need data.

Checkpoint choice: austinpatel/libero, both the prompting and language files (~8.5 GB).

  • It is trained on all LIBERO suites, including libero_spatial, the suite LIBERO-Plus perturbs.
  • The two files use the same architecture and data and differ only in conditioning, so the comparison between them is clean.
  • It has a published number to reproduce first.

The LIBERO-Gen checkpoints test generalisation to unseen task combinations, not perturbation robustness. They are for a later phase.

II.4 Do VLAs normally have context? Does GR00T?

ModelWhat it sees on each call
GR00T N1.7 (our checkpoint)The current frame per camera, 8-D state and the instruction. n_obs_steps: 1: no past frames. It predicts a 16-step action chunk.

BPP2 current frames (img_obs_horizon: 2) plus a separate prompt demo
RoboTTTUp to 8K timesteps (~4.5 min), compressed into fast weights
GEN-1.530 s of video plus sensors
  • Two meanings of "context". Within one call, GR00T's image, text and state tokens sit in one sequence and attend to each other. That is spatial context. Temporal context means frames from earlier moments are in the sequence too. GR00T has none, so it has no memory of two seconds ago.
  • Action chunking is not memory. Executing 16 predicted actions before looking again is an open-loop commitment. The model does not remember anything.
  • How temporal context works. Each past frame goes through the vision encoder and becomes a block of tokens. The blocks are stacked in time order with time or position embeddings, and the transformer attends across them, so the action can depend on "what I saw 3 s ago". A demo in context is the same idea: tokens from another episode, placed where the model can attend to them.
  • Why most VLAs skip it: cost. Every frame adds hundreds of tokens per camera, and attention is quadratic in sequence length. Every long-context model has a workaround. GEN-1.5 pays for it at scale. RoboTTT compresses history into weights. BPP subsamples the demo to 1 Hz, encodes it once and caches it.

II.5 How BPP takes a demo, in code

You don't pass a link or a video file. The prompt is a dictionary of tensors: images, proprioception and actions, cut into 1-second chunks. Source: train_network/model/prompt/prompt_obs_encoder.py (PairPromptTransformerTokenizer, PairPromptObsEncoder) and train_network/env_runner/libero_image_runner.py.

prompt = {
  "obs": {
    "agentview_rgb":   [L, 3, 224, 224],   # one frame per second (20 Hz data, chunk of 20)
    "eye_in_hand_rgb": [L, 3, 224, 224],
    "ee_pos":  [L, 3],  "ee_ori": [L, 6],  # rot6d
    "gripper_states": [L, 2],
  },
  "action": [L, 20, 10],                   # all actions kept: 20 per chunk × (pos3 + rot6 + grip1)
}                                          # L = demo length in seconds

policy.reset()
policy.prompt(prompt)                      # encoded ONCE -> self.prompt_tokens_cache
while not done:
    action = policy.predict_action(current_obs)   # current 2 frames only
Data flow
  1. prompt(), called once. Each 1 s chunk produces image tokens (both cameras), proprio tokens and one action token (a linear projection of the chunk's 20 actions). The chunks are flattened into one sequence, given position embeddings (capped by prompt_sequence_length: 768), encoded by the 6-layer prompt transformer, and cached.
  2. Every control step. Tokens from the current 2-frame observation cross-attend to the cached prompt tokens, which is the "where am I in the demo" lookup; attention-sink tokens are prepended. The output is concatenated with the raw current-observation tokens and passed to the diffusion U-Net, which outputs actions.
  3. reset() clears the cache. A new demo is just another prompt() call. No gradient steps are involved.

In their own evaluation, the runner builds the prompt from a LIBERO .hdf5 demo of the same task via LiberoReplayImageDataset(..., only_prompt=True), from a different initial state than the test episode. In pair mode each parallel environment gets its own prompt.
