0 · Verdict

  1. STAC was aimed at the wrong kind of failure. Sentinel splits failures into erratic (STAC) and no progress (a VLM). All 70 C1 failures are no-progress timeouts: slow, smooth and wrong.
  2. On GR00T, STAC mostly measures how fast the arm is moving (rank correlation 0.77). Failures command smaller moves, so they look "more consistent". Within bands of similar motion size, the inversion mostly disappears.
  3. Draw diversity isn't uncertainty: the 32 draws agree on the executed 16 steps (std 0.014) and spread out only in the unexecuted tail (0.33). Nothing in flow-matching training makes draws spread out on unfamiliar states.
  4. The as-published cumulative detector reads episode length (shuffled control 0.99), because every failure runs to the 18-forward timeout.
  5. Progress toward the object separates early (AUROC 0.91 as a success signal by forward 4, and within every radius band). It is the first detector to test, with start-joint distance from home (0.72) as the baseline to beat.

3 · Q2: why the draws agree on failures

3a · Draw diversity measures demo randomness, not unfamiliarity res-descriptive

  • Flow matching learns a velocity field whose samples reproduce the spread of actions in the demos for similar observations. Its loss says nothing about what to do away from the demos, so there is no term that makes samples spread out on unfamiliar inputs. Networks extrapolate smoothly and confidently there.
  • Measured: the median per-step std across the 32 draws is 0.014 on the executed steps 0–16 and 0.33 on steps 16–40, 24× larger. The model is near-deterministic about what it will do next, in successes and failures alike.
  • Absolute Spread is about 7.7 for every episode (median 7.71 failure vs 7.75 success at f0). It reflects the noise floor of the tail, not the state.

3b · STAC tracks motion size; stalling looks consistent res-descriptive

  • STAC vs commanded action magnitude over the overlap: Spearman ρ = 0.77 (480 episode-forward rows, f0 and f1 pooled). With a fixed RBF bandwidth (σ 8.17), smaller actions mean smaller distances, which means smaller MMD.
  • Failures command smaller translations at f1: median 0.71 vs 0.96 (AUROC failure-high 0.15).
  • Within thirds of motion size, STAC's AUROC is 0.51 / 0.60 / 0.37: the strong inversion (0.18 at f0) mostly disappears. What's left is noise around 0.5, not a hidden detector.
  • A slow or stalled arm changes the observation little between forwards, so consecutive chunks are predicted from nearly the same input and agree. A fixed point is perfectly self-consistent.

3c · Why an OOD start reads as in-distribution hypothesis

  1. State aliasing. The perturbed pose produces a wrist view and an agent view that the vision encoder maps close to some familiar state (a different phase or a different reach). The action head then executes, confidently, the action for that familiar state. R-042 (the wrist carries 0.88 of the effect) fits this: the wrist view is what gets misread.
  2. Proprio is underused. GR00T takes observation.state through a small encoder, but in LIBERO demos the start pose barely varies, so the model can't have learned to rely on it. An off-support joint vector is extrapolated linearly and outweighed by the vision tokens.
  3. Early commitment. The episode is decided at forward 1 (R-057). Once the first chunk heads the wrong way, the next observations are self-generated and consistent with that choice: smooth and wrong.

Tests for 3c (cheap first): (i) nearest-neighbour check of the wrist-image embedding at forward 0 against demo frames: do failures' nearest demo frames come from a different phase or position than their true one? (one GPU forward per frame, or cached embeddings if they exist); (ii) proprio ablation: replace observation.state with the nominal start state at forward 0 and see whether chunk 0 changes. If it barely moves, proprio is being ignored (GPU, about 60 forwards).

5 · Caveats

  • R-059 Part C has no val PASS yet; neither do the res-descriptive numbers.
  • C1 rollouts are B = 32 rollouts: draw 0 of a batched call differs from a B = 1 run by about one bf16 step at f0, and compounds (NOTES.md, known limit 2). The bit-identity control failed (known limit 1).
  • The motion-tertile analysis pools f0 and f1 rows, so episodes count twice; treat it as descriptive.
  • Progress uses the nearest object, not the instruction's target; the AUROCs are on all 240 episodes without calibration. They show a signal exists, not a detector's operating point.
  • The Sentinel comparison rests on SENTINEL_METHODOLOGY.html (the paper as read there), not a fresh read.
