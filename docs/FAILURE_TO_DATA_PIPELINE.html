Method spec · GR00T N1.7 · LIBERO-Plus

From a failure to a data specification

How a perturbed-rollout failure becomes a targeted post-training set: locate the pathway by intervention, name the hole in the corpus, mine against that hole, and test the whole chain against the two cheaper alternatives.

2026-09-23 Pre-registrations R-039, R-040 in RESULTS.md Builds on R-036, R-037, R-038

Part 0 · The shape of the problem

A failure is not training data

The action head of a VLA is trained by behaviour cloning. It has seen a set of demonstrations and nothing else, so anything outside that set is out of distribution in the trivial sense. That makes "is this OOD?" the wrong first question. Every failure is. The useful question is what demonstrations would have prevented this one, because that is the only thing the retraining step can consume.

A failed rollout itself cannot go into the training set. It is a trajectory of wrong actions. What goes in is successful demonstrations of the same situation, and to find or make those you have to know which dimension of the situation to hold fixed and which to resample. That is the whole content of "localising to a mechanism": a mechanism is a data specification.

Part 1 · Why not "train on everything OOD"

Moved is not the same as repairs

The second approach on the table was to score every state with an OOD detector, collect whatever scores high, and fine-tune on all of it. It fails on three counts, and the first is structural rather than empirical.

  • It is not a data specification. "High OOD score" does not name an axis to re-render or a pose region to collect in. You cannot hand it to a data team.
  • It selects on symptom. The one detector we have that separates failures (proprio, 6.9×) sees only arm motion. R-036 showed it separates broadly across every perturbation category, which is what "the episode ended up somewhere unusual because it went wrong" looks like. Harvesting on it over-samples late-episode drift states.
  • The policy's own embeddings do not help. R-036: GR00T's VL embeddings do not separate its own failures. So an embedding-space OOD score is currently blind to exactly the vision failures we most want to mine.

Underneath all three is the point made formally in the trigger-mechanism calculus (Theorem 2). The set of activations that move under a perturbation and the set whose repair fixes the failure are different sets, and neither contains the other. A mechanism can track the trigger perfectly and be causally inert. A mechanism whose output is constant under the perturbation can still be the locus, because the failure lives in its interaction with a trigger-shifted sibling. OOD detectors, linear probes and SAE feature statistics all live on the left side of that picture. Only intervention gets to the right side.

Part 2 · What the policy receives, and where each input enters

N1.7 hands us an entry schedule

The policy sees two RGB images, eight numbers of proprioception and one instruction string. No object list, no goal spec, no task id. The vision-language backbone turns images and text into one token sequence vl_embeds; the state encoder turns the eight numbers into one 1536-wide token; the DiT action head consumes both and emits a 40-step action chunk, of which 7 of 132 dims are live.

The part that makes localisation tractable is how the DiT consumes vl_embeds. It does not merge them into the stream. It cross-attends, and it cross-attends to different token subsets at different block indices. Read off the forward pass, not the config name: text tokens enter at blocks 0, 4, 8, … 28; image tokens at 2, 6, 10, … 30; the sixteen odd blocks receive nothing. State enters once, by concatenation, before block 0.

Part 3 · R-039 · What we actually measure

Three splices, one source, one target, common random numbers

You asked whether the thing we look at is a difference in layer activations. No, or not primarily. Layer-activation differences are an observation, and Part 1 says observations cannot answer the question. What we look at is the action, after replacing one pathway's input with the value it would have had without the perturbation.

For each failure instance, two rollouts are run that differ only in the perturbation: a source under the nominal condition and a target under the perturbed one. Same task, layout, instruction, seed, and the same fixed denoising noise ε at every step. Then three patched target rollouts. In each, at every step, exactly one of the three splice points is overwritten with the source's tensor for that step, and everything downstream recomputes.

Which forward to splice at: none has to be chosen

The first version of this design recorded a nominal source run and spliced its step-k tensors into the target's step k, which only holds while the two runs have not diverged. That is unnecessary for camera, lighting and sensor-noise variants, because they are render-only: the world state is identical to the nominal task. So at every forward the simulator's current state is re-rendered under the nominal condition, without stepping physics, and the backbone is run on that render. The nominal features are then exact for the state the patched run is actually in.

  • Every arm at every forward. From one state the head runs five times under one fixed noise. One arm's chunk is executed; the other four are recorded. The transfer fraction is a curve over forwards for each pathway, at the price of four extra head passes and one extra backbone pass per forward.
  • Onset and anchor. The onset is the first forward at which an arm's transfer fraction crosses 0.5. The per-instance number is read at the forward containing the closest-approach step, which the labelling pipeline records as the failure anchor. Forwards happen every 16 env steps, so an episode has at most 18.
  • Robot initial state is the exception. There the trigger is the state itself, so no render can undo it. Forward 0 of a recorded nominal run is the one clean counterfactual, and that arm stays as the positive control.
Verified 2026-09-23 LiberoEnv.nominal_frames() swaps the camera or light fields in the MuJoCo model, recomputes forward kinematics, force-updates the observables and restores. On one variant each: view changes the frame by 19.5 mean-abs pixels, light by 44.3; restoring reproduces the perturbed frame to 0.001; joint positions, velocities and sim time are bitwise unchanged. For noise the inner env's frame is already the clean one, since the fork blurs only inside its own step. Script: experiments/paired_render_check.py.

Two preconditions, both currently unmet on N1.7

Gate 1 · determinism, at the model The denoise loop starts from an unseeded torch.randn. A patched forward has to be a function: same features in, same noise, same action out. The splice wrapper seeds the draw per forward and restores the generator afterwards, a separate path from the capture's save-and-rewind that R-036 relies on. The gate is at the model: identical input tensors and seed give a bitwise-identical chunk, and identical inputs give bitwise-identical source features. Bitwise equality of two whole rollouts is not required, because the renderer itself jitters by 0.001 mean-abs pixels between two renders of one state. The five arms at one forward share one render and one seed, so their comparison is exact by construction. Script: experiments/r039_determinism_gate.py.
Gate 2 · trigger-only difference Source and target must differ only in P. Holds by construction for camera, lighting and sensor noise: the world is identical, only the render differs. For robot initial state the perturbation is the state, so arm S restores the trigger rather than testing a pathway. It stays in the design as the positive control that proves the splice machinery works (expectation 1, load-bearing).

Why not a cumulative layer sweep

A sweep that patches blocks one at a time and accumulates sums exactly to the total effect, but how the total splits between blocks depends on the order you sweep in. On an AND-gate circuit, sweeping (m1, m2) gives all the credit to m2 and sweeping (m2, m1) gives it all to m1. So R-039 reports the three single splices, which have no ordering, and if any cumulative sweep is ever reported it uses the pre-registered order text → image → state and says so in the figure.

Part 5 · Where the SAE idea fits

Third tier, and only for the residual

A sparse autoencoder over the residual stream gives a dictionary of features and a way to say which ones fire differently on failures. Two things make it the wrong first tool here and the right third one.

  • Feature statistics are on the "moves" side of Figure 2. A feature that lights up on failures is a probe result. It becomes causal only when you ablate or steer it and watch the action, which is a splice by another name, with the extra premise that the steered state stays on-manifold.
  • The dictionary is per-checkpoint and expensive. arXiv:2603.19233's SAE is over N1.5's 16-block DiT and an Eagle backbone. Nothing transfers to N1.7 except the method. Training one is the single most expensive item on the board, and it has to be redone after every post-training round.

Where it earns its cost is the residual bucket: instances where no single splice carries the effect, so the failure lives in an interaction the three coarse splice points cannot separate. There an SAE does two things nothing else does. It gives a finer set of splice points than "text, image, state", and it gives a retrieval key: a feature that is active on the failure and absent across the training set is itself a data specification, and you can mine demonstrations by searching for it. That is the version of "mine similar data by general scenario" that survives contact with the objections in Part 1, and it is only worth building once R-039 has shown how big the residual bucket actually is.

Part 6 · R-040 · Testing the whole chain

Four ways to spend the same demonstrations

None of the above is worth anything unless mechanism-targeted mining beats the alternatives at the same budget. R-040 is that test, and it is the only place "which approach is best" gets a number instead of an argument.

The alternative that matters most is the obvious one: take the failed scenario, have a teleoperator or a passing policy do it successfully, and train on that. That is arm D. It is expected to win on the instances it was trained on. The test is on held-out instances of the same perturbation category that D never saw. A direct demo covers one point; mechanism-targeted mining covers the neighbourhood along the axis R-039 named. If D generalises as well as M, the neighbourhood was not needed and the localisation step should be dropped. If it does not, or if D regresses the nominal control where the failure was assigned to the text or image pathway, that is the R-038 shape: the head has been taught a mapping from an input that does not carry the distinguishing information.

Part 7 · What has to be built

Four pieces, in order

  1. Paired render. Done. LiberoEnv.nominal_frames() re-renders the current state under the trained camera and lights, and LeRobotPolicy.features_for() runs the backbone on it without acting, queueing or touching the capture.
  2. Splice with fixed noise. Done. vla_harness/capture/splice.py wraps the head's denoise entry point, runs all five arms under one seeded draw, records their chunks and returns the driven arm. Thirteen CPU gates in tests/test_splice.py. Model-level determinism gate: experiments/r039_determinism_gate.py.
  3. The R-039 runner. One rollout per instance with the splice attached, the per-forward transfer fractions on the 7 live dims, the recorded-source path for Robot Initial States, and the anchor forward from the labelling pipeline.
  4. R-040's miners. Re-rendering existing LIBERO demos under a LIBERO-Plus condition, pose-region resampling, and instruction augmentation. Each is a data-generation script, not a model change, and each is gated on R-039 saying it is needed.

Nothing in the list touches training until step 4, and step 4 is the only one that costs demonstrations. That is the point of doing the localisation first: the expensive step is spent on the answer, not on the guess.
