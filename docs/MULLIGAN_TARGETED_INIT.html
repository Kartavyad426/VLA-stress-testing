Verdict: the published, rigorous version of our "failure-targeted" arm
  • The method. Supervised batch-online learning. An operator places objects and intervenes. Mulligan decides where each round's episodes start:
    • retry the exact initial states where the last evaluation failed, earliest-breakdown first, up to a cap;
    • fill the rest of the budget with the initial states farthest from everything collected so far, optionally tilted toward a hardness estimate;
    • at a retried state, record a counterfactual full demonstration when a correction would only teach recovery.
    The learner is unchanged (HG-DAgger, or HiL-IDQL with critic best-of-N reranking).
  • The numbers.
    • Collection effect, real world, round-pooled, actor only: +11.7 / +10.9 / +6.0 pp on three tasks (exact McNemar p = 0.002 / 0.0007 / 0.036; 300–350 paired episodes each).
    • Headline "+10–34 pp" is the full recipe (collection + critic) at the final round; only one of three final-round actor-only contrasts is significant on its own.
    • Simulation: +2–12 pp per round, every round positive, 5 seeds verified.
  • About half the simulated gain is better coverage, not failure targeting. Square-Narrow round 1 (Table 5):
    • Sobol coverage alone: +3.9 pp over i.i.d. uniform;
    • performance-guided: +7.0;
    • plus counterfactual demos: +7.8.
    The budget is matched in episodes, not human frames. Mulligan's data held 8–28% more human frames, because counterfactuals are full demos verified.
  • Novelty consequence for us. "Failure-targeted beats uniform at a matched budget" is now published, on real robots, with blinded paired evaluation. Still ours:
    1. choosing which frames to label from an internal mechanism (forward 0, the wrist input);
    2. the mechanism-targeted vs failure-targeted contrast;
    3. a pre-registered mechanism readout.
    Mulligan's hardness signals are behavioural throughout: failure stage, plus geometric heuristics picked by inspecting rollouts.
  • For D23 (C17, C21).
    • Build the failure-targeted arm as Mulligan's Alg. 2, so it is not a strawman.
    • Build the broad arm as low-discrepancy coverage over (joint direction × achieved cm), not i.i.d.
    • Isolate the mechanism claim by holding start selection fixed and varying only what is labelled: full episode vs forward-0/1 bridge frames.
    • Match on labelled frames and sampler share.
    Today's fit test (training directions learned, new directions not) is exactly the gap Mulligan's coverage fill addresses: direction diversity, plus dose per region through the promotion cap.

1. The claim, and what it would take to prove it

The claim verified: in supervised deployment, "failures can concentrate in a small subset of initial states, so uniform collection spends much of the operator's time on states the policy already handles". Choosing each round's initial states from observed performance "outperforms uniform initial-state sampling at matched collection budgets".

The premise is shown directly (§F.1). A round-2 Square-Narrow policy at 86% overall, evaluated on an 80-state lattice × 50 rollouts, has most states at or near 100% and a small tail failing most of the time. That policy came from the coverage-only arm with even collection density, so concentration is not a collection artefact. In Fig. 1, 95% of failures fall in 25% of states.

Proving "targeting helps" needs three things:

  1. the same learner and budget, with only the start-state rule changed;
  2. held-out states disjoint from collection;
  3. paired, blinded evaluation.

The paper does all three for the actor-only contrast (HG-DAgger vs HG-DAgger+Mulligan). Proving "targeting the failures is what helps" needs a further ablation separating it from better coverage. Table 5 provides it in simulation only.

2. The method from first principles

2.1 Setting

An MDP (𝒮, 𝒜, P, r, γ, ρ₀) has sparse 0/1 reward, and ρ₀ is uniform over a bounded box 𝒮₀ of object poses. The goal is autonomous success on ρ₀. Each round r runs verified (Alg. 1):

  1. Train the actor πθ (Diffusion Policy U-Net) by BC on 𝒟h, the human frames of successful episodes, and the critic (Qψ, Vξ) on all data 𝒟all, failures included.
  2. Evaluate with K = 50 unassisted episodes on a fresh block of held-out states.
  3. Analyze: choose the next M initial states (Alg. 2).
  4. Collect M supervised episodes (50, or 100 on Route Cable and in sim), the operator intervening on failure.

Round 0 is 100 base demonstrations (200 on Square-Broad) at scrambled-Sobol initial states.

2.2 Choosing the initial states (Alg. 2)

Given budget M, previously collected initial states 𝒫, this round's evaluation failures ℱ, and a promotion cap mf:

Promotion:  𝒢 ← up to mf states of ℱ, earliest failure stage first(1)
Fill:  while |𝒢| < M:  𝒢 ← 𝒢 ∪ { argmaxc∈𝒮₀ d(c, 𝒫 ∪ 𝒢) · (1 + β h(c)) },   d(c, 𝒴) = miny∈𝒴 ‖c − y‖w(2)
  • Distance. ‖·‖w is a normalized, axis-weighted Euclidean distance: each pose coordinate is scaled to [0, 1], yaw is periodic, and axes with no hardness signal are down-weighted (§F.6).
  • β = 0 gives pure farthest-point coverage. h(c) ∈ [0, 1] is a task-specific hardness estimate.
  • Budgets. Real world: M = 50, mf = 25 (Route Cable 100 / 50). Simulation: M = 100 and every diagnostic failure is promoted.
  • Guardrails (§F.7):
    • the cumulative fill's p95 nearest-neighbour radius must not exceed that of a fresh pure-Sobol block of the same size;
    • no candidate reuses a collected or evaluation state.

The hardness signal is chosen by hand, per round verified (§F.5–F.6):

  • Simulation: after each round the authors inspect where failures and interventions concentrate and write a geometric heuristic h. On Square-Narrow it is a weighted nut-y + yaw term; on Square-Broad, a ridge regression of episode length on seven placement features. β is picked "during the same inspection, typically in [0.5, 1.0]".
  • Real world: β = 0 unless a geometric term predicts failure. On Route Cable, eight pre-registered hardness terms failed. A descriptive analysis then found the rope's bend at the first clip (success 17.6% → 2.5% from straightest to sharpest), used at β = 1.0 in round 5.

2.3 Counterfactual demonstrations

A correction starts from where the policy went wrong, so it teaches recovery. When the mistake is large (a dropped object), the operator also resets to the same initial state and records a full demonstration, which counts against M. This "places the missing behavior in the actor's support" verified.

2.4 The learner (unchanged by Mulligan)

HiL-IDQL trains the critic by IQL on 𝒟all, with an expectile value loss:

minξ 𝔼 |τ − 𝟙[u < 0]| u²,  u = Qψ(s, a) − Vξ(s);   chunked target y = Σi<h γirt+i + γh(1−done) Vξ(st+h)(3)

Real-world critics use a λ-return and a distributional (DIVL) head. At deployment the critic picks the best of N actor samples, ai* with i* = argmaxi Qψ(s, ai). No gradient reaches the actor verified.

5. Critique

  • "Failure-targeted" is a bundle of four things:
    • low-discrepancy coverage (Sobol R0, farthest-point fill);
    • failure promotion;
    • a hand-tuned hardness tilt;
    • full-length counterfactual demos.
    In simulation, coverage alone gives about half the round-1 gain, and failure guidance adds +3.1 pp beyond Sobol. The real world has no coverage-only arm.
  • The hardness signal is human analysis, not an algorithm. Per-round inspection picks h and β. On Route Cable, eight pre-registered terms failed before a post-hoc axis was found. The paper is open about this, but "performance-guided" partly means "an expert looked at the failures". Ironically, that is closer to our mechanism-driven route than the paper's framing admits.
  • An evaluation-grid leak in 3 sim rounds (§F.5): the fill used the previous round's success in the evaluation-set cell. It is disclosed, and the gaps in those rounds are not systematically larger. Real-world results have no such term.
  • Budget unit. Episodes are matched; human frames are not (+8–28%). A frame-matched comparison would be the stricter test.
  • Final-round evidence is thin. Only Insert Marker's actor-only contrast is significant on its own; the pooled-over-rounds effect carries the claim.
  • Not a VLA. A Diffusion Policy U-Net is trained from 100–200 task demos. The authors list generalist policies as open. Whether failure concentration survives a pretrained policy's smoother failure landscape is untested.
  • Well done:
    • blinded, interleaved, paired evaluation on disjoint states;
    • 2,550 real held-out episodes;
    • honest negative findings (FQE and offline critic selection do not work; reranking helps only later);
    • autonomous successes in the actor data hurt.

7. What it implies for D23 (C17, C21)

Context, today's fit test ours (runs/r056_diag/fit_firstchunk.json):

  • The r 16 adapter closed 30% of the first-chunk gap on its own training starts, but 12% on held-out starts at matched cm.
  • Training used 100 one-off random joint directions; the matched held-out subset uses directions 100 and 101.
  • Ranks 4 / 16 / 64 give 65 / 59 / 64 of 108 (r 64 as relayed by prim).

So direction coverage and dose per region are the suspects, and Mulligan's two mechanisms address exactly those inferred.

Design rules taken from Mulligan inferred:

  1. Match on labelled frames and on sampler share (the dose per region), not episodes. Report views per frame per region (post-mortem §3.1).
  2. Evaluate on fresh, disjoint directions drawn low-discrepancy, not two or three fixed ones. Report per direction × cm, paired and blinded where possible.
  3. Keep the base policy's own frames out of the actor's data. Mulligan's actor degrades when its autonomous successes are added (−3.4 pp). R-054's minted frames 16–47 are the base's own actions and were 33% of draws (post-mortem §3.1). Drop them or down-weight them.
  4. Plan for ≥ 2 rounds if the budget allows. Mulligan's gains are largest at R1–R2 and converge later. One round is where targeting should show most.
  5. Promotion cap = dose control. mf ≤ M/2 plus a coverage floor stops targeting from collapsing coverage. That is the trade-off the fit test exposed.

8. What it means for our stack

8.1 Verdict

Same path on "where to collect", different on "what to label". Mulligan is the best available template for the failure-targeted and broad arms, and its evaluation protocol is the bar. It does not touch input- or time-level attribution, so the mechanism contrast remains ours to test.

8.2 What to borrow

B1 · Alg. 2 as our start sampler

Farthest-point fill with a periodic-axis distance over (direction, cm band), Sobol for round 0, a promotion cap, and the p95-radius coverage guardrail. It is cheap: CPU-side code in the start-state generator inferred.

B2 · Their evaluation protocol

A fresh disjoint block per round, arms interleaved, paired McNemar, and rescues vs regressions reported. Ours already pairs seeds; add fresh directions per evaluation.

B3 · Actor-data hygiene

Train the actor on corrected or demonstrated frames only. Any critic or analysis gets everything.

B4 · Stage-wise reporting

Their grasp-vs-insert split shows where a collection strategy helps. Our phase segmentation gives the same split for free (approach / grasp / transport), which matters given scene 1327's grasp-stage regression.
