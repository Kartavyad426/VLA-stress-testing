Root Signal Experiments

Scoping draft · 2026-10-09 · prim with the user · not a pre-registration. Records the experiments planned around policy-side signals for finding where an episode goes wrong (the "root" in MINING_SPEC_DESIGN D13). Once the open decisions in §6 are settled, SFT turns the chosen experiments into a pre-registration in RESULTS.md. Tags: agreed with the user; proposed by prim; open needs a decision. [V] = checked against files today.

Bottom line

  • Today the root comes from rules on simulator state (gripper and object height). They're LIBERO-shaped and don't work outside sim. The idea, agreed in principle by the user on 10-09, is to take the time of the root from signals the policy itself produces, and keep cheap gripper rules only for the phase label.
  • Four signals: (a) consecutive chunks disagreeing with each other, (b) the perturbed chunk drifting from the nominal one, (c) commanded vs achieved motion, (d) the policy disagreeing with a successful demonstrator.
  • Most of this can be tested on the CPU with data we already have (R-047). Two steps need the GPU, and only if the CPU results are promising.

1 · Why

  • A mining-spec instance hangs off its root: the first moment the episode leaves the successful path (D13). The success search, the teleop brief and sim generation all start from there.
  • The current root detectors are ours, built in September (vla_harness/mining/phases.py, phases_libero.py) [V]. Their weaknesses:
    • LiberoPhaseSegmenter labels approach / pre-grasp / grasp / transport / retry from gripper width, the close command and simulator object positions. Its thresholds are placeholders that were never fitted. It has no "place" phase, and the target is "first object in the BDDL file".
    • divergence_point needs a paired nominal run and is broken on LIBERO (it reads ee_xy, which LIBERO rollouts don't log) [V, PoC].
    • The PoC toy root rule (first grasp onset that fails the success criterion) needs simulator object height.
  • Signals that come from the policy and the robot's own motion are robot- and task-agnostic. They also work on external data, by running our policy open-loop on its frames.

2 · The signals

#SignalWhat it comparesAnalogue
aSelf-consistencyGR00T predicts C = 40 steps per forward but executes H = 16. The last 24 predicted steps of forward f cover the same timesteps as the first 24 of forward f+1. A jump between them means the policy changed its plan.STAC (Sentinel), R-059
bGap to nominalThe perturbed chunk against the nominal chunk at the same forward (d_pn). Needs a paired nominal run, so sim only: a privileged reference for (a).Our perturbation map
cCommanded vs achievedThe end-effector motion each executed action commanded, against the motion that actually happened (from proprioception, or an IDM on video). A gap means a slip, a block or a misjudged contact.VLA-Corrector's predicted-vs-actual idea, at action level
dImitation disagreementThe policy run open-loop on frames from a successful demonstration, against what the demonstrator did. It marks where the policy would deviate before any failure happens, so mining targets can be found proactively.D20 first-chunk fit test (an7)

What these signals can't do alone proposed

  • They give when, not what. The instance identity (phase × failure mode, D12) still needs a phase label, so the proposal is: time from the signal, phase from gripper rules at that time.
  • Changing plan is normal at phase transitions (e.g. grasp onset). Thresholds need calibrating on successful episodes (conformal, as in Sentinel).
  • With one sample per forward, flow-matching noise and real plan changes mix. STAC compares distributions over several draws (R-059's multi-draw runner, not built).
  • Failures where the policy is confidently wrong (it steadily heads to the wrong spot, likely our start-pose case [I]) may produce no (a) spike. (c) may still catch the resulting slip.

5 · What would close or open the direction proposed

  • Closed: none of (a)–(c) separates failures from successes better than chance, or the spikes don't line up with roots any better than the gripper rules alone.
  • Open: (c) or (a) reaches an AUROC above about 0.75 for failure vs success, and the spike lands within one forward of the hand-labelled root.
  • Thresholds are placeholders until pre-registration; SFT sets them there.
