A. How a scene goes through the gates

  1. Gate 0 once per robot + camera rig (same robot, gripper, cameras, policy client): reused by every scene from that rig.
  2. Then per scene, in order: Gate 1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → 5. A failed check is fixed (or the scene is dropped) before the next gate. The order matters because one error hides another: the 6 cm table error hid a 6–7 cm camera-pose error (SIM_FIXES faults 1–2), and both made every visual check look fine.
  3. Gate 6 per policy, on a scene that passed 1–5.
  4. Count rollout failures only under the counting rule (G). Everything else is "unattributed".
  5. Any later edit to a scene (size, colour, physics) re-runs the gate it touches and everything after it (2.3).

Automated today: scene_checks.py (1.1, 1.3, 1.5–1.7, 2.1), block_size_check.py (1.4, 4.6 colour part), stack_drop_test.py (2.4), replay_probe.py (3.3, 2.5, 5.2 physics breakdown), policy_batch.py --policy hold (3.2, 5.1), image_swap.py / g6_wrist.py (4.x).

Counting rule. A rollout failure counts as the policy's only if: the rig passes Gate 0; the scene passes Gates 1–5 (including 1.4 object size, 3.3 the played layout is the expert-solved one, 4.2 the wrist swap and 4.4 rendered objects); the expert solves that played layout (3.1) and a do-nothing policy doesn't (3.2); there is no sustained blocked command (5.2) under the recorded physics settings (0.9); and 6.1/6.2 show the policy behaves on real frames and our images don't move its plan. Everything else is "unattributed". Per-episode differences between two batches are read only against the noise of 6.6. Today: nothing on orange is fully attributable (4.2, 4.4, 5.3 open); the early release is attributed to the policy through 6.1.
