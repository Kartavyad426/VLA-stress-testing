The ten changes that matter most
  1. C4. Direction #2's "rescues 24/30" conflates two numbers. R-053: N succeeds 24/30 vs P 6/30; it flips 20/24 P-failures; a plain re-run flips 0/22.
  2. C5. The run-time arm uses the nominal chunk, which is an oracle: it needs the unperturbed episode. The deployable cost-zero baseline is re-homing the arm before running. It must be in the comparison or the business pitch is exposed.
  3. C16. Direction and offset are confounded. Direction 0 never gets past 12 cm (it reaches 0.72 of the requested radius), so every >12 cm failure comes from directions 1 and 2. Stratify, and say so.
  4. C17. For start pose, "mechanism-targeted" and "failure-targeted" now point at the same starts: far offsets, directions 1–2, decided at forward 0. Redefine the arms by which frames are labelled (first forward vs whole episode), or they can't be told apart.
  5. C18. A global visual-token affine (2512.02902) is a camera-row fix, not a start-pose fix. For start pose, the parameter-only arm should be wrist-token steering at forward 0, i.e. #3.
  6. C9. Add Mulligan (arXiv:2610.05882, 5 Oct 2026). Failure-targeted initial-state selection beats uniform at a matched budget, with +10–34 pt. "Failure-targeted vs uniform at matched budget" is now published, so our distinct claim is mechanism-targeted vs failure-targeted.
  7. C10. Update the Jung & Park and FLARE rows from the full reads (JUNG_PARK_INITPOSE.html, FLARE_RECOVERY.html).
  8. C8. Update the 2512.02902 row.
    • Its "LoRA" baseline full-fine-tunes SigLIP (414.8M of 467M parameters), verified against openpi.
    • The FTM average is misprinted.
    • The Spatial-vs-Physical control is missing (SPATIAL_ADAPT_MATHS.html).
  9. C20. Citation fixes:
    • 2507.09061 is Zhang, Pfrommer, Pan, Matni, Simchowitz, not "Simchowitz et al.";
    • three numbers are not in their abstracts (IntervenGen 34–62%, DemoGen ~20×, REALM ~800 pairs);
    • all 41 IDs resolve.
  10. C21. The recommendation's arm list needs rewriting. Minting is dropped, so "the R-054 rescues" arm no longer exists. A proposed six-arm version is in §4.

4.2 C17: mechanism- vs failure-targeted have converged for start pose

Both now point at far offsets in directions 1–2. If both arms draw the same starts, they differ only by accident. Define them by what is labelled, at the same starts and the same frame budget inferred:

  • Mechanism-targeted: first-forward-only labels (k* = 1), wrist-bearing frames, from Option A bridges. Most of the budget goes to bridge frames at forward 0–1.
  • Failure-targeted: whole-episode demos from the same failing starts (Option B scripted expert, or Mulligan-style), with labels spread over the episode.

This tests the one thing our diagnosis adds: that the frames to label are known, not just the starts.

4.3 C18: the parameter-only arm needs the right parameterisation for start pose

FTM is one per-channel affine applied identically to every token. It fixes a global statistics shift: plausible for camera, lighting and texture. A start-pose perturbation is a state-specific change in what the wrist sees, so a global affine is unlikely to help inferred. Two better-posed parameter-only arms:

  1. Camera row: FTM / moment matching (SPATIAL_ADAPT_MATHS E1–E2).
  2. Start pose: steer the wrist-derived tokens toward the nominal ones at forward 0 only. That is #3, and R-053 says reproducing the nominal first chunk is sufficient.

Keep "parameter-only" in #1, but name which.

4.4 C5 expanded: re-homing is the baseline to beat

If start-pose failures are decided at forward 0 and nominal starts succeed at 0.93–0.95, the simplest deployable fix is to drive the arm to the canonical start pose and then run the policy. In simulation that is close to free. Any data or steering repair must beat it, or be justified for settings where re-homing is impossible: mid-task disturbances, cluttered workspaces, or tasks where the start pose is part of the task. Without that arm, a reviewer or customer will ask the question first inferred.

4.5 Does "#1 as the spine, five arms" still hold?

Yes, with a rewritten arm list (C21) inferred. Proposed, all at a matched frame budget, the same recipe and dose reporting, and evaluated per direction × cm band plus nominal:

  1. Replay only: the no-new-data control. This is D23.
  2. Broad coverage: uniform start offsets, all directions.
  3. Failure-targeted: whole-episode demos at far offsets, directions 1–2 (Mulligan-like).
  4. Mechanism-targeted: forward-0/1 bridge labels at the same starts (Option A).
  5. Parameter-only: forward-0 wrist-token steering (#3) for start pose; FTM / moment matching for the camera row.
  6. Run-time, no training: re-home; bridge-then-handback; nominal-chunk oracle as the upper bound.

The headline figure stays "gap closed vs cost". The new contrast that carries the novelty is 4 vs 3.

4.6 Smaller points

  • #2 risk line. "R-037 found that nothing predicts failure well yet": R-037 is about the backbone vs adapter signal, and R-036 is the underpowered negative. Cite R-036/R-047 for "nothing predicts well", and R-059 (STAC, pre-registered) as the pending test verified (RESULTS.md table rows).
  • #3. Add "Observing and Controlling Features" (2603.05487) as the baseline to beat. It is linear-observer steering on π0.5 with flow matching, closer than COAST to what #3 proposes.
  • #5. Jung & Park's +2 teleop poses (30 → 75% at one pose) is a priced human-data result: one targeted human collection, about 76 episodes inferred. It is a natural data point for the "human demo value" comparison.
  • At a glance. #1's novelty is "High". Given Mulligan, "High only for the mechanism-vs-failure contrast" is more accurate.
